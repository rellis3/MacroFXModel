How to read this

The error bar is the point. σ converges as you sample more finely; μ does not — its standard error is σ/√T in calendar span and is unmoved by sampling frequency (Merton 1980). At a 14-day window SE(d) = 0.267, so a reading has to be large before “strong” means anything. A leg whose bar crosses zero is drawn grey.

Carry is observed, not estimated. Covered interest parity pins the forward against spot, so μRN ≈ rquote − rbase is a price rather than a forecast. Broker financing is preferred over the interbank differential because it is what you are actually charged; the spread is stripped from the long/short quote rather than taken from one side.

The standard errors are an upper bound. They assume pair residuals are independent, and FX pairs share risk-on, carry and liquidity factors that survive the decomposition. Read t = 6 as well resolved; do not read t = 2.1 as 96% confident.
