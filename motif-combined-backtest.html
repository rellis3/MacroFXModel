Portfolio — the 2×2 (same motifs on every arm)
Standard quant results-card set at matched risk sizing (1%/trade, 5% concurrent cap). CAGR and Calmar are inflated by the same effect as total return, not a separate trustworthy number: fixed-%-of-equity compounding over ~20-28k trades produces triple/quadruple-digit CAGR and Calmar in the double digits — mathematically correct given the inputs, not a realistic forward expectation (uncapped position count/correlated concurrent risk beyond the 5% cap, no capacity limit modeled). Read CAGR/Calmar as a RELATIVE ranking across the 4 arms, same as Sharpe/Sortino — never as an absolute forecast.

Equity curves — all 4 arms, log scale
Log scale on purpose: fixed-fractional compounding makes the 4 arms differ by orders of magnitude in absolute terms even though the Sharpe/DD gap between them is modest — a linear scale would show 3 flat lines and 1 line going to the moon. Read the SHAPE (drawdown depth/timing, smoothness), not the vertical distance between curves.

Frozen grid baseline

Resamples the CLOSED-TRADE r×size_mult sequence WITH REPLACEMENT and replays each draw as a sequential fixed-fractional equity curve — the classic trade-based Monte Carlo (MultiCharts/TradeStation-style), not a price-path simulation. Answers "how much does this result depend on the luck of this exact trade order/sample" — NOT a recheck of whether the portfolio mechanics (calendar concurrency, risk caps) were realistic; that's what the table above already tested. The final-return percentiles inherit the same runaway fixed-% compounding as CAGR above (read as relative bands, e.g. "how much wider is p95 vs p5", never as an absolute dollar forecast) — the max-drawdown percentiles are the trustworthy, bounded half of this table and the actual point of running it. Same seed every run — the bands don't change unless the underlying trades do.
