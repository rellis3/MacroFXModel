Hedge signals (v2) — retired 2026-11-06

The "hedge signals" are retired. They were never hedges, and they were never tested.

What it was

A pairs-trading (spread mean-reversion) system. It took two correlated pairs, traded a 1:1 log-price spread between them, entered when the spread's z-score passed 2, exited when it came back inside 0.5 and stopped out beyond 3.5. Version 1 ran every 15 minutes on the server and sent entry, exit and stop messages to Telegram; version 2 added a cointegration gate and ran hourly. Its page also ran an in-sample / out-of-sample comparison that was never a pre-registered test.

Why it was retired

  • It is not a hedge: it opens a new two-legged bet rather than reducing the risk of a trade already held.
  • The spread's mean and standard deviation were kept in memory and reset on every server restart.
  • The correlation z-score was measured against a full-history mean.
  • Only 2 signals ever closed with a P&L, +0.035% in total.
  • Nothing in the Evidence Book tests it. It is recorded as retired, untested: not a null, not a pass.

Where hedging lives now

Desk Terminal → Correlations & hedges → Hedge finder: for a market or a book card, the instruments that have moved against it, how often that held and the min-variance size. It is descriptive and untested. A study of hedging the book is under way.

The server no longer computes these signals or sends their Telegram messages. Its old address (/api/hedge-signals-v2) still answers, with retired: true and an empty signal list.
