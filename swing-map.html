How to read this chart, and how to use it
  • Each vertical dotted line is one CME option expiry, at its date. The date sits under it. Faint lines are thin books, too small to read (under 10% of all live open interest).
  • The arrow on top of each line says which side of today's price most of that expiry's open interest sits. ▲ = more above, ▼ = more below, ◆ = balanced.
  • The purple bars on a line are that expiry's three biggest strikes inside the monthly range. Width = their share of that expiry. Bright violet = OI there has been building; grey = flat or fading.
  • Most-touched path (violet line): where most of 2,000 simulated paths sit each day if each expiry pulls price toward its open-interest centre. Grey dashed = the same simulation with no pull.
  • Orange segments marked 1d / 5d / 20d are the expected move: how far the high (above) and the low (below) typically reach within 1, 5 and 20 sessions from the last close (the median, validated range lines). Each segment ends at its date.
  • Level lines (thin dotted, labels on the right): the level map — 1-year high, the 5- and 20-day p75 reach, and the bounce / flush barriers.
  • Option lines (labels on the right): from the latest CME capture, the three biggest call walls (green) and put walls (red), the gamma flip (amber) and max pain (blue). The same lines the OI dashboard draws. Tested here: walls behave like ordinary levels, and max pain does not pin.
  • How to use it: it is a map of where the options book sits, not a forecast. Read three things together: (1) do the heavy expiries lean the same way; (2) is OI building on that side, away from price, before price gets there; (3) does the dealer flow (charm, in the table below) point the same way? When they agree, the picture is coherent. When they disagree, as the line under "Option pull" says, there is no clean read. None of this is tested yet: the expiry-pull score decides whether the lean means anything.
