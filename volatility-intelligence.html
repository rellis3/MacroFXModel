Cost & Normalisation §1

Spread as a fraction of today's expected daily range — a pair whose spread eats 8% of its typical day is a different instrument to trade than one where it's 0.5%, regardless of the setup. Sorted cheapest first.

Carry-to-Vol §6

Annualised short-rate differential (base currency's policy rate minus quote's — js/rateDiffEngine.js, already live for the macro scorecard) divided by annualised vol. Raw carry alone can't be compared across pairs; a fat differential on a wild pair is a worse risk-adjusted trade than a smaller one on a calm pair. FX pairs only — Gold and the indices have no currency-pair carry leg. Sorted highest positive carry-to-vol first.

Vol as Clock — time budgets §3

Median/P75 time (as a fraction of the trading day) a driftless model expects price to take reaching the O-C median target, given today's σ and however much distance remains right now — closed-form Brownian math, not backtested. "Stale" means more time has elapsed than the median first-passage time predicts and the level still hasn't been reached — the thesis may be running behind schedule on timing grounds, independent of P&L.

Tradable Asset — Implied vs Realised §5

The variance risk premium: implied minus realised volatility. Positive means the options market is pricing in more movement than has actually been happening — selling that gap is the classic vol-carry trade. Single-index CBOE coverage (EVZ/GVZ) is EUR/USD and Gold only.

Liquidity & Participation §6

Range consumed per unit of OANDA tick volume so far today — an Amihud-style illiquidity read. Higher means today's range is being produced by relatively thin participation; lower means a heavier tape is behind the same range. Tick count, not real traded volume — magnitude only, and only meaningful relative to other pairs right now or this pair's own recent history, never as an absolute number.
