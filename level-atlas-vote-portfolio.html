Combines each selected pair's own vote-margin trades (barrier-priced, concurrency-capped per pair) into ONE portfolio — reusing js/backtestStats.js's portfolioStats on the combined daily return series, no new metrics. Fixed % risk / trade (default) risks the SAME % of account on every trade off its own real stop distance — no capital-split guess, no need to know trade count/frequency in advance, forward-implementable as-is. NAV split (legacy) instead pre-allocates a fixed capital fraction per pair (equal or inverse-vol) and lets each pair's own raw price-return scale within that slice — kept for comparison, but requires deciding weights up front. See MD files/LEGO_MODULES.md for the full methodology and per-pair validation. Portfolio heat cap now defaults ON at 1% (2026-08-29) — without it, every pair independently risks its target % SIMULTANEOUSLY with no shared account-wide ceiling, which manufactures diversification benefit no real account actually has (uncapped Sharpe on this book ran 3.6-4.0; a realistic 1% simultaneous-exposure cap brings it to ~2.0-2.7, the range worth trusting). Uncheck it to see the old, inflated uncapped number for comparison — the "Without cap" column below always shows both side by side.

⚠ Correlated risk — read before selecting many pairs at once

Every pair here individually shows real edge, but neither FX crosses NOR equity indices are independent bets — many FX pairs share a currency leg (EURUSD/EURJPY/EURGBP all carry EUR exposure, GBPUSD/GBPJPY/GBPAUD all carry GBP, etc.), and NQ/SPX/DOW/US2000/DE30/UK100 are all "risk assets" that tend to move together (often WORSE than FX crosses) on a global risk-off day — so a single macro-shock day can move MANY selected pairs the same direction at once, across BOTH groups. The 5 weakest, highest-cost FX pairs (EURNZD/GBPNZD/AUDNZD/AUDCHF/GBPCAD) have been REMOVED from this page entirely — real edge, but not worth their own spread (see the ⚠ warning on their own page, the single-pair tearsheet). That curation alone is NOT the fix for the risk below — tested with all 26 FX+gold pairs selected (before removal) at 1% fixed risk/trade: one real day (2024-09-06) saw 72 trades resolve simultaneously, realizing a single-day -27% loss, and the full combination's max drawdown over the period was -97.8% — an effective wipeout. Excluding the 5 weak pairs improved Sharpe substantially but STILL left a -70.65% max drawdown from the SAME correlated-stacking effect among the remaining 21 — adding 6 correlated indices on top does not remove that risk, it very plausibly adds to it. The portfolio heat cap above does NOT fix this at this scale (tested 2-10% thresholds — stayed catastrophic) because its "keep whichever trade arrived first" rule becomes close to arbitrary once many pairs compete for a small shared budget, rather than protecting the best trades. A genuinely EXPOSURE-aware risk model (capping net risk per underlying currency AND per correlated asset cluster, not just per trade count) is the real fix and isn't built yet. Until then: keep total selected pairs modest, size risk down substantially before selecting many pairs together, and don't trust the raw numbers from a large multi-pair selection at 1% risk/trade. Separately, SPX/DOW's own headline Sharpe (5.6-5.7) is real but rests on notably LESS validated history than everything else on this page (OOS only from mid-2024, roughly half the track record of the other 25 pairs) — treat those two as promising, not proven.

"Select recommended" above unchecks the 10 pairs a leave-one-out analysis found to be the biggest correlated-drawdown contributors (GBPAUD, GBPCHF, USDCAD, AUDCAD, NZDJPY, EURGBP, GBPJPY, NZDUSD, EURJPY, EURCAD — mostly non-USD crosses whose currency exposure overlaps heavily with other selected pairs). This is NOT a claim that those 10 pairs are individually bad — GBPAUD, for instance, has decent standalone Sharpe — only that combining them with the others compounds correlated risk more than it diversifies. Removing a SINGLE one barely helps (the single best candidate only moved max DD from -64.7% to -61.0%) — the exclusion set only works as a GROUP, found by greedily removing the worst contributor and re-checking 10 times. Because that selection process itself risks overfitting to this exact sample (the same trap that broke the drawdown throttle earlier), it was OOS-tested the same way: chosen on the first 70% of history only, then checked against the untouched last 30% — max DD there improved from -42.7% to -25.1% and Sharpe from 2.36 to 3.65, so the pattern holds on data it wasn't picked from. Still an improvement, not a fix — -25% max DD is far better than -65-98%, but nowhere near what the individual 5-pair or 21-pair portfolios showed earlier; the real fix is still the not-yet-built exposure-aware risk model above.

Portfolio heat cap impact — same trades, with vs without the cross-pair budget

Each pair already caps ITS OWN concurrent positions; this catches what that leaves open — several different pairs firing at once, each independently risking the same %, silently stacking simultaneous exposure. Real before/after, not an assumption.

Drawdown throttle impact — same trades, with vs without de-risking in a drawdown

Cuts risk-per-trade to a fraction once the portfolio's OWN equity curve breaches a drawdown threshold, restores once it recovers — targets a sustained losing STRETCH over time, which the heat cap above was shown NOT to fix.

Fade-stop tightening impact — same trades, with vs without a tighter fade stop

Re-prices FADE decisions only (follow is left untouched — the same analysis found its current stop is already close to right) using each pair's OWN fade-winners' real MAE, never widening. OOS-validated first (93% of pairs improved OOS Sharpe using a stop chosen only from earlier data) before being wired in here — see scripts/oos_validate_fade_stop.mjs.

Fixed SL fraction impact — same trades, with vs without a uniform tighter stop

Re-prices FADE decisions only, tightening each trade's OWN existing bracket stop to a fixed fraction of its current distance (composes with fade-stop tightening above if both are on — applied second, on top). OOS-validated first (a real adverse-excursion-timing signal — a fade trade already 75% of the way to its stop goes on to lose ~2x more often than one that isn't, at every time horizon checked) before being wired in here — see analysis/mae_timing_study.mjs and analysis/sl_tightening_backtest.mjs.

p90 rung impact — same portfolio, with vs without the outermost ladder rung

p90 is excluded everywhere else (excludeRungs: ['p90']) because a fade there has no real stop in the 3-rung ladder — and its sample is too thin for the normal vote-margin mechanic to ever fire (checked directly: 0 votes across hundreds of OOS touches). This trades it UNCONDITIONALLY instead, priced against each pair's own empirical 90th-percentile continuation distance (the real distance price travels once it clears p90, fit on in-sample data only). Real per-pair spread cost killed the tighter percentiles tested (p75's OOS Sharpe went negative) — only the 90th held up on portfolio Sharpe, per-trade Sharpe, and a cross-pair heat-cap sensitivity check, so that's the only percentile offered here, not a tunable grid. See analysis/p90_empirical_outer_backtest.mjs and scripts/build_p90_votetrades.mjs.

Early exit (no re-leverage) — same fade trades, with vs without cutting likely losers short

Built after finding the "Fixed SL fraction" lever above was mostly an implicit-leverage artifact — tightening the DECLARED stop let fixed-fractional sizing size UP every trade (winners included), so avg win more than doubled while avg loss barely moved. This is the corrected version: position sizing stays anchored to each trade's ORIGINAL, unchanged stop (a winner's risk-adjusted result is mathematically identical on or off), and only the exit trigger changes — if the real M1 path shows a fade trade crossing 40% of its original stop distance before it would normally resolve, it exits there instead, a genuinely smaller realized loss. Unlike the p90/SL-fraction grids, this one has a real peak (same threshold, both in-sample and out-of-sample) instead of running to the edge of the tested range. That said: even correctly computed, this lever's own Sharpe 95% CI sits entirely above any believable range for a real strategy (~[5.8, 9.5] at a realistic 1% heat cap). Trust the shape of the result below — avg win unchanged, avg loss and drawdown genuinely reduced — not the Sharpe number itself.

Intraday MAE — worst point reached WITHIN a day, not just the net result

Every other number on this page is an end-of-day net result. That hides a real risk for a prop account with an intraday drawdown rule: 6 losses followed by 2 big wins can net a fine-looking day while still breaching a hard intraday limit before those wins arrive. This reconstructs each day's running equity path (trades ordered by entry time) and reports the worst point reached mid-day, across the whole backtest.

Currency daily loss gate impact — same trades, with vs without the circuit breaker

Blocks new trades in a currency (e.g. JPY, USD) for the rest of the day once that currency's REALIZED loss that day already breaches the threshold — built after inspecting the worst portfolio days showed no single pair drives more than ~30% of a bad day's loss, but a single currency often drives 40-80% of it. OOS-validated first (IS-chosen 1% cap improved EVERY OOS metric — Sharpe, vol, max DD, and CVaR — not just Sharpe) before being wired in here — see scripts/oos_validate_currency_loss_gate.mjs. Strictly causal: only counts a trade's loss once it has actually CLOSED, never one still open.

Cumulative portfolio return %

Compounded reinvests each day's % return into the next day's base (what a real account does). Non-compounded just sums the daily % returns (what a fixed-size bet run every day would show) — the gap between the two lines IS the compounding effect. A flat-then-explosive compounded curve on a LINEAR axis is the expected shape of a STABLE growth rate, not evidence it's accelerating — toggle log scale to see the real underlying rate (a constant rate draws as a straight line; only a genuine change in slope means the edge itself changed).

In Fixed % risk / trade mode, Return % is already the real risk-scaled outcome (pair weight = 1, a no-op) — Currency P&L's Risk($) = Account × Risk% (the SAME risk% the chart was built with, auto-synced above). R (both modes) = the trade's own outcome ÷ its own real stop distance — a per-trade ratio, always left unscaled by weight since scaling both sides of a ratio cancels out.
