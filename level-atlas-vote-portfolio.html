Combines each selected pair's own vote-margin trades (barrier-priced, concurrency-capped per pair) into ONE portfolio — reusing js/backtestStats.js's portfolioStats on the combined daily return series, no new metrics. Fixed % risk / trade (default) risks the SAME % of account on every trade off its own real stop distance — no capital-split guess, no need to know trade count/frequency in advance, forward-implementable as-is. NAV split (legacy) instead pre-allocates a fixed capital fraction per pair (equal or inverse-vol) and lets each pair's own raw price-return scale within that slice — kept for comparison, but requires deciding weights up front. See MD files/LEGO_MODULES.md for the full methodology and per-pair validation.

⚠ Correlated risk — read before selecting many pairs at once

Every pair here individually shows real edge, but neither FX crosses NOR equity indices are independent bets — many FX pairs share a currency leg (EURUSD/EURJPY/EURGBP all carry EUR exposure, GBPUSD/GBPJPY/GBPAUD all carry GBP, etc.), and NQ/SPX/DOW/US2000/DE30/UK100 are all "risk assets" that tend to move together (often WORSE than FX crosses) on a global risk-off day — so a single macro-shock day can move MANY selected pairs the same direction at once, across BOTH groups. The 5 weakest, highest-cost FX pairs (EURNZD/GBPNZD/AUDNZD/AUDCHF/GBPCAD) have been REMOVED from this page entirely — real edge, but not worth their own spread (see the ⚠ warning on their own page, the single-pair tearsheet). That curation alone is NOT the fix for the risk below — tested with all 26 FX+gold pairs selected (before removal) at 1% fixed risk/trade: one real day (2024-09-06) saw 72 trades resolve simultaneously, realizing a single-day -27% loss, and the full combination's max drawdown over the period was -97.8% — an effective wipeout. Excluding the 5 weak pairs improved Sharpe substantially but STILL left a -70.65% max drawdown from the SAME correlated-stacking effect among the remaining 21 — adding 6 correlated indices on top does not remove that risk, it very plausibly adds to it. The portfolio heat cap above does NOT fix this at this scale (tested 2-10% thresholds — stayed catastrophic) because its "keep whichever trade arrived first" rule becomes close to arbitrary once many pairs compete for a small shared budget, rather than protecting the best trades. A genuinely EXPOSURE-aware risk model (capping net risk per underlying currency AND per correlated asset cluster, not just per trade count) is the real fix and isn't built yet. Until then: keep total selected pairs modest, size risk down substantially before selecting many pairs together, and don't trust the raw numbers from a large multi-pair selection at 1% risk/trade. Separately, SPX/DOW's own headline Sharpe (5.6-5.7) is real but rests on notably LESS validated history than everything else on this page (OOS only from mid-2024, roughly half the track record of the other 25 pairs) — treat those two as promising, not proven.

Cumulative portfolio return %

Compounded reinvests each day's % return into the next day's base (what a real account does). Non-compounded just sums the daily % returns (what a fixed-size bet run every day would show) — the gap between the two lines IS the compounding effect. A flat-then-explosive compounded curve on a LINEAR axis is the expected shape of a STABLE growth rate, not evidence it's accelerating — toggle log scale to see the real underlying rate (a constant rate draws as a straight line; only a genuine change in slope means the edge itself changed).

Every kept (post concurrency-cap) trade across the selected pairs, tagged with which pair it belongs to. In Fixed % risk / trade mode, Return % is already the real risk-scaled outcome (pair weight = 1, a no-op) — Currency P&L's Risk($) = Account × Risk% (the SAME risk% the chart was built with, auto-synced above). In NAV split mode, Return %/Currency P&L are scaled by each trade's pair weight so summing either column reproduces the portfolio's combined result. R (both modes) = the trade's own outcome ÷ its own real stop distance — a per-trade ratio, always left unscaled by weight since scaling both sides of a ratio cancels out.
