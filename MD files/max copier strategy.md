# Max Copier Strategy

## 1. Strategy Overview

I identify important higher-timeframe price levels on the 1 hour and enter when the market produces a strong impulse move beyond them.

These levels are formed around significant previous highs and lows, areas where price has historically reacted and where fresh momentum may enter the market.

Rather than trading constantly, I wait for price to push through one of these predefined areas with conviction, then settle into a period of consolidation before looking for an opportunity.

## 2. What Is an Impulse Move?

An impulse move is a strong, decisive push in price beyond a significant previous high or low.

An impulse above a previous high may indicate increasing buying momentum, while an impulse below a previous low may indicate increasing selling momentum.

I do not chase the impulse itself. Instead, I wait for the market to pause and consolidate on the lower timeframes after the move, then look to join the momentum from a position of value.

## 3. What I Am Waiting For

I am waiting for:

- Price to produce a strong impulse move past a significant higher-timeframe high or low
- A period of consolidation to form on the lower timeframes following that impulse
- Price to rotate down into the value area low of the consolidation
- A hidden divergence to confirm that momentum is likely to continue
- The setup to remain within my risk parameters

No position does not mean no activity. It means the market has not yet presented a setup that meets my rules.

My entry flow is as follows:

> Scanning → Level Identified → Impulse Move Past Level → Lower-Timeframe Consolidation → Value Area Low Reached → Hidden Divergence Confirmed → Position Opened

The market presents the opportunity. I decide how aggressively that opportunity should be pursued.

## How I Turn Impulse Moves Into Opportunity

Every impulse move starts the same. What happens next is decided by the market.

I trade the moments after gold pushes decisively through important higher-timeframe highs and lows and then pauses to consolidate.

These are the areas where some of the strongest continuation moves in the market can begin.

When price drops into the value area low of a valid consolidation and a hidden divergence confirms the setup, I'll enter between three and five positions, giving me multiple opportunities to capture the continuation as it develops.

From there, the market reveals what kind of momentum we are dealing with.

- Will the continuation explode?
- Will price hesitate before pushing on?
- Or will the momentum fade completely?

As a client, you will understand exactly what is happening and why I am responding the way I am.

## The Moment the Setup Begins

Before any position is opened, I am waiting for gold to push through an important higher-timeframe level with a clear impulse move.

This could be:

- A major previous high
- A major previous low
- A clearly defined boundary that price has struggled to move beyond

Once price impulses through that area, I shift to the lower timeframes and watch for consolidation to form.

This consolidation is where the opportunity is built. As price rotates down into the value area low of that consolidation, I check for a hidden divergence. This is my confirmation that the pullback is a pause in momentum rather than a reversal.

When the value area low is reached and the hidden divergence confirms, I activate.

Instead of relying on one position, I'll enter a basket of three to five separate positions.

This gives me flexibility.

- If the continuation is powerful, I'll keep more positions active.
- If momentum weakens, I'll begin reducing exposure by taking positions off.
- If the setup fails, I'll cut the loss and step away.

The entry creates the opportunity. My risk management decides how much of that opportunity we keep.

## Three Possible Outcomes

Every new setup becomes an event to follow.

At the moment of entry, we do not yet know which scenario the market will produce.

That is where the excitement begins.

### Scenario 1: The Full Continuation

This is the move everyone wants to see.

Gold completes its consolidation, lifts away from the value area low and continues moving decisively in the direction of the original impulse.

There is little hesitation, limited retracement and strong momentum behind the move.

Because the continuation remains clean, I have more reason to keep the full position basket active.

**What you may see**

- I enter three to five positions at the value area low
- Price lifts out of the consolidation and begins moving in the intended direction
- I keep more positions open
- Profits build across the basket
- I stay involved as momentum continues

This is where my entry structure can become extremely powerful.

Instead of capturing the move with only one position, several positions may participate at the same time.

The cleaner the continuation, the more positions I can keep active and the more profit potential the market may present.

This is the ideal momentum environment.

I have identified the opportunity, the hidden divergence has confirmed it and the position basket is given room to participate.

### Scenario 2: The Managed Move

The move is still alive, but I become more selective.

Not every successful continuation moves in a straight line.

Sometimes price lifts from the value area low, moves into profit and then slows down, stalls or pulls back deeper into the range.

In this situation, I may begin reducing risk.

For example, I may enter five positions, but only keep two active long enough to benefit from the continuation.

**What you may see**

- I initially open three to five positions
- Price begins moving in the direction of the impulse
- Momentum temporarily slows
- I close or de-risk some positions
- I allow the remaining positions to continue
- Part of the original basket finishes in profit

This does not mean the trade has gone wrong.

It means the market has not yet earned the right for every position to stay open.

I protect the account by reducing unnecessary exposure while keeping selected positions involved in case momentum returns.

I take risk away from weaker positions while keeping the strongest opportunities alive.

This is a key part of how I operate.

I do not simply hold everything and hope.

I adapt.

### Scenario 3: The Failed Setup

The market rejects the continuation and I respond.

Occasionally, price will reach the value area low, trigger my entry conditions and then push down through the consolidation instead of continuing higher.

This tells me the impulse has run out of energy and the pause was in fact the start of a reversal.

I may initially enter the position basket because my entry conditions were present, but if momentum disappears, I begin cutting exposure.

**What you may see**

- Price reaches the value area low and the divergence confirms
- I enter three to five positions
- The market pushes through the consolidation in the wrong direction
- I begin closing positions
- I accept an overall loss
- I wait for the next opportunity

Losses are a normal part of trading.

The important point is how I respond when the market does not behave as expected.

Rather than holding the position indefinitely, my risk-management framework is designed to recognise when the opportunity is weakening and reduce the damage.

When the market fails to deliver momentum, I protect the account and move on.

## Why Multiple Positions Matter

The position basket is one of the most important parts of how I trade.

A single position only gives me one decision: Hold or close.

Multiple positions give me more control.

I can:

- Keep the full basket active during strong momentum
- Close selected positions during uncertainty
- Reduce risk without abandoning the opportunity completely
- Allow the best-performing positions to run
- Exit fully if the setup fails

This means I can react differently depending on the quality of the continuation.

- **Strong continuation** — I keep more positions active.
- **Medium continuation** — I remove some positions and let the remaining positions continue.
- **Failed setup** — I close the basket and contain the loss.

I am not guessing how much profit to take before the trade begins.

I allow the market to show how much opportunity is available.

## The Market Determines the Performance

I cannot control whether gold produces a strong continuation, a slow continuation or a failed setup.

What I can control is how I respond.

This is why performance may look different from one trade to the next.

- A clean continuation may produce a larger result because I keep several positions active.
- A slower continuation may produce a more modest result because I have reduced some exposure.
- A failed setup may result in a controlled loss.

I respond to the quality of the opportunity the market presents.

Instead of looking only at whether one trade won or lost, you can look at the type of move the market delivered and how effectively I managed it.

## What to Watch When Positions Open

When I enter, there are three things to watch.

1. **How quickly does price lift away from the value area low?**
   Fast continuation may indicate a cleaner setup.

2. **Does price stall or rotate back into the consolidation?**
   This may cause me to reduce some positions while keeping others active.

3. **How many positions remain open?**
   The number of remaining positions can help show how I am responding to the move.

- If several positions remain active, the continuation may still be showing strong momentum.
- If only one or two positions remain, I may be protecting profit or reducing risk.
- If all positions close, the setup may no longer meet my criteria.

This gives you a transparent view into what I am seeing and why I am making certain decisions.

## Quiet Periods Are Part of the Process

This is not designed to trade for the sake of being active.

I wait for price to impulse past important higher-timeframe levels and then build a clean consolidation on the lower timeframes.

If gold is trading in the middle of a range, or no valid impulse and consolidation sequence is present, I may remain inactive.

That does not mean I have stopped working.

It means the market has not yet produced the type of opportunity I am built to trade.

I wait through ordinary market conditions so I can be ready when meaningful momentum arrives.

When price begins pushing towards a major high or low, the next setup starts coming into view.

That is when attention begins to build.

## Every Move Tells a Different Story

Each setup begins with the same sequence.

- Gold impulses past an important level.
- The consolidation forms.
- Price reaches the value area low, the hidden divergence confirms and I enter my positions.

Then the market decides what comes next.

- Will all five positions stay active?
- Will I de-risk and allow two positions to continue?
- Or will the setup fail and cause me to exit?

This makes every trade easy to follow.

You are not simply watching a profit-and-loss figure move up and down.

You understand the structure behind the trade, the opportunity being targeted and the decisions I am making as the move unfolds.

## The Momentum Strategy Formula

**Enter with value.**
I'll enter multiple positions at the value area low of a qualified consolidation, confirmed by hidden divergence.

**Hold with momentum.**
The cleaner the continuation, the more positions I'll keep active.

**Reduce with uncertainty.**
When momentum slows, I'll reduce exposure while keeping part of the opportunity alive.

**Exit when the setup fails.**
If price rejects the continuation and pushes back through the consolidation, I'll cut the losses and wait for the next opportunity.
