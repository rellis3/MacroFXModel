Every engine on this dashboard scores its currencies on the same −1..+1 scale. This page groups those dimensions into six factors — rates & policy (rate differential, real yield, yield curve), inflation (CPI, PPI), growth (GDP, business activity), labour market, domestic demand (retail sales, consumer confidence), external balance (trade balance) — and averages the factors with equal weight.

Why group first. Three of those dimensions measure rates and two measure inflation. A flat average across all twelve therefore hands rates roughly a quarter of the score and inflation a sixth — not because anyone decided rates matters three times more than the labour market, but because three series happen to point at it. Adding a fourth rates series tomorrow would quietly push rates to a third of the score with nobody having chosen that. An accidental weighting is worse than a stated one you disagree with, because there is nothing to argue with. Equal weight per factor is the stated choice; if it ever changes it should carry evidence with it.

What it is for. Explanation, not prediction. Macro-as-signal has been tested and banked as null in this project five times over, so this composite is not a forecast — it is the answer to "why does the FX board look like this today", and the factor lines are where that answer actually lives. Central-bank text sentiment is shown per currency but is in no factor and scores nothing: its momentum was tested against forward price and banked null too.

Missing data is treated as missing, never as neutral — a factor with nothing fresh behind it is left out rather than counted as zero, and the n/6 factors column says how much of each score is genuinely backed.
