How to read a market, in three questions. First, what is today organised around — on any given day one or two things are moving everything else, and the rest are passengers. Second, what does X do to Y — separately for the same window (which explains) and for tomorrow (which is what you would trade on, and is mostly nothing). Third, what actually moved. This page is those three, in that order. Nothing here is a trade; the whole point is that most cross-asset stories are true as description and worthless as prediction, and the only way to know which is which is to have tested them and kept the answer.

1 · Who is driving

Each market's daily moves against every other market's, over the last 60 sessions. High up the list = this thing moves when everything else moves. It is the tape's organising principle, not its cause: two markets can move together because a third moved both. Description. No forward claim.
