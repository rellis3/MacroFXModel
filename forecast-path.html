📐 Forecast Path — Cone & Replay

What the model actually claims about the next N days (D1) or next few hours (M5/M15, session-shaped σ) — drawn, then graded against history. Not a strategy; a calibration viewer.

What this page is

The prediction line, in one sentence

The predicted line is the market's recent average daily drift projected forward — around it, the model runs many dice-rolls (Monte-Carlo simulations) using the pair's current volatility and the typical rhythm of each trading session, and the line is simply the middle of where all those simulated futures agree the price is most likely to be.

How the cone works

The cone is the expected price envelope. Its width comes from the pair's live volatility, shaped by the clock — quiet through Asia, widest into the London and New York opens — so it breathes with the trading day rather than growing at a constant rate. The inner (green) band claims to contain price half the time; the outer (amber) band 75% of the time. Toggling Sample paths shows the individual simulated futures the claim is built from; their median is the "most-agreed path". The calc switch chooses whose band constants build the daily envelopes (COG's export calibration by default, or the platform's Feller bands).

Volatility left in the day

Pure climatology — no prediction, just this pair's own range history. It shows how much high-low range you've used so far today, whether that's busy or quiet for this hour (a percentile vs prior same-hour days), the typical full-day range, and how much range a typical day still has left from here. Use it to gauge whether the day's move is mostly spent or there's room to run — a quiet morning (low percentile) tends to have more still to come.

The Day-forecast lines (the daily export, drawn)

The Day forecast toggle draws the daily vol & range forecast — the same numbers as the daily export — as eight horizontal lines anchored at the day's opening price (the first bar of the UTC day you're viewing; scrub the replay and they re-anchor day by day, always computed only from D1 history before that day). Four families, each with an upper and lower line:

  • HL med (blue, dashed) — the median expected High-to-Low range: half of days stretch further than this from the open, half don't.
  • HL 75% (amber) — the 75th-percentile range: only 1 day in 4 stretches beyond it. These are the lines the volatility bot trades.
  • OC med (green, dotted) — the median Open-to-Close move: where the day's close typically lands relative to the open.
  • OC 75% (teal) — the 75th-percentile close displacement.

Each line is labelled on the price axis with its family, side and the active calc (COG or Feller — the switch changes these constants). Note the live bot plan anchors at London midnight, which differs from UTC midnight by one hour in summer — same maths, so lines can sit fractionally off the bot's exact levels then.

News, and why candles blow through the cone

Amber arrows are scheduled economic releases for the pair's two currencies (one marker per candle — "+N" counts a bundle of simultaneous releases). Price violence usually has an arrow next to it, and releases falling inside the future cone window are listed in the forecast card — so when the picture jumps, the "why" is printed beside it. With Event σ on, the cone also widens into release windows: the widening multiplier is learned from this pair's own history around past releases (never asserted), and the calibration card carries an A/B — event windows graded with the widening off and on — so the conditioner only deserves to stay on where that A/B says it helps. Implied σ (EUR/USD, GOLD, indices only) is a second, measured-first conditioner: it scales the whole day's cone by implied vol (EVZ/GVZ/VIX) versus its own recent baseline, testing whether forward-looking implied vol adds anything beyond the realized σ already in the cone. Default OFF — its own A/B in the calibration card decides, and the honest prior is a coin flip.

It grades itself

Nothing here asks to be trusted. The hindcast trail walks each recent most-agreed path forward over the candles it was predicting (purple) so you can watch the predicted trajectory diverge from reality, and marks every window with a green dot (final close inside the P75 claim) or a red square (busted) — the calibration table, made spatial. The calibration card counts, over hundreds of past windows, how often the 50% and 75% claims actually held, split by the hour the forecast was made, and reports path adherence — of every predicted bar, how often price stayed inside the median / 75th band around the path (a range fit, not a direction call). It also reports stop reality: for a stop placed at the fixed P50 / P75 distance, how often the intrabar high/low touches that line versus how often the close actually settles beyond it. The touch rate is always higher — that gap is the reflection effect, and it's why a stop on the line gets hit more than the endpoint stats imply: size stops to the touch rate, not the close rate. The replay slider rewinds to any past moment and shows the forecast exactly as it would have looked then, built only from data available at the time. (The wiggle you see in a single most-agreed path is Monte-Carlo sampling noise around the smooth drift — the model claims the envelope, not the exact wiggles.)

Target reachability (the "price" tool)

Enter any level — a take-profit, a stop, a key line — and the reachability box returns the calibrated probability price touches it within the horizon (a wick counts), plus the typical time to get there. It's a Monte-Carlo first-passage on the live cone, so it inherits the cone's calibration; the box also shows this pair's own reliability gap — how far predicted touch-rates sat from realized ones across history — so you trust the number only as far as the pair earns. This turns "is my TP realistic?" from a guess into a priced claim. Still not direction: it prices whether a level is reachable, not which way to bet.

What this is NOT

It does not predict direction. The calibration card shows the up/down call is roughly a coin flip — that is the honest, expected result for a liquid market, not a defect. What the model genuinely knows is the size of the plausible move and when the market moves most, far better than which way it goes. Read it as a rangefinder and a risk map — where price can plausibly reach by when, and which levels sit inside today's reachable range — not as a buy/sell signal. It is a calibration viewer, not a strategy, and any candle-shaped detail in the sample paths is illustrative, not forecast.

How to read this honestly. The cone is the model's real claim: a capped EWMA drift plus uncertainty envelopes. On D1 the envelopes are the Feller close-displacement bands from the SAME σ math as the live forecaster (volSigmaSeries → computeBands, imported — never copied). On M15 σ is per-bar EWMA shaped by a causal hour-of-day profile (Asia quiet, London/NY loud — learned from strictly-prior data), with Gaussian envelopes. The P50 band claims 50% containment, P75 claims 75%, drift direction claims to beat a coin flip — the calibration card grades every claim on non-overlapping windows. Sample paths are draws from that same claim (wicks cosmetic); their per-step median is the "most-agreed path". The hindcast trail draws the very windows the calibration card grades — each faint segment is the path the model claimed at that moment (causally), ending green (final close inside the P75 claim) or red (busted). Day forecast draws the daily export's lines over the intraday candles, anchored at the day's opening price — blue dashed = H-L median, amber = H-L 75th (the lines the volatility bot trades), green dotted = O-C median, teal = O-C 75th — each axis-labelled with family/side/calc; σ from real D1 history strictly before that day. Levels are prior-day H/L, pivots, daily opens and round numbers (axis-labelled; zoom out if only one is in view). The calc switch (COG default / Platform-Feller) swaps the band constants behind the D1 cone envelopes + its calibration card and the Day-forecast lines — the drift ("most-agreed") line never moves with the calc, and the intraday cone keeps its own per-bar Gaussian claim (both calibrations are daily-session constants that don't apply per 5/15-min step). On D1 the calibration card grades whichever calc is selected — COG's tighter medians claim the same 50/75%, so the card shows honestly whether his lines or the platform's hold their claim better. If containment sits near its claim and direction sits at ~50%, that IS the honest result: the model knows range well and direction barely — don't let the picture say otherwise.
