📐 Forecast Path — Cone & Replay

What the model actually claims about the next N days (D1) or next few hours (M5/M15, session-shaped σ) — drawn, then graded against history. Not a strategy; a calibration viewer.

What this page is

The prediction line, in one sentence

The predicted line is the market's recent average daily drift projected forward — around it, the model runs many dice-rolls (Monte-Carlo simulations) using the pair's current volatility and the typical rhythm of each trading session, and the line is simply the middle of where all those simulated futures agree the price is most likely to be.

How the cone works

The cone is the expected price envelope. Its width comes from the pair's live volatility, shaped by the clock — quiet through Asia, widest into the London and New York opens — so it breathes with the trading day rather than growing at a constant rate. The inner (green) band claims to contain price half the time; the outer (amber) band 75% of the time. Toggling Sample paths shows the individual simulated futures the claim is built from; their median is the "most-agreed path". The calc switch chooses whose band constants build the daily envelopes (COG's export calibration by default, or the platform's Feller bands).

News, and why candles blow through the cone

Amber arrows are scheduled economic releases for the pair's two currencies (one marker per candle — "+N" counts a bundle of simultaneous releases). Price violence usually has an arrow next to it, and releases falling inside the future cone window are listed in the forecast card — so when the picture jumps, the "why" is printed beside it.

It grades itself

Nothing here asks to be trusted. The hindcast trail draws every past prediction over the candles it was predicting — ending in a green dot where the claim held and a red square where reality busted it — and the calibration card counts, over hundreds of past windows, how often the 50% and 75% claims actually held, split by the hour the forecast was made. The replay slider rewinds to any past moment and shows the forecast exactly as it would have looked then, built only from data available at the time.

What this is NOT

It does not predict direction. The calibration card shows the up/down call is roughly a coin flip — that is the honest, expected result for a liquid market, not a defect. What the model genuinely knows is the size of the plausible move and when the market moves most, far better than which way it goes. Read it as a rangefinder and a risk map — where price can plausibly reach by when, and which levels sit inside today's reachable range — not as a buy/sell signal. It is a calibration viewer, not a strategy, and any candle-shaped detail in the sample paths is illustrative, not forecast.

How to read this honestly. The cone is the model's real claim: a capped EWMA drift plus uncertainty envelopes. On D1 the envelopes are the Feller close-displacement bands from the SAME σ math as the live forecaster (volSigmaSeries → computeBands, imported — never copied). On M15 σ is per-bar EWMA shaped by a causal hour-of-day profile (Asia quiet, London/NY loud — learned from strictly-prior data), with Gaussian envelopes. The P50 band claims 50% containment, P75 claims 75%, drift direction claims to beat a coin flip — the calibration card grades every claim on non-overlapping windows. Sample paths are draws from that same claim (wicks cosmetic); their per-step median is the "most-agreed path". The hindcast trail draws the very windows the calibration card grades — each faint segment is the path the model claimed at that moment (causally), ending green (final close inside the P75 claim) or red (busted). Day forecast draws the vol-forecast v2 daily lines over the intraday candles — blue dashed = H-L median, amber = H-L 75th (the lines the volatility bot trades), green dotted = O-C median — off each replay day's open, σ from real D1 history strictly before that day. Levels are prior-day H/L, pivots, daily opens and round numbers (axis-labelled; zoom out if only one is in view). The calc switch (COG default / Platform-Feller) swaps the band constants behind the D1 cone envelopes + its calibration card and the Day-forecast lines — the drift ("most-agreed") line never moves with the calc, and the intraday cone keeps its own per-bar Gaussian claim (both calibrations are daily-session constants that don't apply per 5/15-min step). On D1 the calibration card grades whichever calc is selected — COG's tighter medians claim the same 50/75%, so the card shows honestly whether his lines or the platform's hold their claim better. If containment sits near its claim and direction sits at ~50%, that IS the honest result: the model knows range well and direction barely — don't let the picture say otherwise.
