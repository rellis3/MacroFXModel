What this is

The trading system is the Telegram alert itself, and nothing else: a touch-motif forms, price closes through the level, the 🟢/🔴 alert fires, and a trade is entered on the open of the next H1 bar with a 20-pip stop and a 1.5R target — the frozen grid motif_track.py already logs live. Every alert that was sent is a trade here. By default there are no gates, filters, caps or overlays — no concurrency limit, no exposure cap, no drawdown throttle, no currency rule, no session or regime filter, no minimum on the alert's own confidence panel. Nothing is skipped: —.

AnalogML/motif_alert_backtest.py replays the bot's own emission path bar by bar over 2016–2026 — one live touch-run per pair, the provisional refusal, one nearing alert per run, entry only once an H1 close confirms — and races exactly those alerts through pylego.barrier_race, the same walker the tracked record uses. It re-tunes nothing.

The only two inputs above are risk % per trade and a vol target, and neither removes a trade: the first scales R into account %, the second is a display lens for one card. Pair selection below subsets the book; it does not change any pair's own trades.
Two stated divergences from live, both measured rather than assumed away. (1) Tick vs bar — the nearing poller sees a live quote every 60s; a backtest sees H1 bars, so a 👀 is counted when the bar's whole traded range came within 0.5 × ATR of the level. The strictly-tighter close-only count is exported beside it (—). This affects the 👀 funnel counts only — never a trade, since entry always waits for a confirmed H1 close. (2) Confidence panel — the 📊 played out · PF · n figures are replayed with a causal cutoff (only motifs confirmed strictly before the alert bar), which live does not need; a motif confirmed but still open at alert time is excluded from the replayed PF, where live would have raced it to a partial timeout.

Stats come from js/backtestStats.js's portfolioStats, js/metricsCore.js's summarizeTrades/sharpeStdError/ minTrackRecordLength, and js/levelAtlasVoteReview.js's buildPortfolioDailySeries/computeIntradayMAE — shared measurement bricks, reused so a number here and the same number elsewhere in the repo cannot drift. No metric is computed in this page, and no trading rule is imported from any other strategy.

Sharpe/DD are mark-to-close with no spread variation. Compounding at a fixed % of ever-growing equity over ~30k trades makes the raw CAGR absurd; read Sharpe, max DD and PF, not CAGR.

If you reinvest instead (compounded — risk grows with equity)

Shown for comparison only. Compounding at a fixed % of ever-growing equity over ~30k trades produces absurd totals, which is why the fixed-risk figures above are the ones this page leads with.

Cumulative portfolio return %

Compounded reinvests each day's % return into the next day's base (what a real account does). Non-compounded just sums the daily % returns (what a fixed-size bet every day would show) — the gap between the two lines IS the compounding effect. Each has its own axis, so both stay readable at once. A flat-then-explosive compounded curve on a LINEAR axis is the expected shape of a STABLE growth rate, not evidence it is accelerating — toggle log scale to see the real underlying rate (a constant rate draws as a straight line; only a genuine change in slope means the edge itself changed).

Monte Carlo — randomised trade order

Same trades, same days — just reshuffled into a different chronological order — replayed times to build a corridor of possible equity curves. This is path-dependent risk: total return is invariant to order (it is the same sum either way), but drawdown and how bumpy the ride feels are NOT — a bad stretch landing early vs spread out changes what you would actually have lived through. The bold line is what happened; the bands are what COULD have happened on the same edge with different luck in the ordering. Note the split: reordering cannot change the TOTAL (it is the same sum either way), so the drawdown figures below come from reshuffling, while the return range and P(profitable) come from resampling WITH replacement — a different question (sample-size uncertainty) that reshuffling is structurally unable to answer.

Extreme tail fit — Peaks-Over-Threshold (Generalized Pareto)

Capacity — cost-sensitivity test

Proxy for "how much size/crowding can this absorb": re-prices every trade at a multiple of its own pair's real transaction cost (bigger size and more competition for the same levels both show up as effectively higher realised cost) and finds where the edge dies. Because the stop is a fixed 20 pips, a pair's cost is a constant number of R, so this is exact arithmetic on the recorded R rather than a re-race. It does NOT give a clean capital figure — that needs a real size-to-slippage assumption, which there is no order-book data here to calibrate — but it gives the right order of magnitude and, more usefully, exactly which pairs run out of room first.

Monthly & daily breakdown — click a month to see the days inside it

Non-compounded (additive) daily returns, the same convention as the fixed-risk KPIs — every trading day, no reinvestment assumption, so a bad week does not look worse or better just from where it lands on the equity curve. Most recent month first. Click any row to expand its individual trading days and see which pairs made them up — useful for checking whether a rough week you are living through actually looks unusual against the strategy's own history, or is well within normal variance.

A dial YOU set, not a cap the system imposes — it does not limit upside, it shows what the SAME trades look like scaled to whatever volatility you are willing to run.

Outlier sensitivity — is this carried by a handful of trades?

Excess kurtosis above describes the daily-return distribution's tail shape, but not whether a few individual trades do the work — several trades can land on the same day and average out. This reads per-trade P&L directly: what share of total profit comes from the biggest winners, and what happens to Sharpe/CAGR/drawdown if you had simply missed them.
