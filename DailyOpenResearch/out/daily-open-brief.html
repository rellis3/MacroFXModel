The 23:00 Open

Ten years of one-minute data, run through the retail daily-open playbook: opening-range breakouts, impulse legs with fib pullbacks, VWAP bands, the Asian range into London, prior-day levels, the first candle on every timeframe. Each claim is scored against what a random walk would produce, and every trade sim shows gross next to net so you can see what the spread does.

1 · Where the volatility is

Mean one-minute true range in ADR units for the first eight hours after the open. The spike in the first block is the CME re-open on gold and NQ (the "Asian injection"); the second rise around +2h to +3h is Tokyo and Shanghai cash. None of it compares with London or New York, which is where the anchor comparison says an open study should live.

2 · When the day's extremes form

Share of days whose high or low sits in each 30-minute bucket after the open. The first bucket is high by construction (the open is often near an extreme), the Asian and London hours are flat, and the mass is in 13:00–16:00 UK. The first hour holds an extreme less often than a shuffled ordering of the same day's moves would, on all three instruments.

3 · Impulse leg, fib pullback

A leg is drawable the first time price gives back 23.6% of a swing of at least 0.12 ADR from the last confirmed pivot, within four hours of the open. Nothing after that moment defines the leg. Depth is the fraction of the leg given back; a TradingView fib drawn low-to-high shows 1 − depth, so the 0.618 line on that chart is 0.382 depth here. The null is the random-walk probability from the close of the bar that first reached the depth.

After a continuation, the extension reached before the day ends or price falls back through half the original leg: 1.1× on 82–86% of legs, 1.272× on 64–68%, 1.618× on 43–48%. Those match the 1.1 / 1.2 / 1.618 targets on the chart in the screenshot, but they are close to what an unbiased walk gives as well.

5 · Asia range into London and New York

The Asia range (23:00 to 07:00 UK) is the retail trader's second favourite box. Which sides get taken says something about each instrument's character: gold spends its day inside or on one side of the box, NQ runs through both sides more often than not.

6 · The rest of the checklist

What I would investigate next

  1. Move the anchor to where the range is. Re-run the suite at London 08:00 and New York 14:30 UK. The anchor comparison puts 22–28% of the day's range in the first hour after 08:00 versus 9–12% after 23:00, and on EURUSD it is the only open worth anchoring to.
  2. Measure the real 23:00 spread. Every sim here is decided by cost, and rollover spreads on gold and indices are typically several times the daytime spread for exactly the minutes the injection candle prints. Log live spreads by minute-since-open and re-run with the measured curve.
  3. Scale the impulse-continuation effect up. It is the only stable number above null. Test legs of 0.35 ADR and larger on 5-minute bars, across all 26 pairs and the whole day, with an ATR stop instead of the origin, and find the minimum leg size at which the edge exceeds the spread.
  4. Treat NQ and gold differently in Asia. NQ trends away from VWAP and takes both sides of its range 54% of the time; gold reverts and takes both sides 35%. Continuation logic on NQ and range logic on gold, as a paired walk-forward.
  5. Condition on the previous session, not the calendar. Major-news days changed nothing. The session-handoff work already in this repo shows volatility clusters session to session; condition the open studies on the prior NY session's range and where it closed.
  6. Use the gap the right way round. The 0.15–0.30 ADR gaps fill 74–82% of the time with a median fill over an hour; test fading them with a stop beyond the opening extreme and study the days they do not fill.
  7. Replace the fixed reaction race at prior levels with a distribution. Record full MFE/MAE after the first touch, split by the session it is touched in.
  8. Walk-forward the two best-looking cells with their neighbours. Gold wide-Asia-range breakout (+0.05R net, n=549) and EURUSD sweep-fade (gross t=2.3). Both are single cells out of dozens; a rolling annual refit is the honest test, and the leave-one-out tooling in the repo already exists for it.
  9. Get bid/ask bars. Fib limit fills showed adverse selection worth 3–8% of R; with two-sided bars the fill assumption becomes exact.

Method notes

  • Anchor: the broker daily candle (17:00 New York boundary). The feed follows CME hours, so gold and NQ print their first bar at 18:00 NY = 23:00 UK all year; EURUSD trades through it.
  • ADR = trailing 20-day median daily range, strictly prior. All distances are in ADR so the instruments compare directly.
  • Nulls: driftless random walk for every race (with the fill or breakout bar's overshoot accounted for), and a shuffled-returns null for the early-extreme claims. Wilson 95% intervals; minimum 30 per reported cell; 60/40 in-sample/out-of-sample by date; per-year, weekday, vol-regime and major-news-day tables in the full reports.
