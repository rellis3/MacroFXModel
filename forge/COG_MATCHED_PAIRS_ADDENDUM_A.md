# COG-MATCHED-PAIRS, Addendum A: pre-run audit, leg decomposition, and the exact p-values fed to the cross-candidate correction

*Registered 2026-10-16, before the extra contracts' series were loaded and before any pair-level result existed. It fixes (1) the audit that must be published before the tests run, (2) how any apparent lead is attributed to a leg or to the spread, (3) the single p-value each candidate and test contributes to the family-wise correction, and (4) what happens to results that look promising but fail a gate. It changes none of the rules in COG_MATCHED_PAIRS_PREREG.md or the earlier registrations it cites.*

## 1. Audit (published in `analysis/output/cog_video_0918/MATCHED_PAIRS_AUDIT.md` before the tests run)
1. **Reference periods and expiries.** For every contract used: IBKR contract details (local symbol, contract month, last trade date). Reference periods are **derived from exchange convention, not read from IBKR**, and are labelled as such (CME SR3: reference quarter from the third Wednesday of the contract month to the third Wednesday three months later, last trade the business day before the end; ICE Euribor "I": the three-month Euribor fixing for the period starting on the third Wednesday of the contract month, last trade two business days before that Wednesday; ICE ER3 (3-month compounded ESTR): the same IMM quarter as SR3, settled on the compounded overnight rate over it). Each derived last-trade date is checked against IBKR's value; a mismatch voids the convention for that contract and the pair is flagged. Output: a table of period start, period end, last trade date for each leg and, per pair, the overlap of reference periods in days and whether the legs' last-trade dates differ (when one leg expires before the other, the line changes character after the first expiry; the window is cut at the earlier expiry).
2. **Coverage and staleness per leg and per pair.** First and last stamp, minutes with data, days, share of minutes with an unchanged price, longest unchanged run, per-UTC-hour coverage, and for each pair the share of 15-minute bars where both legs print (at least 5 minutes), where either leg is flat versus 15 minutes earlier, and where both are flat (the spread is mechanically unchanged). Usable sample: days and 15-minute bars with both legs and Nasdaq, per half, and the smallest detectable correlation (2.8 / sqrt(days)).
3. **Timestamp alignment.** (a) The convention under audit: IBKR stamps a 1-minute bar by its start; the registered 15-minute bars are right-labelled and right-closed, so the bar labelled t includes the minute stamped t, which ends at t + 1 minute (a one-minute information overhang at the decision time). All three legs of every comparison, Nasdaq's CFD and the NQ futures share this convention, so relative alignment is unchanged. Test: rebuild every pair with a strictly causal convention (the bar labelled t contains only minutes stamped up to t - 1 minute) and report the correlation of the two versions' 15-minute changes, the share of bars whose value differs, the same-hour link under both, and the number and timing of A0 turn confirmations under both. The registered tests run on the registered convention unchanged; the strict version is reported as a sensitivity run for T1, T2 and T3 primary only; if the strict version overturns any gate for a pair, that pair's result is labelled **timestamp-sensitive** and cannot pass. (b) **Asynchronous updates**: cross-correlation of the two legs' 1-minute changes at lags of -5 to +5 minutes; a lagged peak means one leg updates after the other, which can create short-lived spread moves and spurious leads, reported per pair. (c) **Session overlap**: the UTC hours in which both legs have at least half their peak coverage; test results are also reported for those hours alone.
4. **Raw versus display.** A check that the research series equals ((100 - price_US) - (100 - price_EU)) x 100 exactly, that none of the research code references the display scale (0.1), offset (1.445), inversion flag, smoothing or the Pine lead, and the list of the lines that mention "offset"/"scale"/"lead" in the research scripts (the futures-splice offset applies to Nasdaq only and is flagged as such). The Pine plot offset (`leadDays`) is never an input.

## 2. Leg decomposition for any apparent lead
For every T2 cell (candidate, W, H) that either passes the in-sample FDR or is one of the three largest in-sample |t| (so a near miss is also decomposed), run, on the same bars and splits, a joint regression of Nasdaq's forward return on the US leg's change and the euro leg's change over W (both in bp, both standardised in-sample, Nasdaq's previous 1 h and 4 h returns as controls, date-clustered SE). Classification (fixed thresholds): **spread effect** if the two coefficients have opposite signs, each |t| >= 1.0, and a Wald test of "b_US = -b_EU" is not rejected at 5%; **US-leg effect** if the euro coefficient has |t| < 1.0 and the US coefficient |t| >= 2.0 in-sample; **euro-leg effect** the mirror; **common level (both legs the same sign)** if the coefficients share a sign with both |t| >= 1.0; otherwise **unattributed**. A pair-level result that is a leg-only or common-level effect is reported as a single-leg result: it is **not** evidence for the spread, and it is counted under the leg candidates, not the pair, in the correction.

## 3. The p-value each candidate x test contributes to the correction (fixed now)
Family = the 4 pairs x tests {T1, T2, T3, T4, T5} and the 3 legs x tests {T2, T5}: 26 candidate-test p-values (P0 is the reference and is not counted).
- **T1**: the larger (weaker) of the validation and out-of-sample one-sided bootstrap p-values for "this pair's same-hour R-squared exceeds P0's".
- **T2**: if no cell passes in-sample FDR, p = 1; otherwise the smallest over passing cells of the larger of the validation and out-of-sample one-sided p-values.
- **T3**: the smallest over families A0, A1, A2 of the larger of the validation and out-of-sample matched-control one-sided p-values on the 2 h signed return.
- **T4**: the larger of the validation and out-of-sample matched one-sided p-values on the primary spec's 2 h signed return.
- **T5**: the share of the 60 shifted-line placebo draws whose combined validation + out-of-sample IC gain is at least the real gain (floor 1/61), for 4 h direction; volatility gets its own p the same way (so T5 contributes 2 p-values per candidate: 33 in total).
Benjamini-Hochberg at 10% across the whole family. A candidate-test passing the correction but failing any registered gate of the cited study (economic, volatility regression, release exclusion, placebo mean near zero, consistency rule, timestamp sensitivity) is **exploratory**, not a result.

## 4. Skipping and underpowered pairs
A candidate whose in-sample window holds fewer than 60 days with both legs and Nasdaq (after the day screen) is run on T1 and T2 descriptively and is excluded from T3 to T5 and from the correction (reported as underpowered, not negative), as registered in COG_MATCHED_PAIRS_PREREG.md. Windows are cut at the earlier of the two legs' last trade dates (item 1).

## 5. Promising-but-failing results
Nothing is adjusted after results. A pair, leg or cell that looks promising but fails a gate is written to the exploratory table with the gate it failed. No rerun with changed specifications is allowed under this registration; a new idea needs its own registration, and its sample is then not the sample that suggested it unless the forward record is used.

## 6. Reporting (all in one document, `MATCHED_PAIRS_RESULTS.md`)
Usable sample sizes per candidate and half; per-candidate and per-test results with the gates; incremental out-of-sample performance versus the Nasdaq-only baseline (IC and R-squared gains with intervals) and the placebo mean / 95th percentile beside each real number; the correction table (raw p, BH threshold, pass/fail); the leg-decomposition table; the exploratory table.
