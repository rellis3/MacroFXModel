⚠ Correlated risk — read before selecting many pairs at once

Every pair here individually shows real edge on its own tearsheet, but FX pairs are NOT independent bets — many share a currency leg (EURUSD/EURJPY/EURGBP all carry EUR exposure, GBPUSD/GBPJPY/GBPAUD all carry GBP, etc.), so a single macro-shock day can move many selected pairs the same direction at once. Update (2026-08-30): this engine's OWN leave-one-out + OOS-validation study is now done (analysis/fib_atlas_oos_validate_pair_selection.mjs, same method as level-atlas-vote-portfolio.html's own — 70/30 split, exclusion set frozen on the first 70%, checked unchanged on the untouched last 30%). Per-trade stats — same trades, computed off INDIVIDUAL wins/losses (2026-08-31)

Everything above pools every day's trades (~—/day on average) into ONE daily return before computing Sharpe/win-rate/profit-factor — that's the right basis for portfolio risk (it reflects the real correlation across same-day trades), but it makes win rate and profit factor look far stronger than any SINGLE trade's own odds, since a day nets positive easily even off a modest per-trade edge. This card answers "is a random individual trade actually a good bet" directly: computed straight off js/metricsCore.js's summarizeTrades (same brick the single-pair tearsheet uses), no daily pooling at all. The Sharpe tile below shows the UN-annualized, single-trade figure (mean ÷ stdev of one trade's own pnl) as the headline — the annualized version (multiplying by √trades/year, shown in that tile's own sub-text) is easy to mistake for "this system is incredible" when it's really just a large multiplier applied to a much more modest number, especially at Fib Atlas's trade frequency (thousands/year). The day-level Sharpe above remains the one to size real risk from either way — this card is about individual trade quality, not portfolio risk.
