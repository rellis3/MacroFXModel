## Post-hoc audit (not pre-registered): the clock confound

The pre-registered T1 controls time with 4 sessions. Level availability depends on the clock: session opens
exist only after their time, and after 22:00 London the "previous close" becomes today's NY close, sitting
next to price. 62% of previous-close at-line touches are after 22:00 (4% of all touches), where 92–97% of
touches stall. Before 22:00 it changes nothing (37.0% vs 38.2% continue). So T1 was re-run with HOURLY cells
(line family × London hour × range used), 300 bootstrap draws (scripts/rangebook/levels_hour_audit.py):

## Verdict
- No level type, no stack, no trend alignment and no OI wall passes T2: 0 of 32 (+2 OI) tests survive
  Benjamini–Hochberg, and every at-the-line row loses net in at least one half.
- Real but small: an unfilled H1 fair value gap at the line, or 2+ level types stacked there, adds about
  1 point of CONTINUATION. The gap to break-even is 10–20 points, so it goes into the book as a column only.
- Direction of the small effects: where levels move anything it is toward MORE continuation, not rejection.
- Previous-day close and session opens looked REAL under the 4-session control; the hourly re-run shows that
  was time of day. Lesson for the book: control time by the hour whenever a feature's existence depends on the clock.
- The owner's downtrend down-line bounce: fades 37%-continue touches no better than any other line
  (fade −0.041R both halves; with-trend vs against-trend −0.3pp [−1.2, +0.4]). A 5-day trend does not change the odds.
