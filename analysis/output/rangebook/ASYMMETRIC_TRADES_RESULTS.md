## Reading
- **Win rates sit almost exactly on the break-even for their payoff** (hold 5R: 16% vs 17%; 10R: 8% vs 9%; daily open
  5R: 17% vs 17%). Before spread, these trades are fair bets, as a random walk predicts. After spread they lose, and with
  a tight stop the spread is a big slice of the risk: 0.32R per trade at a 0.1σ stop on FX/gold, 0.16R on indices.
  Halving the spread's share (0.2σ stop) halves the loss. No cell survives; 0 of 36 pass.
- **Splits that point somewhere (descriptive, need their own pre-registered test):**
  - BREAK trades do better when vol is QUIET on FX/gold (+0.063R at 0.2σ / 10R vs −0.159R heavy) and when vol is
    HEAVY on indices (+0.155 to +0.220R). Opposite directions on the two sets.
  - BREAK trades on FX/gold do better when implied vol is HIGH relative to realised (+0.12 to +0.17R top third vs
    −0.10 to −0.18R) and when the options skew is in the trade's direction (+0.02 to +0.19R vs −0.06 to −0.24R).
  - HOLD trades are worst in the high IV÷RV third, the opposite of the textbook reading.
- **What this says about the screenshots:** at 13–20 : 1, being right 1 time in 20 breaks even before costs, and the
  data says these entries are right about 1 time in 20, so the posted winners are what a fair bet looks like when
  only winners are shown. A real edge would need either fills far cheaper than a CFD spread at 01:00, or information
  the levels do not carry.
