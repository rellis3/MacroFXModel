**The insight, as in E01:** when the economy stands on one leg, an AI wobble is a *growth* scare,
so stocks and yields fall together. The data confirms the switch.

**Credit now trades with tech.** HYG/TLT vs Nasdaq correlation is +0.70 over the last 20 sessions
(−0.25 before). Credit and AI equities have become one trade, which is AP's "credit is where
someone's getting punched in the face".

**Liquidity gauges agree.** BTC vs Nasdaq correlation +0.68 (was +0.30). Silver −11.5% and gold −4.9%
since 10-09, falling *even as yields fall*. Normally lower yields help gold, so this looks like
deleveraging ("sell what you can") plus the end of the debasement trade: **gold at $4,008 has given
back essentially all of the post-Bessent rally** (peak $4,698 on 08-24).

## 2. Where it breaks
- **The weak link:** whether AI borrowers can keep raising money (Pattern N: availability,
  not price). The tell to watch: whether the *strongest* names (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon) keep or
  raise capex guidance, and how credit reacts. AP's two schools: if capex holds and spreads
  stabilise, it's School 1 (reprice, then the returns take over). If someone blinks on capex,
  it's School 2 ("the first out the door takes everyone").
- **The variable that would make it blow up** (Pattern J): credit. The S&P is only −2.5% from its
  high and VIX is 19.5 (not panic), but MOVE (bond volatility) is 103, up from 73 in early
  September. **The stress is in rates and credit, not equity volatility.** If equity vol starts
  catching up with bond vol, the correction is turning into something bigger.
- **The second credit story:** France (section 7a).

## 3. Is the data agreeing?
- **Oil is a supply shock, not a boom:** crude +12% in two weeks while copper −2.5% and natural gas
  −18%. When only oil rises and the growth-sensitive metals fall, that's a squeeze on demand.
- **Energy stocks lag oil** (XLE/crude ratio falling: 0.71 → 0.67). That's Brent's tell: equity
  investors don't believe oil profits are a durable boom.
- **Oil vs yields flipped:** daily-move correlation −0.37 now, +0.49 before. Oil up = yields *down*.
  That's Pattern M (2008 analogue): the market has changed which story it's trading.
- **The US data itself is still strong** (consensus Q3 GDP +3.0%, GDPNow 3.5%, core PCE 0.3% m/m).
  That's AP's counterweight: **the economy isn't weak yet. This is a market-led growth scare, not a
  data-led one.**

## 7. Market by market: each one's own chain

### 7a. EURUSD: two lines pulling opposite ways (Brent's chart, rebuilt)
```
 US yields fall more than German ──► US-DE 10y gap 1.74 → 1.63pp (−12bp)   ──► EUR UP
 Front-end gap (Mar-27)          ──► 1.57 → 1.54pp (≈ flat)                ──► neutral
 French spread                   ──► +25bp since 10-09 (Italy +10bp)       ──► EUR DOWN
 Euro stocks                     ──► STOXX50 −1.7%                         ──► EUR DOWN (mild)
                                         │
                                         ▼
                     EURUSD 1.1213 (10-09) → 1.1204 (10-23): FLAT
```
- **Exactly Brent's point:** EURUSD sits between the rate line (up) and the France line (down),
  so the drivers cancel and **"agnostic" is the honest answer.**
- **Who's in charge day to day: France.** EURUSD's daily moves track the French spread (−0.72) more
  than rates (−0.42).
- **Is it contagion or just France?** Italy widened +10bp vs France +25bp, and the two move together
  (+0.85). So it's **mostly France-specific with some periphery spillover**, not yet a eurozone
  crisis. If Italy starts matching France bp for bp, that changes the story.
- **Odd detail:** French equities (CAC) are about flat over two weeks while the spread widened.
  The bond market is doing the worrying.
- **Positioning:** fast money is the shortest EUR in 20 weeks. That's the setup for a squeeze if
  France calms (Moody's was Friday; the market's reaction on Monday is the information, Pattern B).
- **What decides it:** ECB on Friday (does it lean dovish because of France? EUR-negative via
  rates, but possibly *positive* via spreads if it reassures); French politics; whether US yields
  keep falling faster than Bund yields.
- **Scorecard:** Brent's E02 "EUR to 1.08-1.09" hasn't happened; his E01 "constructive EUR" hasn't
  either. EUR has been stuck since 10-09.
