#### Why FX Size Matters

The sheer size of FX markets means they're highly liquid and efficient — harder to manipulate, faster to price information. When you see FX moving, it's because large, sophisticated players are repositioning based on macro views.

For equity traders, FX is the **transmission mechanism** between central bank policy and global asset prices. Policy divergence shows up in currencies before it shows up in stocks.

### How FX Is Quoted

FX is always quoted in pairs — one currency against another:

**EUR/USD = 1.0850** means 1 Euro buys 1.0850 US Dollars. The first currency (EUR) is the "base," the second (USD) is the "quote." If this number rises, EUR is strengthening vs USD.

**USD/JPY = 150.00** means 1 US Dollar buys 150 Japanese Yen. If this number rises, USD is strengthening vs JPY (yen is weakening).

**Convention matters:** Most currencies are quoted as USD/XXX (dollar first), but EUR, GBP, AUD, NZD are quoted as XXX/USD (dollar second). This is historical convention, not logic.

### Market Participants

Who's actually trading FX? The mix matters for understanding flows:

| Participant | Motivation |
| **Central Banks** | Reserve management, intervention, policy |
| **Commercial Banks** | Client facilitation, proprietary trading |
| **Corporates** | Hedging trade/investment flows |
| **Asset Managers** | Portfolio hedging, currency overlay |
| **Hedge Funds** | Speculation, macro trades |
| **Retail** | Small fraction, mostly noise |

### FX Instruments

Most FX exposure is traded through these instruments:

| Instrument | What It Is | Who Uses It |
| **Spot** | Immediate exchange (T+2 settlement) | Everyone — the "price" you see quoted |
| **Forwards** | Agreement to exchange at future date at agreed rate | Corporates hedging future flows |
| **Swaps** | Simultaneous spot + forward (roll positions) | Banks, institutions managing funding |
| **Options** | Right (not obligation) to exchange at strike | Hedgers wanting protection with flexibility |

**Why this matters:** FX swaps are the largest segment by volume. When you hear about "dollar funding stress," it's often showing up in the swap market first. We'll cover this in the cross-currency basis section.

#### Section Summary

- **FX is the largest asset class market by turnover** — ~$7.5T daily (spot, forwards, swaps combined)

- **USD is on one side of ~88% of trades** — the dollar is the center of the FX universe

- **Currencies are quoted in pairs** — base/quote; rising pair = base strengthening

- **Sophisticated participants** — central banks, commercial banks, asset managers dominate

- **Swaps are the biggest segment** — dollar funding shows up there first

### Safe Haven vs Risk Currencies

Currencies broadly fall into two camps:

**Safe Havens (USD, JPY, CHF):** Strengthen during market stress. Investors flee to these when scared. JPY is the purest safe haven — rallies in risk-off even when Japan's fundamentals are weak.

**Risk Currencies (AUD, NZD, CAD, NOK, SEK, EM):** Strengthen when risk appetite is high. Weaken during stress. Often tied to commodities or carry trades.

**Trading Implication:** In risk-off, you expect JPY and CHF to rally, AUD and EM currencies to fall. If AUD is rallying during a supposed "risk-off" episode, question the narrative.

### Commodity Currencies

Some currencies are heavily influenced by commodity prices:

- **AUD:** Iron ore, coal → China demand proxy

- **CAD:** Oil → tracks crude prices

- **NZD:** Dairy, agriculture → China demand

- **NOK:** Oil → tracks Brent closely

**Why this matters:** If oil rallies, expect CAD and NOK to strengthen. If China stimulus boosts iron ore, AUD benefits. These linkages let you cross-check narratives.

#### Section Summary

- **Each currency has a personality** — safe haven, risk, commodity-linked

- **USD, JPY, CHF are safe havens** — strengthen in risk-off

- **AUD, NZD, CAD, NOK are risk/commodity currencies** — weaken in stress

- **JPY is the purest risk-off signal** — rallies even when Japan fundamentals are weak

- **AUD is a China proxy** — useful for gauging China sentiment

## What Drives Currencies

Different factors dominate at different timeframes. Understanding this hierarchy prevents you from using the wrong lens — and helps you identify when markets are mispricing information.

#### The Driver Hierarchy

Currency moves are driven by different factors depending on your time horizon:

**Short-term (hours to days):** Flows, positioning, sentiment, data surprises

**Medium-term (weeks to months):** Rate differentials, policy divergence — this is the sweet spot for most macro traders

**Long-term (years):** Valuation (PPP), current account balances, structural shifts

| Timeframe | Primary Drivers | Importance |
| **Intraday** | Order flow, liquidity, data releases, headlines | Noise-dominated; hard to predict |
| **Days to Weeks** | Rate expectations, risk sentiment, positioning | Mix of signal and noise |
| **Weeks to Months** | Rate differentials, policy divergence, relative growth | Highest signal — the sweet spot |
| **Months to Years** | Current account, valuation (PPP), structural flows | Slow-moving anchors; mean reversion |

### Rate Differentials: The Dominant Driver

As covered in Lesson 4, rate differentials are the **primary driver of FX over medium-term horizons**. Let's deepen the understanding:

**Why 2Y differentials matter most:** The 2-year yield reflects expectations for central bank policy over the next two years — the horizon most relevant for carry trades and macro positioning. It's more stable than short-end rates but more policy-driven than long-end.

The relationship isn't perfect, but over weeks and months, rate differentials typically explain a substantial portion of major currency pair moves — often cited as 50-80% depending on the period and pair.

**When it breaks:** Safe-haven flows can override rate differentials (JPY rallies in risk-off despite rate disadvantage). Intervention can temporarily disrupt the relationship. Extreme positioning can cause reversals against fundamentals.

### Risk Sentiment: The Overrider

In calm markets, rate differentials dominate. In stressed markets, **risk sentiment overrides**:

#### Risk-Off FX Pattern

1
Fear rises
VIX spikes, credit spreads widen, equity markets sell off

2
Safe haven bid
USD, JPY, CHF strengthen — regardless of rate differentials

3
Risk currencies sell
AUD, NZD, EM currencies weaken — carry trades unwind

4
Rate differentials resume importance
Once panic subsides, fundamental drivers reassert

### Long-Term Anchors

Over years, currencies tend to revert toward fundamental value — but slowly:

**Purchasing Power Parity (PPP):** The idea that exchange rates should adjust so identical goods cost the same across countries. In practice, currencies can deviate from PPP for years, but extreme deviations tend to mean-revert eventually.

**Current Account Balance:** Countries running persistent deficits need to attract foreign capital, often requiring weaker currencies or higher rates. Surplus countries have structural currency support.

**Practical implication:** Long-term valuation doesn't help with timing, but knowing a currency is extremely over/undervalued on PPP provides context. Extreme undervaluation can limit downside; extreme overvaluation can limit upside — eventually.

#### Section Summary

- **Different drivers at different timeframes** — match your analysis to your horizon

- **Medium-term (weeks to months) is the sweet spot** — rate differentials dominate

- **Risk sentiment can override fundamentals** — safe havens rally in stress regardless of rates

- **Long-term anchors (PPP, current account) are slow** — useful for context, not timing

## The Dollar Cycle & Dollar Smile

The US dollar matters more than any other currency. It's on one side of ~88% of all FX transactions, it's the global reserve currency, and its strength or weakness ripples through **every** asset class globally. Understanding dollar dynamics is essential for any macro trader.

#### Why the Dollar Is Special

**Reserve currency:** Central banks hold ~58-60% of reserves in USD (declining gradually but still dominant). There's always structural demand.

**Invoicing currency:** Most global trade is invoiced in dollars, even between non-US countries.

**Funding currency:** Most global debt is denominated in dollars — when the dollar strengthens, this debt becomes harder to service.

**Safe haven:** In crisis, the world wants dollars — even when the crisis originates in the US.

### The Dollar Smile Theory

The dollar is strong at both extremes — US exceptionalism OR global panic. It's weak in the middle.

**US Exceptionalism** US growth outperforms Fed more hawkish → USD Strong
**Synchronized Growth** Global growth strong Risk appetite high → USD Weak
**Global Risk-Off** Fear dominates Flight to safety → USD Strong
### Reading the Dollar Smile

The dollar smile framework helps you understand *why* the dollar is moving:

| Regime | Characteristics | USD Direction |
| **Left Side (US Exceptionalism)** | US growth > RoW, Fed hawkish, rate differentials favor USD | Strong |
| **Middle (Synchronized Growth)** | Global growth strong, risk appetite high, carry trades work | Weak |
| **Right Side (Risk-Off)** | Global panic, VIX spiking, flight to safety | Strong |

**Key insight:** A strong dollar doesn't always mean the same thing. In 2022, USD was strong for "left side" reasons (Fed hawkish, rate differentials). In March 2020, USD was strong for "right side" reasons (panic). The implications for other assets differ.

### Dollar Cycles

The dollar moves in long cycles — historically lasting roughly 7-10 years peak-to-peak:

- **1980s cycle:** Strong dollar early (Volcker tightening), weak late decade

- **1990s cycle:** Weak early, strong late (tech boom, US exceptionalism)

- **2000s cycle:** Weak dollar (EM boom, commodity supercycle)

- **2010s cycle:** Dollar bottomed ~2011, strengthened through 2022

**Caution:** Cycle timing is imprecise. Don't try to call major dollar turns — focus on understanding the current regime and what would change it.

### Dollar Strength: Good or Bad for Risk Assets?

It depends on *why* the dollar is strong:

| Dollar Strength Driver | Risk Asset Impact | Why |
| **US growth outperformance** | Mixed to positive (for US) | Strong US economy supports US equities; pressures EM and exporters |
| **Fed hawkishness** | Negative | Higher rates = lower equity valuations; especially hits growth stocks |
| **Global risk-off** | Negative | Dollar rallying because everything else is selling; risk assets suffering |

**Trading implication:** Don't just ask "is the dollar strong?" Ask "why is the dollar strong?" The answer determines what it means for your portfolio.

#### Section Summary

- **The dollar is the center of global finance** — reserve currency, invoicing currency, funding currency

- **Dollar Smile:** Strong when US exceptional OR global risk-off; weak when global growth synchronized

- **Dollar cycles last roughly 7-10 years** — don't try to time major turns precisely

- **Why the dollar is strong matters** — same direction, different implications for risk assets

## Carry Trades — Mechanics and Risks

The carry trade — borrowing in low-yielding currencies to invest in high-yielding ones — is one of the most persistent strategies in FX. Understanding its mechanics helps you anticipate currency moves and recognize when crowded carry trades are at risk of violent unwinds.

#### Carry Trade in Plain English

Imagine you can borrow Japanese yen at 0.1% interest and invest in Mexican pesos earning 10%. You pocket the 9.9% difference — that's the "carry."

The catch? You're exposed to currency moves. If the peso falls 10% against the yen, you've lost more than a year's carry. If the peso stays stable or rises, you collect steady returns.

**Carry trades are often described as "picking up pennies in front of a steamroller"** — steady small gains, occasional catastrophic losses.

### Carry Trade Mechanics

Carry Return = Interest Rate Differential − Hedging Cost
In practice, the carry return comes from:

- **Interest differential:** The gap between rates in the two currencies

- **Spot return:** Whether the high-yielding currency appreciates or depreciates

- **Roll yield:** The forward points when rolling positions

In calm markets, carry returns are dominated by the interest differential — you earn steady yield. In volatile markets, spot moves dominate — and can overwhelm years of carry in days.

### Classic Carry Pairs

Historically popular funding and investment currencies:

| Role | Currencies | Why |
| **Funding (borrow)** | JPY, CHF, EUR (historically) | Low or negative rates; stable economies |
| **Investment (buy)** | AUD, NZD, EM currencies | High rates; commodity exposure |

**The JPY carry trade:** For decades, Japan's near-zero rates made JPY the world's favorite funding currency. Trillions of dollars of carry trades have been built on borrowed yen. This is why JPY can move violently during risk-off — carry unwinds.

### Why Carry Works (Usually)

In theory, high-yielding currencies should depreciate to offset the interest advantage (Uncovered Interest Parity). In practice, they often don't — at least not immediately:

- **Risk premium:** High rates often reflect higher risk — carry return is compensation for that risk

- **Momentum:** Capital flows toward yield, which can strengthen the high-yield currency further

- **Slow adjustment:** Markets don't always price in expected depreciation efficiently

Historical studies suggest carry strategies have generated positive returns over long periods, but with significant drawdown risk during crises.

### Carry Unwind: The Risk

When carry trades unwind, they can be violent and self-reinforcing:

#### Anatomy of a Carry Unwind

1
Trigger event
Risk-off shock, volatility spike, unexpected policy shift

2
Initial selling
Some carry traders close positions, selling high-yield currencies

3
Spot moves against carry
High-yield currencies weaken; funding currencies (JPY) strengthen

4
Stop-losses trigger
More traders forced to exit; selling begets selling

5
Cascade
JPY can rally 5-10% in days; years of carry wiped out

**The reflexivity:** Carry unwinds are self-reinforcing. As JPY strengthens, more carry traders hit stop-losses, forcing more JPY buying, causing more strengthening. This is why JPY moves can be explosive in risk-off.

#### 🎯 Practical Application: Monitoring Carry

- **Watch JPY in risk-off:** If JPY starts rallying sharply, carry is unwinding — be cautious on all risk positions

- **COT positioning:** CFTC data shows speculative positioning in currency futures; extreme short JPY = crowded carry

- **Volatility as warning:** Carry works in low-vol environments; rising FX volatility = carry trade headwind

- **Rate differential narrowing:** If differentials compress (e.g., Fed cuts), carry becomes less attractive

#### Section Summary

- **Carry = borrowing low-yield to invest in high-yield** — earns the rate differential

- **JPY is the classic funding currency** — near-zero rates for decades

- **Carry works in calm markets** — steady returns when volatility is low

- **Unwinds are violent and self-reinforcing** — can wipe out years of carry in days

- **JPY strength in risk-off signals carry unwind** — a warning for all risk positions

## Cross-Currency Basis — Dollar Funding Stress

This is **higher-tier content** that institutional traders watch closely. The cross-currency basis reveals stress in global dollar funding markets — information that doesn't show up in spot FX rates but matters enormously for understanding global financial conditions.

#### Why This Is Advanced But Important

The cross-currency basis is a deviation from a theoretical relationship (Covered Interest Parity) that "shouldn't" exist in efficient markets. Yet it persists, and when it moves sharply, it signals real stress in the global financial system.

Understanding this gives you insight into dollar funding conditions that most retail traders (and many professionals) miss entirely.

### What Is Covered Interest Parity?

**Covered Interest Parity (CIP)** is a theoretical relationship that says you shouldn't be able to earn risk-free arbitrage by borrowing in one currency and lending in another (after hedging FX risk).

In formula terms: the interest rate differential between two currencies should equal the forward premium/discount. If US rates are higher than Japan rates, the forward should show yen at a premium (dollar at a discount) to offset the rate advantage.

Forward Premium ≈ Interest Rate Differential
If this relationship held perfectly, you'd earn the same return investing in either currency (hedged). Arbitrage would eliminate any deviation instantly.

### The Basis: CIP Breakdown

In reality, CIP doesn't hold perfectly. The **cross-currency basis** is the deviation:

**Negative basis:** It costs MORE to obtain dollars via FX swaps than CIP predicts. Non-US entities are paying a premium for dollar funding. This signals dollar scarcity / stress.

**Positive basis:** It costs LESS to obtain dollars than CIP predicts. Rare, usually minor.

The basis is typically quoted in basis points. EUR/USD basis of -30bps means European entities pay 30bps extra per year to swap euros for dollar funding.

### Why Does the Basis Exist?

Several factors cause the basis to deviate from zero:

- **Dollar demand:** Global entities need dollars for trade, debt servicing, and reserve holdings

- **Bank balance sheet constraints:** Post-2008 regulations limit banks' ability to arbitrage small deviations

- **Quarter-end effects:** Banks reduce balance sheets for reporting dates, temporarily widening basis

- **Risk aversion:** In stress, counterparty concerns prevent arbitrage

### Reading the Basis as a Stress Indicator

| EUR/USD Basis Level | Interpretation |
| **0 to -20 bps** | Normal — Typical range, no stress |
| **-20 to -50 bps** | Elevated — Some dollar demand pressure |
| **-50 to -100 bps** | Stressed — Significant dollar funding pressure |
| **Below -100 bps** | Crisis — Severe dollar shortage, 2008/2020-style |

**Note:** These levels are rough guideposts. The basis structure has evolved over time as regulations changed. What matters most is the direction and speed of change, not absolute levels.

#### 📚 Case Study: March 2020 Dollar Squeeze

The March 2020 COVID panic caused a classic dollar funding crisis:

- **Basis blew out:** EUR/USD basis moved from ~-20bps to beyond -100bps in days

- **DXY surged:** Despite US being the pandemic epicenter, the dollar rallied as everyone needed dollars

- **Fed response:** Fed opened swap lines with foreign central banks, providing dollar liquidity globally

- **Basis normalized:** Once swap lines were active, basis quickly recovered — the Fed effectively backstopped global dollar funding

**Lesson:** The basis warned of dollar stress before it showed up in other indicators. And it showed the Fed's intervention was working when it normalized.

#### 🎯 Practical Application: Monitoring the Basis

- **Track EUR/USD and JPY/USD basis:** These are the most liquid and watched

- **Watch for sharp moves:** Basis moving 20-30bps in a day signals funding stress emerging

- **Context for USD strength:** If USD is rallying AND basis is blowing out, it's a funding squeeze — more concerning than rate-driven USD strength

- **Fed swap lines matter:** When the Fed activates or expands swap lines, expect basis to normalize

- **Quarter-end effects:** Basis typically widens at quarter-end due to bank balance sheet management — don't overreact to predictable seasonal moves

#### Section Summary

- **Cross-currency basis measures deviation from Covered Interest Parity**

- **Negative basis = premium to obtain dollar funding** — dollar scarcity signal

- **Basis blowout signals funding stress** — watch for rapid widening

- **March 2020 was a classic example** — basis warned of dollar squeeze, normalized after Fed action

- **Fed swap lines are the release valve** — they provide dollar liquidity to foreign CBs

- **Pattern:** Verbal warnings → rate checks → actual intervention

- **Scale:** Tens of billions of dollars spent buying yen

- **Effect:** Sharp short-term reversals (USD/JPY dropped several yen in hours)

- **Limitation:** Could not sustainably reverse the trend while rate differentials remained wide

- **Resolution:** USD/JPY only sustainably reversed when US rate expectations shifted

**Lesson:** Intervention can slow the move and hurt speculators, but can't fight fundamentals forever. Japan needed US rate expectations to change before JPY could sustainably recover.

#### 🎯 Practical Application: Trading Around Intervention

- **Know the levels:** Markets identify "lines in the sand" where CBs have intervened before

- **Watch the warnings:** Verbal intervention escalation often precedes actual intervention

- **Respect the firepower:** Don't fight intervention in the short term — they can move markets

- **But don't overestimate:** Intervention buys time; it doesn't change fundamentals

- **Use for entry:** Intervention-driven moves can provide entry points if fundamentals still favor the original direction

#### Section Summary

- **CBs intervene when currency moves are "disorderly" or harmful**

- **Intervention can work short-term** — especially against one-sided positioning

- **Intervention struggles against fundamentals long-term**

- **BoJ, SNB, PBoC are most active interveners** — Fed rarely intervenes

- **Japan 2022-2024:** Intervention slowed but couldn't reverse USD/JPY until rate differentials shifted

## FX for the Non-FX Trader

Even if you never trade FX directly, currency signals can improve your equity, bond, and macro trading. This section ties everything together: how to use FX as a **confirmation tool, risk gauge, and early warning system**.

#### Why Equity Traders Should Watch FX

**FX is Level 3 in the hierarchy** (after CB policy and bonds). It often moves before equities fully price information.

**FX reveals risk sentiment:** JPY strength, EM weakness, basis blowouts all signal risk-off before VIX might spike.

**FX affects earnings:** Dollar strength hurts US multinationals' foreign earnings; helps importers.

### FX Signals for Different Purposes

| Signal | What to Watch | Implication |
| **Risk-off warning** | JPY strengthening sharply, especially vs AUD | Carry unwinding; risk assets likely to follow |
| **Dollar funding stress** | Cross-currency basis widening rapidly | Global liquidity tightening; credit stress possible |
| **EM contagion risk** | Broad EM FX weakness, especially "fragile" currencies | Risk-off spreading; may hit DM eventually |
| **China sentiment** | AUD weakness, CNY fixing surprises | China growth concerns; commodities and EM exposed |
| **Rate expectations** | Rate differential moves vs FX moves | If FX isn't following rates, something else is driving |
| **Dollar regime** | DXY direction and Dollar Smile position | Strong USD = headwind for US multinational earnings, EM, commodities |

Daily FX Scan Pre-Trade Check Divergence Alerts
#### 🎯 Daily FX Scan (5 Minutes)

Check these each morning before trading:

- **DXY direction:** Is the dollar strengthening or weakening? Why?

- **USD/JPY:** JPY strengthening = risk-off signal

- **EUR/USD:** Proxy for dollar strength vs developed markets

- **AUD/USD:** Risk sentiment and China proxy

- **EM FX:** Any broad weakness or specific stress?

- **Cross-currency basis:** Any blowout? (if you have access)

#### 🎯 Pre-Trade FX Confirmation

Before taking equity risk positions:

- **Long risk?** Confirm JPY is stable/weak, AUD stable/strong, EM FX not breaking

- **Short risk?** Confirm JPY strengthening, AUD weak, EM FX under pressure

- **US equities?** Consider dollar direction — strong USD = multinational earnings headwind

- **EM/international?** Strong USD = headwind; weak USD = tailwind

- **Commodities?** Dollar strength typically negative for USD-denominated commodities

#### 🎯 Divergence Warning Signals

When FX and equities disagree, investigate:

- **Equities up, JPY strengthening:** FX warning of risk-off not yet in equities

- **Equities up, EM FX breaking:** Risk may be rotating out of weakest links first

- **Equities down, AUD strong:** Equity selloff may be technical, not fundamental

- **Rate differentials widening, FX not moving:** Intervention or positioning effect — watch for catch-up

- **VIX low, basis widening:** Funding stress not showing in equity vol — yet

**General rule:** When FX and equities diverge, FX is often right — or there's a specific reason for the divergence worth understanding.

#### Section Summary

- **FX signals improve equity trading** — confirmation, early warning, risk gauge

- **JPY is the key risk-off indicator** — sharp strengthening = carry unwind, risk-off

- **AUD is China/risk-on proxy** — weakness signals growth concerns

- **Cross-currency basis reveals funding stress** — invisible to most traders

- **Divergences between FX and equities are warnings** — investigate, don't ignore
