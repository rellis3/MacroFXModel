The weekly macro terminal
#### Soon you will be reading data that almost nobody in this market has *seen discussed anywhere*.

Not leaked. Not privileged. Published continuously, in the open, by central banks and exchanges, and almost never assembled, because assembling it is slow, technical, unglamorous work that nobody does on your behalf.

You have felt its absence. A position goes against you on a morning when nothing on your screen explains why. Weeks later you learn that a funding spread had been widening for a month, that liquidity had drained for three straight weeks, and that the move which ran you over had been forming in plain sight the entire time, on a page you were never shown.

This is that page.

It assembles the conditions your market is actually priced in, week after week. The money, the credit, the plumbing, the curve, the flows. Every series scored against its own history, so the question stops being how far something moved and becomes whether that move was unusual at all. Every number carrying its source on the same row, so nothing asks for your trust.

What changes is not just how much you know and understand, but the structure of how you read a market. You stop reaching for a story and start reading conditions: what the data agrees on, where it diverges, and how far the current state sits from its own history. And when those conditions have occurred before, you have the base rates for what followed, expressed as a distribution rather than a claim.

And it is pointed at your market rather than at a general audience: the series that price the things you actually trade, read fresh each week against everything moving around them.

None of it is a conclusion handed to you. It is material. The series, the scores and the distributions are inputs you can argue with, filter, and build your own systems and ideas on top of.

A view you cannot audit is a story. Everything here arrives with **its receipt**.
Research and information only. Not advice, and not a forecast. Every figure shown in the screens that follow is generated in this page to demonstrate the method.

#### The screen you watch is *one tile*

A price chart shows you the outcome and almost nothing about what produced it. The conditions that set the level are published continuously, in the open, and almost never brought into one place. Assembled, they change what the same chart means.

The market you trade is priced by data you have **never been shown**.
The grid continues past the frame, and coverage widens with every release.
Act three **of four**
#### Every series, *scored against itself*

A two percent move means nothing on its own. Every series is scored against its own history first, so the question stops being how far it moved and becomes how unusual it was. Scored that way, a week becomes comparable to every week before it, which is what makes it possible to ask what has historically followed the same environment and to express the answer as a distribution of outcomes rather than a view.

A move is only news once you know **what ordinary looks like** for that series.
Volatility adjusted, measured against the full available history of the series.
Act four **of four**
#### When did it *sit like this* before

Every past week is a point in the space of those conditions. The present week is placed among them, the nearest are found, and crowded neighbours are removed so the answer cannot be last month wearing a disguise.

A range and a median of what followed, drawn from the weeks that **most resembled this one**.
Historical distribution of what followed the nearest weeks. Past behaviour is not a guide to future behaviour.
