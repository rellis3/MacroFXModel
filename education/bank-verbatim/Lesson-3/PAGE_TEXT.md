# Why Public Strategies Don't Deliver Excess Return or Long-Term Profitability

Understanding why strategies circulated on social media, YouTube, and mass-market courses are structurally incapable of producing sustainable returns — and what actually generates alpha.
Section 01
The Most Common Trap
One of the most common traps traders fall into is believing that the strategies circulating publicly — on YouTube, social media, and mass-market courses — are viable paths to consistent profitability. On the surface, they are presented with confidence: simple rules, clean charts, and backtests that appear convincing.

In reality, **they cannot produce sustainable excess return** due to the nature of how financial markets actually operate. This is not a matter of finding the right public strategy — it is a structural impossibility.

Any strategy that is openly available to millions of participants **cannot sustain an edge**. By definition, once a trade idea is public, the market adjusts. Opportunities vanish as price action incorporates the signal earlier and earlier until the edge no longer exists.

Section 02
How Public Edge Decays
Even in the rare case where a publicly shared strategy once contained a genuine statistical edge, the process of making it public triggers a predictable and irreversible decay. The timeline varies, but the outcome is always the same.

STAGE 01
#### Edge Exists

A genuine inefficiency is identified by a small number of participants

STAGE 02
#### Strategy Published

The method is shared publicly — courses, videos, social media

STAGE 03
#### Adoption Spreads

Thousands of traders act on the same signal simultaneously

STAGE 04
#### Market Adapts

Price incorporates the signal earlier — the inefficiency shrinks

STAGE 05
#### Edge Eliminated

The strategy becomes negative EV after transaction costs

This is not theoretical — it is an axiom within institutional finance. **Alpha is capacity-dependent.** A given edge can support only a limited amount of capital before it decays. Exclusivity preserves performance; public diffusion destroys it. This is why allocator due diligence focuses not just on past returns, but on whether a process is proprietary, capacity-aware, and forward-looking.

Section 03
Why Most Never Contained an Edge
The decay process described above assumes the strategy once had an edge. The more uncomfortable reality is that **the vast majority of publicly circulated strategies never contained a genuine edge to begin with**. They are products of the following mechanisms:

Mechanism 01
#### Curve Fitting

Parameters are optimised against historical data until the backtest looks profitable. The resulting equity curve is a product of overfitting, not genuine predictive power. Without rigorous out-of-sample validation and walk-forward testing, these results are statistically meaningless.

Mechanism 02
#### Selective Presentation

Winning trades are highlighted, losing periods are omitted. The strategy is presented as consistently profitable when the full record tells a different story. Survivorship bias in strategy selection compounds this — you only see the ones that looked good enough to market.

Mechanism 03
#### No Economic Basis

The strategy is not derived from any genuine market driver — no macro thesis, no flow logic, no structural inefficiency. It is a pattern match against noise. Without an underlying economic rationale that can be quantitatively validated, there is no reason to expect the pattern to persist.

Mechanism 04
#### Hindsight Construction

The model is built retrospectively — designed so that in hindsight it appears accurate, then packaged for public release. As soon as the consumer applies the framework in live conditions, all apparent accuracy evaporates because it was never genuinely present.

#### The Hindsight Trap

Public models are structured so that they can appear accurate retrospectively in order to be marketed effectively. The attractive equity curves are not evidence of edge — they are marketing material.

The moment the consumer applies the framework in live market conditions, all accuracy is lost. It was never present — it was **engineered after the fact** to appear that way.

Section 04
How Institutions Actually Generate Alpha
Hedge funds and asset managers operate on a fundamentally different framework. Their alpha does not come from publicly available patterns — it comes from **exploiting structural inefficiencies** that require proprietary data, infrastructure, and quantitative rigour to access.

Risk-Transfer Premia
Earning returns by absorbing risks that other market participants are willing to pay to offload — insurance-like strategies that are **structurally compensated** over time and can be quantitatively modelled.

Liquidity Provision
Providing liquidity to markets when others need it most — earning the spread between urgency and patience. Requires **deep market microstructure knowledge** and real-time flow data that retail participants cannot access.

Execution Asymmetry
Superior execution infrastructure — lower latency, tighter spreads, prime brokerage relationships, and co-location. The cost of trading is materially lower, meaning **strategies that are negative EV for retail can be positive EV for institutions**.

Information Differentials
Access to proprietary data — positioning reports, flow analytics, alternative datasets, satellite imagery, credit card spending data. **Quantitative models built on data that the public does not have** provide edges that cannot be replicated from a standard charting platform.

Regulatory Arbitrage
Exploiting inefficiencies created by regulation — constraints on what certain participants can hold, forced selling, index rebalancing mechanics. These are **structural, repeatable, and quantifiable** — and invisible to those who do not understand the regulatory landscape.

The common thread across all institutional alpha sources is the same: they are **proprietary, capacity-constrained, and quantitatively validated**. None of them can be replicated by following a publicly available strategy. Once an edge enters the public domain, it is competed away.

Section 05
Position in the Information Chain
Following publicly circulated strategies places you at the very back of the information and execution chain. By the time a strategy reaches a YouTube video or social media post, every participant with a genuine edge has already positioned, profited, and moved on.

Back of the Chain
- ✗ Reacts to the market rather than anticipates it

- ✗ Trades after the edge has already been exhausted

- ✗ Accepts models never designed to survive outside cherry-picked windows

- ✗ No quantitative framework to assess whether an edge still exists

- ✗ Operates on recycled ideas that have already lost their informational value

Front of the Chain
- ✓ Identifies genuine market drivers before they are priced in

- ✓ Builds proprietary frameworks from real macro and flow data

- ✓ Validates every thesis quantitatively before committing capital

- ✓ Monitors edge decay and adjusts capacity accordingly

- ✓ Anticipates events and positions ahead of institutional flow

This is why most retail traders lose money. They operate at the very back of the information and execution chain, relying on ideas that have **already lost their edge** — if one ever existed in the first place.

Section 06
Moving Toward a True Edge
Rather than chasing strategies that were never real or are already arbitraged away, the path forward requires building an entirely different kind of understanding — one rooted in the actual mechanics of how markets price, move, and allocate capital.

#### Genuine Market Drivers

Monetary policy, liquidity flows, volatility regimes, and cross-asset dynamics that institutions actually use to anticipate price. These are the inputs that produce alpha — not the patterns that result from them.

#### Proprietary Frameworks

Instead of copying public concepts, constructing analytical models derived from true market drivers and validated against real data. A process that is forward-looking, capacity-aware, and not dependent on public signals.

#### Quantitative Validation

Every thesis tested against positioning data, macro indicators, flow metrics, and historical regimes before capital is at risk. If the data does not support the view, the view does not get traded — regardless of how compelling the narrative appears.

By understanding how institutions operate, you move from being steps behind to being **positioned in alignment with the drivers that actually move markets**. That shift — from public consumer to informed participant — is the single most impactful change a trader can make.

Key Takeaway
Public strategies cannot deliver sustainable returns because the market eliminates publicly known edges by design. The path to consistent performance runs through proprietary frameworks, genuine market drivers, and rigorous quantitative validation — not through the next publicly available system.
