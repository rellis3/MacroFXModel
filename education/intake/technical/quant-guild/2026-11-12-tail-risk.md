## Lesson
A static normal fitted to SPY daily returns says a 5σ day comes once in about 1.74 million trading days (about 7,000
years); there were 19 in 25 years. Conditioning on a volatility regime (GARCH(1,1) terciles) makes the observed tails
plausible: about 12.5 days' wait in the high-vol state. But even a good conditional model fails at the next crisis,
because the next one is not like the last: a variance-premium fund sized in 2002 on the dot-com tail is blown up by
2008. So "the goal is never prediction, it's positioning for survival": hold insurance (convexity) so an unseen shock
cannot end you, like a casino refusing a bet it cannot afford to lose.

## Approach
- **Question**: how likely is an extreme day, conditional on state, and can the book survive one bigger than any seen?
- **Evidence**: SPY 1999-2025 daily; 5σ count vs normal waiting time; GARCH regime terciles; a 2002-calibrated VRP fund
  walked into 2008; GBM simulations of a hedged vs unhedged 2.5-beta book.
- **Kill rule**: a model under which the observed data are near-impossible is wrong ("full-scale failure of static parametric modeling").
- **Habit**: size on a survival question (what does a 20/40/60/80% shock do?) as well as on the estimated distribution.
- **Failure modes**: walk-forward "can overfit"; "you can overfit in a walk-forward validation process if you tune your hyperparameters to death"; model and parameter risk are permanent.

## What the code actually does
- The 5σ threshold and the normal use the **full-sample** mean and σ. The regime figure fits GARCH on the full sample
  and cuts terciles of the full-sample conditional vol: in-sample labels.
- The "walk-forward" VRP fund fits GARCH on **1999-2009 including 2008**; only the tercile cuts and VaR/ES use data to
  2001. Its P&L is invented: premium 5×σ²_GARCH minus 4× realised variance, sized so ES99 = 35% of equity, up to 120x
  notional. The 2008 blow-up is real in direction but the numbers are assumptions.
- The hedge simulation is GBM (no jumps, in a tail-risk lecture) with a zero-cost collar (10% floor, 28% cap,
  **carry 0%**), reset every 63 days.
- Video 133 ("crisis alpha"): his own 2025 IBKR account TWR vs SPY, one year, one crisis, strategy not disclosed,
  leverage paths "financing ignored". An n = 1 track record, not a test.

## What we could learn
- **ALREADY DO**: conditional tails over static ones (desk σ engines; forecast-miss studies).
- **DON'T DO: a beyond-sample survival stress.** Sizing and risk notes use the worst seen (stop-risk p99, the
  Portfolio Book's max DD 29%). Add one line per book: the loss under a shock k× the worst observed, and under named
  gaps the instrument has had (CHF 2015-01-15 for USDCHF/EURCHF, 2020-03 for indices, 2008 for the book), and whether
  the account survives it. Cheap and descriptive.
- **ALREADY DO / his gap**: his "walk-forward" fits the model through the test crisis. Data-rules forbids this
  (`lookahead-and-oos.md`, training years only for design choices).
