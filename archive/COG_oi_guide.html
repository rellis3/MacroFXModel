📚 Open Interest Basics

Open Interest (OI)
The total number of outstanding options contracts that have not been settled, closed, or expired. Each contract has a buyer and seller, so OI represents the total "open" positions in the market.

OI vs Volume

Metric What It Measures Resets?
Volume Number of contracts traded today Yes, daily
Open Interest Number of contracts still open No, cumulative
Example

Day 1: Trader A buys 10 calls, Trader B sells 10 calls

Volume = 10, OI = 10

Day 2: Trader A sells 5 calls to Trader C (who is opening new)

Volume = 5, OI = 10 (no change — position transferred)

Day 3: Trader A sells remaining 5 calls to Trader B (who closes)

Volume = 5, OI = 5 (decreased — position closed)

How OI Changes

Action Buyer Seller OI Effect
New Position Opens new Opens new +1
Close Position Closes existing Closes existing -1
Transfer Opens new Closes existing 0

Why OI Matters

High OI at a Strike
Large positions exist there. Price may be "attracted" to this level (magnetism) or find support/resistance.
Rapidly Increasing OI
New money entering. Traders are building positions — strong conviction about direction.
Declining OI
Positions being closed. Traders taking profits or cutting losses — reduced conviction.
OI Concentration
Clustered OI at certain strikes creates "walls" that can act as barriers to price movement.

🧱 OI Walls

OI Wall
A strike price with unusually high open interest. These represent large concentrations of options positions and often act as support (put walls) or resistance (call walls).

Call Walls vs Put Walls

Wall Type What It Is Acts As Why?
Call Wall Strike with high call OI Resistance Call sellers (often dealers) hedge by selling underlying as price rises toward this level
Put Wall Strike with high put OI Support Put sellers hedge by buying underlying as price falls toward this level

How to Interpret Walls

Price Approaching Call Wall
Potential Resistance

As price rises toward a major call wall, expect selling pressure. Call sellers (often market makers) need to hedge by selling the underlying. This creates a "ceiling" effect.

What to watch: If price breaks through with volume, the wall may flip to support as dealers cover shorts.

Price Approaching Put Wall
Potential Support

As price falls toward a major put wall, expect buying pressure. Put sellers need to hedge by buying the underlying. This creates a "floor" effect.

What to watch: If price breaks through, panic selling may accelerate as put holders profit.

⚠️ Walls Can Break

OI walls are not guaranteed support/resistance. In high-momentum moves (news, earnings, macro events), price can slice through walls. Use walls as probabilities, not certainties.

💀 Max Pain

Max Pain
The strike price at which the total value of all outstanding options (calls + puts) would be worth the least at expiration. In other words, the price where option holders collectively lose the most money.

The Theory

The "Max Pain Theory" suggests that price tends to gravitate toward the max pain strike as expiration approaches. The reasoning:

  • Options sellers (often large institutions/market makers) have an interest in price settling where most options expire worthless
  • These entities have the capital to influence price through hedging activities
  • As expiration nears, the "gravitational pull" toward max pain increases

How It's Calculated

Calculation Method

For each strike price, calculate the total "pain" (intrinsic value) of all options if price expired there:

Pain at Strike X = Σ(Call OI × max(0, X - Call Strike)) + Σ(Put OI × max(0, Put Strike - X))

The strike with the minimum total pain is the max pain strike.

When Max Pain Works Best

✅ High Reliability
  • Weekly/monthly expirations
  • Low volatility environments
  • Last 2-3 days before expiry
  • High OI relative to volume
❌ Low Reliability
  • During major news events
  • High volatility / trending markets
  • Far from expiration (>1 week)
  • Low liquidity contracts
💡 Pro Tip

Don't trade max pain blindly. Use it as a magnet zone — if price is above max pain, bias is slightly bearish; if below, bias is slightly bullish. Combine with other analysis.

⚖️ OI Skew

OI Skew Ratio
The ratio of open interest above the current price vs below it. Measures whether options positioning is heavier on the upside (bullish) or downside (bearish).

How It's Calculated

Formula
OI Skew = (Total OI Above Current Price) / (Total OI Below Current Price)

Skew > 1.0: More OI above price = More call activity = Bullish positioning
Skew < 1.0: More OI below price = More put activity = Bearish positioning
Skew = 1.0: Balanced positioning

Interpreting Skew

Skew Value Interpretation Market Sentiment
> 1.5 Heavy call positioning above price Strongly Bullish
1.1 - 1.5 Moderate call bias Mildly Bullish
0.9 - 1.1 Balanced positioning Neutral
0.7 - 0.9 Moderate put bias Mildly Bearish
< 0.7 Heavy put positioning below price Strongly Bearish
⚠️ Contrarian Signal?

Extreme skew readings can sometimes be contrarian signals. When everyone is positioned one way, the market often reverses. A skew of 2.0+ (everyone bullish) might indicate a top; skew of 0.5 (everyone bearish) might indicate a bottom.

🧲 Strike Magnetism

Strike Magnetism Score
A composite score ranking how likely price is to be "pulled" toward a specific strike. Based on OI concentration and proximity to current price.

Why Strikes Act as Magnets

Options market makers must hedge their positions. When they sell options, they delta-hedge by buying/selling the underlying:

  • Sold Calls: As price rises, dealers sell underlying to hedge → creates selling pressure
  • Sold Puts: As price falls, dealers buy underlying to hedge → creates buying pressure
  • High OI = More Hedging = Stronger Magnet

Magnetism Score Calculation

Formula
Magnetism Score = (Total OI at Strike) × (1 / Distance from Current Price)

Strikes with high OI that are close to current price have the highest magnetism. Far OTM strikes with low OI have minimal pull.

How to Use Magnetism

High Magnetism Strike Above Price
Bullish Magnet

If a strike above current price has a high magnetism score, price may be pulled upward toward it. This is especially true heading into expiration.

High Magnetism Strike Below Price
Bearish Magnet

If the strongest magnet is below current price, expect downward drift. Price often "pins" to the highest magnetism strike at expiration.

Price Between Two Strong Magnets
Range Bound

When strong magnets exist both above and below, price often chops in a range. Trade the range until one side breaks.

Dashboard Shows

  • Top Magnet Strike — The strike with highest magnetism score
  • Magnetism Rankings — All strikes ranked by pull strength
  • Direction Bias — Whether magnets favor upside or downside

🚀 OI Momentum (Change Analysis)

OI Change / Momentum
The daily change in open interest at each strike. Positive change = new positions being opened. Negative change = positions being closed.

Why Changes Matter More Than Absolute OI

Static OI tells you where positions exist. OI change tells you where money is flowing right now. This is leading indicator information.

OI Change Price Action Interpretation
+OI Price Rising New longs entering — bullish confirmation
+OI Price Falling New shorts entering — bearish confirmation
-OI Price Rising Short covering rally — weak/unsustainable
-OI Price Falling Long liquidation — capitulation, possible bottom

Momentum Signals

Call OI Building
Large increase in call OI at specific strikes = traders betting on upside. Especially significant at strikes above current price.
Put OI Building
Large increase in put OI = traders betting on downside or hedging. Significant at strikes below current price.
OI Unwinding
Decreasing OI across multiple strikes = traders closing positions. Often happens after big moves or before expiration.
OI Shifting
OI decreasing at one strike while increasing at another = traders rolling positions. Watch where the new positions are going.

Dashboard Shows

  • Biggest Call OI Increases — Where bullish bets are being placed
  • Biggest Put OI Increases — Where bearish bets are being placed
  • Net Change — Overall building vs unwinding
  • Change Heatmap — Visual of where new positions are concentrating
💡 Pro Tip

Large OI increases at a specific strike often mark significant levels. If 10,000 new contracts open at 1.1700, that strike becomes important. Watch for price to react when it reaches that level.

🎯 Trading Strategies Using OI Data

Strategy 1: Wall-to-Wall Range Trading Low Risk

Setup

  • Identify the nearest major call wall (resistance) and put wall (support)
  • Confirm price is between the two walls

Execution

  • Buy near the put wall with stop below it
  • Sell near the call wall with stop above it
  • Target the opposite wall

Best When

  • Low volatility environment
  • No major news expected
  • OI walls are clearly defined with high contrast
Strategy 2: Max Pain Reversion Medium Risk

Setup

  • Calculate max pain for nearest expiration
  • Wait for price to deviate significantly from max pain (>1%)

Execution

  • If price is above max pain: Look for short entries
  • If price is below max pain: Look for long entries
  • Target: Max pain strike
  • Timeframe: Enter 2-3 days before expiration

Best When

  • Within 3 days of expiration
  • High OI in the contract
  • No major catalysts expected
Strategy 3: Magnetism Pinning Expiration Play

Setup

  • On expiration day (or day before), identify the highest magnetism strike
  • Confirm it has significantly more OI than surrounding strikes

Execution

  • Trade toward the magnet strike if price is within 0.5%
  • Sell premium at the magnet strike (expect pinning)
  • Exit by end of day

Best When

  • Expiration Friday (weekly/monthly)
  • Low VIX environment
  • Clear dominant magnet strike
Strategy 4: Momentum Confirmation Trend Following

Setup

  • Identify a directional move (price trending up or down)
  • Check OI change data to confirm new positions

Execution

  • Bullish: Price rising + Call OI increasing = Add to longs
  • Bearish: Price falling + Put OI increasing = Add to shorts
  • Caution: Price moving on declining OI = Move may be weak

Best When

  • Clear trend is established
  • Looking for confirmation to add size
  • Avoiding false breakouts
Strategy 5: Wall Break Momentum High Risk / High Reward

Setup

  • Identify a major OI wall that price is testing
  • Wait for a decisive break through the wall

Execution

  • Enter on confirmed break (close beyond wall)
  • Stop loss: Just inside the broken wall
  • Target: Next major wall or 2x risk

Why It Works

When a wall breaks, dealers who were hedging suddenly need to reverse their hedges. This creates a cascade effect that accelerates the move.

⚠️ Risk Management

OI data is one input among many. Never trade OI signals alone. Combine with:

  • Technical analysis (price action, support/resistance)
  • Fundamental awareness (news, earnings, macro)
  • Volume confirmation
  • Proper position sizing and stop losses
