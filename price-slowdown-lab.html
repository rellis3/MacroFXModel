Price Slowdown Lab · the two volatility budgets

Every trading day spends two different, non-interchangeable volatility budgets. Telling them apart is how you read whether price is exhausting (about to fade) or trending (about to run). Built on the shared bricks — computeBands, volSigmaSeries, touchFeatures — so it can't disagree with the forecaster.

1 · The two budgets

A day can spend a lot of one and none of the other. That gap is the fade.

RANGE budget — the path travelled

(runHigh − runLow) / open

Total distance covered. Every move spends it — out AND back. This is what the Feller HL bands (1.572σ / 2.049σ) model. A day that pokes out and comes home has spent almost all of it.

DISPLACEMENT budget — distance from the open

(price − open) / open

How far price is from the open, right now. The reach-back gives it back. This is what the close bands (0.67σ / 1.15σ) model. "Fade to open" = this draining to zero.

When the two curves move together → trend day (path ≈ displacement). When range keeps climbing but displacement returns toward zero → the reach-out-and-back, i.e. the fade.

3 · So how do you know it's slowing? — the evidence

Fade rate by approach velocity into the tag — the speed of the last leg into the exhaustion line, in σ units. Two targets: the tradeable retrace to the OC-median line vs the full fade all the way back to open.

Approach into HL50n→ OC-median (tradeable)→ open (full fade)

Same gradient holds across three pairs (10y each) — a spike into the line roughly 1.5× the tradeable-fade rate of a grind, and 2–3× the full-fade-to-open rate. Consistency across pairs is what separates signal from one lucky slice.

Range-budget-consumed at the tag (how much of the projected range was already spent) was weak and inconsistent for full fade-back — no clean gradient across the three pairs. The kinematics of the approach carry the signal, not the static budget count. This matches the platform's earlier finding that the pre-day day-type score is dead (AUC≈0.50) while the at-the-moment approach velocity is significant OOS (p<0.001).

4 · What this means for the vol strategy

  • Target the inner band, not the open. Conditional on tagging the median exhaustion line, only ~ of days come all the way back to the open — but ~ retrace to the OC-median line. Your fade TP should be the next band in (which is exactly what the per-line engine does), not "back to open".
  • The trigger is velocity, not a range quota. "Price is slowing down" reads best as a fast thrust into an exhaustion line that then decelerates — a spike lifts the tradeable-fade rate from ~30% to ~50%. A slow grind into the line is the one you don't fade.
  • Two budgets, two bands — use both. HL lines (range) locate where exhaustion can happen; OC lines (displacement) are where the fade goes. The signed gap between them, live, is your fade-vs-trend read.
  • Honest status. This is a descriptive diagnostic over 10y × 3 pairs — no costs, no IS/OOS split. It says the relationship is real and stable; it is not a costed backtest. The costed OOS fade edge already lives in the per-line book.
