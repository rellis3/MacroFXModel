Multi-timeframe overlay
Read the delta, not the percentage. Two timeframes of the same oscillator on the same price are mechanically correlated — the slow wave is close to a smoothed version of the fast one — so a high agreement % is mostly arithmetic. The baseline is the same statistic over re-phasings of the slow series, which keeps each series' own persistence but destroys their real time correspondence.
Why not direction? Comparing instantaneous slopes across two very differently-lagged smoothings reads as systematic disagreement when the dominant price cycle is short relative to the slow wave's lag — measured well below its own baseline on trending data. level and zone behave the way you'd expect; zone is the strongest but is undefined unless both waves sit inside an OB/OS band.
This is descriptive. Alignment being measurable is not evidence it predicts anything.
MTF stack — do all timeframes point the same way?
One labelled row per timeframe, green = up / red = down, fastest at the top. The SCR row is the alignment score: the mean direction sign, −1 (all down) to +1 (all up). Watch a flip cascade down the rows — the fast timeframe turns first.
Why a rolling VWAP and not the session VWAP? A cumulative/session VWAP is near timeframe-invariant — measured max 115 ppm difference between the M1- and M15-computed curve, and its slope sign flipped only twice in a whole session — so every timeframe gives the same answer (~99% agreement) as arithmetic. A rolling window scales with the timeframe (20 M1 bars = 20min, 20 M15 bars = 5h), so the curves genuinely differ.
It is a score, not a confidence. Unanimity is a description; nothing here has been tested for predictive value. Read the delta against the baseline, as above.

What the fan is. Percentiles of where WT1 actually went the last N times the wave looked like this — p10–p90 and p25–p75 bands with a dashed median, anchored to the current WT1 so it continues the line. It projects the OSCILLATOR, not price: WT is bounded ±100 and self-normalising, so the fan sits at the same scale as the line it extends. A price cone tilted by the same information would be about 1 pip on a 50-pip envelope — invisible. Bands, not a forecast — they describe past continuations from this state. M5 only, because that is the granularity it was measured on.

Money Flow caveats. On FX volume is OANDA's tick count, not size traded — so this is activity-weighted candle direction, not money changing hands. Its amplitude is also rescaled for display: the brick divides by the single largest bar, and real M15 has ~18× volume outliers that squash the wave flat, so the drawn height is set from a robust percentile and clamped. Shape and sign are the indicator's; height is not information.
