Start Here

The Theory Lab teaches the ideas behind trading FX, rates, volatility and macro: the maths, the statistics and the market mechanics, each explained from scratch. It has more than 160 lessons. Nobody needs all of them, and none of them assumes you've seen the notation before. This page shows you how a lesson works and where to begin.

How a lesson works

1

Intuition first, then the maths

Each full lesson opens with a TL;DR, explains the idea in plain English, and only then writes it down, defining every symbol. If a page gets dense, the Explain Like I'm 5 button adds a plain-language pass alongside it. If a Greek letter stops you, the Notation Glossary says what it means. Most lessons also come as a ⚡ visual guide: a short slide deck of the same idea, good on a phone. Switch with the Reading mode buttons on the hub, and start there if you only have ten minutes.

2

A real trade, a chart you can move

Every lesson works a numeric example, then walks the same numbers through a concrete trading decision. Most charts have sliders: move them and watch the maths respond. That's usually where the idea clicks.

3

Check yourself

Lessons end with key takeaways and a self-test. Try the questions before opening the answers; recalling an idea is what makes it stick. Once you have finished a lesson, its questions come back for review a week later, on this page and the hub.

4

Four ways into the same idea

Every lesson opens with one picture of the whole idea and a short spoken version, a trader and a quant talking it through, good for a walk. The key formula builds one term at a time, and a ▶ whiteboard draws the mechanism. Before you touch a calculator you lock in a guess, and near the end a dated scenario drill asks you to make the call before you see what happened. "Where this fits" says what to read first and next.

Once you are learning: make it stick

Reading is the weakest way to learn something. These pages turn what you read into what you can do.

  • Practice: one real, dated market situation a day. Commit to a call and say how sure you are. It charts whether your confidence can be trusted.
  • Spot the error: real mistakes this lab once printed. Check the working, not the conclusion.
  • Pre-trade checklist and trade journal: the questions to answer before a position, then a log of which lessons your trades relied on and whether they paid.
  • Cheat sheets to print for each path, and an Anki flashcard export on every lesson and every path.
  • On every lesson: type your answer before you open a self-test question, grade yourself, and use the ☀/☾ button if you prefer a light page.
Your progress. A lesson counts as done once you've read 90% of it, in either format. Progress is kept in this browser; to carry it to a phone or another computer, use Sync across devices. There's no account and nothing to sign up for.
What this is and isn't. These lessons explain how ideas work and where they came from. They are education, not trading advice or a recommendation to buy or sell anything. Markets punish ideas that are applied without testing, and several lessons are about exactly that.
