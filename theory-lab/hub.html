Theory Lab

A from-scratch, math-explained curriculum in the ideas behind quantitative FX/macro research — not a course transcript, but a teaching series written to sit alongside the existing course notes in this repo. Every lesson explains a concept the way you'd want it explained if you'd never seen the notation before: plain-English intuition first, then the math step by step with every symbol defined, then a worked numeric example, then how the idea connects to what's actually built in MacroFXModel.

How to read the status badge on each lesson. CONCEPT means the idea is explained here but not built or tested anywhere in this repo — treat it as background knowledge, not a claim. ALREADY IN USE means this repo has a real, running brick that this lesson is the math behind (e.g. the Hurst feature in rangeBiasCore.js, or the GARCH estimator in volBacktestEngine.js). Nothing on this page is a trading signal, and nothing here has cleared this repo's out-of-sample bar just by being explained well — see CLAUDE.md's Lego Principle #5 and the "how we talk about results" discipline. A method is not a strategy until it's tested.

Primer 3

Correlation & Linear Regression

1 · Foundations & Epistemics

Start here. How to think about evidence, priors, and the null hypothesis before touching any market-specific math.
Lesson 1

The Efficient Market Hypothesis

The null hypothesis every trading idea has to beat — weak/semi-strong/strong forms, the random walk model, and why EMH can never be tested in isolation.
Lesson 2

Bayesian Inference & Updating

Prior, likelihood, posterior — derived from scratch, plus the base-rate fallacy that makes an "80% accurate" classifier much less useful than it sounds.
Lesson 3

Multiple Testing & the Look-Elsewhere Effect

Why testing 70 pair/regime slices at once makes finding a few "winners" almost guaranteed by chance alone — Bonferroni, FDR, and the math behind this repo's own house rule.

2 · Time Series & Regimes

The math of how a single price series behaves over time — mean reversion, volatility clustering, and inferring an unobserved regime.
Lesson 4

Ornstein-Uhlenbeck & Mean Reversion

The stochastic differential equation behind "reversion," and how to convert a fitted AR(1) coefficient into an actual half-life in days.
Lesson 5

Hurst Exponent & Variance Ratio Test

Rescaled-range analysis and the variance-ratio test — the math behind a feature already computed live in js/rangeBiasCore.js.
Lesson 6

GARCH & Volatility Clustering

Why big moves cluster, ARCH → GARCH(1,1), and the math behind one of the three sigma estimators js/volBacktestEngine.js already runs.
Lesson 7

Hidden Markov Models & Regime Detection

Transition matrices, emission distributions, and inferring a latent regime — a statistically principled alternative to a hand-set threshold rule.
Lesson 8

Kalman Filter & Adaptive Relationships

Predict/update recursion and the Kalman gain — letting a relationship (like a hedge ratio) drift smoothly instead of assuming it's fixed.

3 · Cross-Asset & Structure

Moving from one series to many — when a relationship between two markets is real, and how to summarize a whole basket's shared risk.
Lesson 9

Cointegration & Statistical Arbitrage

Why two random walks can show a spuriously high correlation, and the Engle-Granger test for telling a real equilibrium relationship from noise.
Lesson 10

Principal Component Analysis & Factor Regimes

Eigenvectors and eigenvalues explained from scratch — how much of a basket's move is one shared risk factor, extending js/diversificationCore.js.

4 · Options & Derivatives

The pricing theory underneath open interest, gamma walls, and why FX gaps around scheduled data releases.
Lesson 11

Black-Scholes-Merton Option Pricing

The no-arbitrage replication argument, the closed-form formula, and the Greeks — the foundation underneath the existing Open Interest course notes.
Lesson 12

Merton Jump-Diffusion Model

Adding sudden gaps to Black-Scholes' smooth diffusion — why NFP/CPI risk produces fat tails, and the theory behind this repo's skew/kurtosis metrics.

5 · Risk & Sizing

Once an edge exists — sizing it correctly, and honestly estimating how bad the tail can get.
Lesson 13

Kelly Criterion & Position Sizing

The growth-optimal bet size, derived from expected log-wealth — and why over-betting a real edge can still ruin you.
Lesson 14

Extreme Value Theory & Tail Risk

Why 500 daily returns can't tell you a 1-in-2000-day loss — the Generalized Pareto Distribution, and the theory one level up from this repo's histVaR/histCVaR.

Where this sits next to what's already here

This folder is deliberately separate from education/, which holds raw lecture notes from an external course (Colez Trades) — read those for the applied trading frameworks and case studies. Theory Lab is the from-scratch math underneath: read a course-notes lesson, then come here when a formula or term needs unpacking. Once a concept from either place turns into an actual hypothesis worth testing, it belongs in code that follows the repo's own discipline — one shared core, a real out-of-sample split, and a registered entry in LEGO_MODULES.md if it becomes a reusable brick.
