Stories

Every idea in this lab was learned the hard way by somebody. This page starts from the events instead of the equations: a crash, a squeeze, a bank run, a model that broke. Each one links straight to the lessons that tell it, and each lesson tells it from its own angle: the same March 2020 is a lesson in forced selling, in volatility clustering, in correlation, and in what the Fed does when it decides to.

19 October 1987Crashes & squeezes

Black Monday and portfolio insurance

The US market fell more than 20% in one session. Portfolio insurers were running Black-Scholes replication in size: as prices fell, the model told them to sell more futures, so they did, into a market with no buyers. Afterwards, index options never again priced crash risk as if it were impossible: the volatility skew was born.

1987–1996The human side

What real investors actually did

Brokerage records from tens of thousands of households turned behavioural theory into measurement. Investors sold winners far more readily than losers, and the winners they sold went on to beat the losers they kept. The households that traded most earned the least. August–September 1998Models that broke

Long-Term Capital Management

A fund run by some of the best-credentialed people in finance made small, carefully measured bets that related prices would converge, levered roughly 25 times.
