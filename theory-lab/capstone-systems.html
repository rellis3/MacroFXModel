One Honestly Tested Strategy

One strategy idea taken from a written hypothesis to a verdict, documented so someone else could rebuild it and would believe the result, including when the result is null.

CAPSTONE WORKBENCH~35 minYour notes stay in this browser
How to use it. Each step has a box for your words and, where there is arithmetic, a calculator with worked defaults you can overwrite. Do the steps in order: the pass bar in step 1 is written before you see any result, and steps 4 and 5 use the same trial count. A null result written up honestly is a finished capstone.
Step 1 of 8

Write the hypothesis, the base rate and the pass bar first

Before you load any data: one sentence on what you expect and why, the base rate it has to beat, and the exact result that would count as a pass. A pass bar written after the test is not a pass bar.

Learn it in: The Research Pipeline: Hypothesis to Honest Backtest · Bayesian Inference & Updating
Step 2 of 8

Build the dataset point-in-time

Every value must be what you could have known on that date: no later revisions, no instruments chosen because they survived. List the sources, the as-of rule and what you did about dead instruments.

Learn it in: Point-in-Time Data & Survivorship Bias
Step 3 of 8

Check every feature for leakage

For each feature, write when it becomes known and when you use it. Any feature that is computed with data from the same bar it predicts is a leak. Record the code version and the data snapshot so someone else can rebuild the exact result.

Learn it in: Feature Engineering, Leakage & Reproducibility
Step 4 of 8

Test it walk-forward, and count what you tried

Fit on the past, test on the next unseen block, roll forward, and leave a gap between the two so overlapping labels cannot leak. Then count every variant you ran: each parameter, filter or instrument choice is a trial. Pure luck alone produces a best Sharpe this high.

Learn it in: Walk-Forward & Purged Cross-Validation · Multiple Testing & the Look-Elsewhere Effect
Step 5 of 8

Report the deflated Sharpe ratio, not the raw one

The deflated Sharpe ratio asks how likely the true Sharpe is above zero once you allow for the number of trials and for fat tails. The defaults reproduce the lesson's worked example.

Learn it in: Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar & the Deflated Sharpe Ratio
Step 6 of 8

Subtract realistic costs

Spread, commission and slippage come off every round trip. An edge that is a few basis points per trade can vanish entirely.

Learn it in: Execution Algorithms: TWAP, VWAP, POV, IS · Market Impact Models & Almgren-Chriss
Step 7 of 8

Size it, and face the drawdowns you will have to sit through

Size from the edge and the volatility, then look at the pain that size brings: how likely a deep fall is, and how long a losing streak to expect.

Learn it in: The Kelly Criterion & Position Sizing · Drawdowns, Risk of Ruin, and Why Good Systems Get Abandoned
Step 8 of 8

Log the decision before you know how it turns out

Write the decision, what you expect, and what result would make you stop. Read it again in three months, judging the decision, not the outcome.

Learn it in: Judge the Decision, Not the Result
