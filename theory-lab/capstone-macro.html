How to use it. Every number shows the date it is from; rates, FX and equities close on different calendars and some official series publish a day or more late. "Change" means over the last five observations of that series, roughly a week. Write your sentence, then read the verdict beside it: most of the famous macro "leads" came back null on this desk, so a good read says what is happening now and stops there.

Step 2 of 7

The 2-year, the 10-year and the curve

Desk test: nothing foundAn inverted curve is not a trade. Fifty years of 10s2s collapse to ten inversion episodes. Recession within 24 months followed 6 of 10 (95% interval 31% to 83%, which contains a coin flip), and steepening episodes did worse for the Nasdaq at 12 months than inversions did.
Desk test: nothing foundA 2-year shock does not mean a wild FX week. After a ±14bp week in the 2-year (about 211 shocks per pair), the next week's FX volatility was not higher; after a 2-year rise, EUR/USD and GBP/USD ran calmer.
Learn it in: What the Yield Curve Says About Growth
Step 3 of 7

Split the 10-year into real yield and breakeven

Desk test: nothing foundA big 10-year day does not reliably widen FX. Over 260 de-clustered top-decile days per pair since 2010, only EUR/USD cleared (+0.101 ATR next session); USD/JPY and GBP/USD did not, and no half-sample held.
Desk test: nothing foundBreakevens do not "catch up" to oil. A ±10% 20-day oil move was followed by no excess breakeven change at 5, 10 or 20 sessions. If oil moved and breakevens did not, that is the read, not a pending trade.

Trace the one domino chain that is moving today

Pick the one link from steps 1 to 3 that moved most, and write the chain it runs through, link by link: what moved, through what mechanism, to what. Stop at the link where today's numbers stop confirming it.

Desk data: base rateStocks and bonds falling together. A negative 20-day stock/yield correlation happened in 71 episodes since 2008, a median of 5 sessions long, 17% of days. Only 36 clean flips: too few to test what follows. Describe it if you see it; do not forecast from it.
