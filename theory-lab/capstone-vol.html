A Weekly Volatility Read

For the S&P 500, one page on what the options market is pricing for the coming week: a range, never a direction. Today's numbers are filled in. You write the sentences, and next to each one sits the desk's tested verdict on the claim behind it.

How to use it. Each step has the numbers, the arithmetic done for you, a box for your sentence, and the desk's verdict on the claim you are about to make. Verdicts update from the desk's ledger when a study is re-run. Write the sentence first, then check it against the verdict: if the ledger says null, your sentence should not lean on it.
Step 1 of 6

Turn the VIX into an expected one-week move

Not tested hereThe band is arithmetic, not a forecast. VIX × √(5/252) is the one-week standard deviation the options imply, and "two weeks in three inside it" assumes normal returns. The desk has not scored how often the S&P actually finishes inside this band, so write it as what is priced, not what will happen.

Step 2 of 6

State the term structure: contango or inverted

Desk test: held upAfter an inversion, a wider week. On the first day the VIX closed above VIX3M, the next five sessions ran wider than matched controls: SPX500 +0.76 ATR, NAS100 +0.82 (86 entries since 2008, all intervals clear of zero). Inversions are brief, a median of one session.
Desk test: held up…and a wider month, with no direction. Next 20 sessions' range ratio +0.52 on SPX500 and +0.44 on NQ against a matched control, 14 de-clustered firings, holding in both halves of the sample. Direction is null: 57% up against an 80%+ control.

Step 3 of 6

Compare implied with recent realised volatility

Desk test: held upThe gap closes from below. The folk read is "implied far above realised means over-insured, so calm ahead". The desk found the opposite: with VIX ÷ 20-session realised in its top decile (88 first days, 2010 on), NAS100's next-5 range was +0.38 ATR and SPX500's next-20 +0.67. Realised catches up to implied.

Step 4 of 6

Give the dealer gamma sign, and what it does not tell you

Desk test: held upRange, Nasdaq only, never direction. On 793 days of NQ, the next-day range was +0.315 higher on short-gamma days, matched on trailing vol. The reliable state is long gamma is quiet (about 0.85 of a random walk); short gamma sits near 1.02, essentially normal. It did not generalise to FX and carries no direction.
Desk test: nothing foundNo pin to max pain. Price pinning to the options max-pain strike into expiry tested null, with timing, DTE, gamma and sign conventions all checked.

Step 6 of 6

Write the range expectation

Pull steps 1 to 5 into three or four sentences: the priced range, whether the term structure, the implied/realised gap and gamma argue for wider or narrower than that, and where the tails make the band too narrow. After each sentence, put its verdict in brackets: (validated), (null) or (untested).

Desk test: held upSize, never sign. Every vol signal on this page that survived testing predicts how far, not which way. The same study that found the inverted curve widens the month found direction null. If your read says "down", it has left the evidence behind.
