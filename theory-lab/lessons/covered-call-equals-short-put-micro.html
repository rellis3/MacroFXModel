The Covered Call Is a Short Put

One is sold as the conservative income strategy, the other as the risky one. At expiry they are the same position, to the cent. Once you see that, call open interest stops looking like a crowd of bulls.

Section 1

Two trades, two reputations

Covered call

Own the stock at $100. Sell someone the right to buy it from you at $105. Pocket the premium.

Ends with: the stock if it's below $105, otherwise $105 cash. That is min(ST, 105).

Cash-secured short put

Hold cash that grows to $105. Sell someone the right to sell you the stock at $105. Pocket the premium.

Ends with: $105 cash if the stock's above $105, otherwise the stock. Also min(ST, 105).

Same ending in every possible world. Two things that pay the same in every world must cost the same today, or someone collects a free lunch.
Section 2

Put-call parity says so

European options, same strike K, same expiry T, no dividends.

C − P
=
S − Ke−rT

Rearrange so each side is a strategy someone actually runs:

S − C
covered call
=
Ke−rT − P
cash + short put
At expiry
Covered call: ST − max(ST − K, 0) = min(ST, K)
Short put + cash: K − max(K − ST, 0) = min(ST, K)
Exact at expiry. Dividends, financing, taxes and early assignment on American options separate them a little in practice: second-order, not a different risk.

Section 4

Worked example, priced with Black-Scholes

S = 100, K = 105, r = 4%, σ = 20%, T = 3 months. Call ≈ $2.39, put ≈ $6.35.

Covered call outlay

100 − 2.39 = $97.61

Cash + short put outlay

105·e−0.01 − 6.35 ≈ 103.96 − 6.35 = $97.61

Three endings, both strategies
Stock at $130: both hold $105 → +$7.39. Stock alone: +$30. You sold $22.61 of rally.
Stock at $100: both hold $100 → +$2.39, the premium.
Stock at $80: both hold $80 → −$17.61. Stock alone: −$20. The cushion was $2.39.
Section 5

What you give up, what you keep

Give up

Every dollar above the strike. Profit caps at K − S + C however far the stock runs.

Keep

The full downside, all the way to zero, less the premium. The premium is a cushion its own size and nothing more.

"Conservative" is only true relative to owning the stock outright, and only by one premium. And if a short put frightens you, so should the covered call: same position.
Section 6

Open interest never records who opened the contract

Every contract has a buyer and a seller. The count is the same whichever started it.

Put open interest
  • Bought put: bearish bet or hedge
  • Sold put: bullish (and a covered call by another name)
Call open interest
  • Bought call: bullish bet
  • Sold call against stock: capped upside, neutral to mildly bullish
The trap

Covered-call overwriting is one of the biggest systematic option flows. It piles into call open interest from sellers who gave up the upside, pushing the put/call ratio down: exactly the signature a naive reading calls "bullish". Without trade side, open interest is a count, not a vote.

Section 7

It actually happened: 2022–23

It actually happened

By 2022–23 covered-call ETFs were among the most popular US income products, holding tens of billions of dollars between them. A widely held Nasdaq-100 covered-call ETF owns the index and sells at-the-money index calls every month.

2022: index down about a third

Covered-call funds fell too, by less: the monthly premium cushioned each down-month. Cushioned, not protected.

2023: index up more than 50%

Covered-call funds captured only part of it and trailed by a wide margin. An ATM call each month hands over each up-month's gain above the strike.

The same year, desks and the financial press widely argued that all that call selling left dealers long gamma and helped keep index volatility subdued. Argued, not demonstrated, and a claim about range, not direction.

Section 10

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

Long stock + a short call at K has the same expiry payoff as…

Both pay min(ST, K). Parity: S − C = Ke−rT − P.
Question 2

S = 100, K = 105, call premium $2.39. Maximum profit on the covered call?

K − S + C = 105 − 100 + 2.39 = 7.39, reached anywhere at or above $105.
Question 3

An income fund sells index calls every month. Its effect on open interest is best read as…

Open interest counts contracts, not who initiated them. The overwriter is neutral to mildly bullish with the upside sold.
Question 4

What did the desk find about dealer gamma?

Long gamma is quieter than a vol-matched baseline on NQ; it does not carry to FX, and wall-magnet direction scored 48.8% vs a 49.9% placebo.
Section 11

What to carry forward

01

Long stock + short call ≡ cash + short put: S − C = Ke−rT − P, both paying min(ST, K).

02

You sell the upside above K and keep the whole downside, less one premium.

03

Open interest never records who opened the contract. A sold put is bullish; a sold call against stock is capped.

04

Gamma's range effect is real on the Nasdaq only; its direction is falsified. Covered calls themselves are untested here.

05

Max-pain pinning came back null, a weak null with no sample size recorded.

What this now lets you do

Recognise a covered call and a short put as one position whatever the label, size its real downside, and refuse to read call-heavy or put-heavy open interest as a direction.
