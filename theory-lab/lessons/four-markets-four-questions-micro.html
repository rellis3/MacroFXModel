How Markets Talk to Each Other · 5 of 5

Four Markets, Four Questions

Stocks, Treasuries, options and currencies aren't four votes on "is today good or bad?" Each one answers a different question. Ask each its own question, read the answers side by side — and pay most attention when they disagree.

Section 1

Each market answers its own question

📈

Equities — mood

How do investors feel about growth and future earnings? Can't tell you why.

🏛️

Treasuries — safety & rate path

How much do people want safety, and where are rates heading? Can't separate growth from inflation — unless you split the yield.

🎲

Options / VIX — uncertainty

How big will the moves be? Never which way.

💱

FX — whose money?

Relative rates and flows: havens (JPY, CHF) vs growth-linked (AUD), and the dollar against everything.

Gold answers several questions at once (rates, dollar, reserves), which is why it's hard to read. Prediction markets quote explicit probabilities, but their macro contracts are thin — a price from a handful of trades isn't a consensus.

|Δreal| ≥ |ΔBEI|

Real-rate driven — growth or the expected policy path is moving the yield.

|ΔBEI| > |Δreal|

Inflation driven — expected inflation is moving the yield.

Example: 10y +15bp, real +3bp → breakeven ≈ +12bp → an inflation story. Only compare legs that printed on the same day.

Section 4

Reading one (made-up) day

S&P −1.8% · 10y +12bp (real +10, BEI +2) · 2y +14bp · VIX 16→21, VIX3M 22 · dollar up broadly, yen flat.

Equities: mood soured. That's all.
Treasuries: yields up on a down day → no flight to safety. Real leg dominates, 2y leads → a hawkish rate-path repricing.
Options: 21/√12 ≈ 6.1% a month — but 21 < 22, still contango; no inversion flag.
FX: dollar up, yen flat → relative rates, not a haven scramble.
One story: a rates shock, not a panic. And stop there — don't forecast the next FX week from it (see Section 8).

Section 6 · It actually happened

Two days the markets disagreed

Real episodes

Stocks crashed and the VIX closed at a record 82.69 on 16 March 2020. But for several days the 10-year yield rose while stocks fell, and the dollar surged.

The bond market's "wrong" answer was the right reading: a scramble for cash and dollars, not ordinary risk-off. The Fed's response targeted exactly that — rate cut, Treasury purchases and wider dollar swap lines from 15 March, made open-ended on 23 March. The S&P's closing low came on 23 March.

Mid-August 2019: the 10-year dipped below the 2-year for the first time since 2007, and stocks fell sharply that day.

Bonds answered "where are rates heading?" — lower — and a recession did follow (NBER peak February 2020, caused by a pandemic). Stocks answered "what are earnings worth?" — and the Nasdaq rose 48.5% over the following year. Each was right about its own question.
Section 7

Tested: does one market lead another?

Desk test: nothing found curve-inversion: TEN episodes since 1976. Recession within 24 months 6 of 10 — 60%, CI [31%, 83%]. Forward Nasdaq +12m +10.5% vs +13.0% control (p=0.33). Steepenings did worse (−1.1%, n=6).
Desk test: nothing found rates-pivot-lead: 2,010 de-clustered 2-year pivots: Nasdaq at 1h −0.005% vs control [−0.021, +0.012]. Up-move after a rates low 54.1% — base rate 54.1%.
Desk test: nothing found front-end-shock: ~211 2Y shocks per pair. After a 2Y UP shock EUR/USD and GBP/USD ran calmer (−0.34 and −0.28 ATR).
Section 8

Tested: what survived is range, inside a market

Desk test: held up vix-inversion: first day VIX > VIX3M → next-5-session range: SPX500 +0.76 ATR, NAS100 +0.82, USD/JPY +0.44, XAU/USD +0.43 (86 entries since 2008, all CIs clear of zero).
Desk test: held up breadth-all-down: all six indices down (n=1,002 since 2008) → next-5 range SPX500 +0.31 ATR [+0.17, +0.45], NAS100 +0.26 [+0.15, +0.38]. Direction 58–61% up — the ordinary drift.
Desk data: base rate stock-bond-flip: stocks and bonds falling together — 71 episodes since 2008, median 5 sessions; too few clean flips to test what follows.
Section 9

What the desk learned

Validated — range

vix-inversion · mv-vixterm-range · iv-over-rv-wider · breadth-all-down. All read inside one market. None says which way.

Null — leads & direction

curve-inversion · rates-pivot-lead · front-end-shock · yields-to-fx-direction · fear-gold. All one market forecasting another.

Markets talk to each other at the same moment, not in a queue. Read all four together to understand today; don't use one to forecast another.

Section 11

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

Which market answers "how big will the moves be?"

Options price the size of future moves — uncertainty — and say nothing about direction.
Question 2

Stocks −4% and the 10-year yield is UP. Most likely reading?

In ordinary risk-off Treasuries are bought and yields fall. Yields rising in a crash means Treasuries are being sold too — check the split, and remember March 2020.
Question 3

"The 2-year just bottomed — buy Nasdaq now." What did this desk find?

rates-pivot-lead was null at 1h, 2h and 4h. Rates and equities move in the same bar, not in sequence. Never quote a hit rate without its base rate.
Question 4

What do the desk's validated cross-market findings have in common?

vix-inversion and breadth-all-down widen the expected range; the nulls are the claims that one market forecasts another.
Section 12

What to carry forward

01

Equities: mood. Treasuries: safety demand and the rate path. Options: uncertainty (size). FX: whose money people want.

02

Split the yield — nominal ≈ real + breakeven — before calling a move growth or inflation.

03

Disagreement is the signal. Treasuries sold in a crash = a scramble for cash (March 2020).

04

Validated: range, read inside a market (VIX inversion, breadth wipe-out). Never direction.

05

Null: the curve's equity signal, rates leading equities, rate shocks meaning wild FX. Same moment, not a queue.
