Options from the Ground Up · Lesson 1 of 3

The Four Option Positions, and who owes whom

Two yes/no choices — bought or sold, call or put — give four positions. From those two choices alone you can read off who holds a right, who carries an obligation, the worst case, and the sign of every Greek.

Section 1

Four words, four meanings

L

Long

You bought it. You paid the premium and you hold a right.

S

Short

You sold it. You collected the premium and you carry an obligation.

C

Call

The right to buy at the strike.

P

Put

The right to sell at the strike.

A put is bearish only for the person who bought it. The person who sold it is bullish — holding the identical piece of paper.

The reference card

Short put · bullish

Short call · bearish

Obligation to sell. Collects premium. Max gain = premium. Max loss unlimited.
Δ− Γ− Θ+ ν−

Long put · bearish

Right to sell. Pays premium. Max loss = premium. Max gain = strike − premium.
Δ− Γ+ Θ− ν+

Buyers (one diagonal): long gamma, short theta, long vega. Sellers (other diagonal): the mirror. Only delta depends on the row.

Section 4

Why the signs follow from the side

C − P
=
S − Ke⁻ʳᵗ

Put-call parity: a call minus a put at the same strike is a forward — a straight line in S with no σ in it. Differentiate it twice in S, or once in σ, and you get zero.

So

A call and a put at one strike have identical gamma and identical vega. Their deltas differ by exactly 1 and their thetas by a small financing term. Buy either one and you are long gamma and long vega; sell either one and you are short both.

Section 5

Premium = intrinsic + extrinsic

Calls, S = 100, σ = 20%, 3 months.

K = 90 (in the money): 11.09 = 10.00 intrinsic + 1.09 extrinsic
K = 100 (at the money): 4.23 = 0 intrinsic + 4.23 extrinsic
K = 110 (out of the money): 1.04 = 0 intrinsic + 1.04 extrinsic

Intrinsic is what exercising right now would pay. Extrinsic is the price of the chance things improve before expiry. Only extrinsic value decays (that is theta), it is largest at the money, and at expiry it is zero.

Section 6

Delta is N(d₁) — not the chance of finishing in the money

The model's risk-neutral probability of a call expiring in the money is N(d₂), and d₂ = d₁ − σ√τ. Push vol and time up and watch them separate.

Try it
Volatility σσ = 20%
Days to expiry30 days
ATM call: Δ = 0.5229 · N(d₂) = 0.5000 · gap = 0.0229
30 days at 20%: 0.52 vs 0.50 — the shortcut is fine. One year at 60%: 0.63 vs 0.39. And N(d₂) is a pricing-measure probability, not anyone's real-world forecast.
Section 7

What "obligation" means on a bad day

Option sellerSells far from spot, collects a small premium
→
ShockPrice gaps through the strike with no trades in between
→
BrokerMarks the short option up, calls for margin now
→
SellerCan't pay, is closed out at whatever price exists
→
OutcomeLoss many times the premium; shortfall lands on the broker
It actually happened · 15 January 2015

The Swiss National Bank had held a 1.20 floor under EUR/CHF since September 2011. It dropped it without warning and cut its deposit rate to −0.75%. The franc surged within minutes and EUR/CHF traded well below parity at the extreme; stops could not fill. Client losses exceeded balances at leveraged FX brokers: Alpari's UK arm went into insolvency and FXCM took a rescue loan of about $300 million. Anyone short the franc's upside — including sellers of options paying out on it — held the obligation side.

Section 8

What the desk has tested

Not tested here "A put-heavy market is bearish." No ledger entry scores the put/call ratio, and its bullish/bearish thresholds have no recorded provenance. The 2×2 says why to doubt it: open interest cannot tell a bought put from a sold one.
Desk test: nothing found "Price pins to max pain into expiry." Tested 2026-09-10 and null, with publish-lag timing, DTE, gamma, sign convention and spot basis verified — a weak null, with no sample size or interval recorded: "not shown", not "proven false". A level to know about, not a magnet.

"Put open interest is rising" honestly translates to: more insurance changed hands. Who bought, who sold, and why are separate questions.

Section 9

Pitfalls

✗

"Puts are bearish"

Only for the buyer. A short put is long the market.

✗

"Small premium, small risk"

The premium is the seller's best case, not a measure of the worst.

≈

"Short options always earn theta"

Almost always. A deep-ITM European put can have slightly positive theta for its holder.

→

"Same row, same risk"

Short put and short call share three Greek signs but not their worst case. That's the next lesson.

Section 10

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

Which pair are both bullish?

The short put seller keeps the premium if price holds or rises — a bullish position on a put contract. Long call + long put are both buyers; short call + short put are both sellers.
Question 2

K = 100, put premium 3.73. The short put's max loss is…

If the underlying goes to zero the seller must buy at 100 something worth 0, less the 3.73 collected: 96.27. Bounded, because prices stop at zero.
Question 3

A 1-year ATM call at 60% vol has delta 0.63. The model's chance it finishes in the money is…

d₁ = 0.333, d₂ = d₁ − σ√τ = −0.267. N(d₁) = 0.631 is delta; N(d₂) = 0.395 is the risk-neutral ITM probability. The gap grows with σ√τ.
Question 4

Why do a call and a put at one strike have identical gamma?

C − P = S − Ke⁻ʳᵗ. The right side is linear in S, so its second derivative is zero, making Γ_call = Γ_put.
Section 11

What to carry forward

01

Long = bought a right, pays premium. Short = sold an obligation, collects premium.

02

Buyers' worst case = premium. Sellers' best case = premium. The far end: strike − premium for puts, unlimited for calls.

03

Gamma, theta, vega signs come from the side; delta's sign from the row. Parity makes a call and put share gamma and vega exactly.

04

Premium = intrinsic + extrinsic; only extrinsic decays, and it peaks at the money.

05

Delta is N(d₁), the ITM probability is N(d₂): 0.52 vs 0.50 short-dated, 0.63 vs 0.39 at one year and 60%.

06

Put/call ratio as direction: untested here. Max pain as a magnet: tested null, but a weak null (no sample size recorded).
