QE and QT: What the Fed's Balance Sheet Actually Does

"The Fed is printing money" is half right. QE is a swap on two balance sheets — long bonds out of the market, bank reserves in. Learn to draw that swap and every argument about yields, funding markets and the Fed's corridor gets simpler.

The core move

A purchase is a swap, not a gift

The Fed pays for a bond by crediting the seller's bank with reserves it creates on its own books.

Δ Fed securities
=
Δ Reserves
=
X

Assets up by X, liabilities up by X. The private sector holds one fewer bond and more reserves — not more wealth. Reserves are money only banks can hold, used to settle with each other and with the Fed.

Kept out on purpose

The real balance sheet has other liabilities — currency, the Treasury's account, reverse repos — that also shift reserves around. They matter in practice; they are left out here so the core swap stays visible.

Who sold matters

Bank seller or non-bank seller

Try it
Bank: Treasuries −X
⇄
Bank: Reserves +X
One asset swapped for another. The bank's balance sheet does not grow.
Pension: Treasuries −X, Deposits +X
→
Bank: Reserves +X, Deposits +X
The bank grows on both sides; the pension fund swapped a bond for a deposit. Most QE bonds were bought from non-banks, so QE raised deposits as well as reserves.

Why yields move

A long yield = expected policy path + term premium

yₙ
≈
(1/n)·ΣE[r]
+
TPₙ
⚖️

Portfolio balance

Fewer long bonds in private hands → investors bid up what is left → term premium (TP) falls.

📣

Signalling

Buying trillions of bonds makes "low for long" credible → the expected-path piece falls. Announcements move yields before anything is bought.

🚰

Liquidity

A buyer of last resort in a broken market. About stopping fire sales, not the level of yields.

Scale: $100bn of 4% 10-year par bonds (modified duration ≈ 8.18) carries about $82m of risk per basis point. QE moves that risk from private portfolios onto the Fed.

The reverse

QT: runoff with caps, not sales

Fed bond matures
→
Treasury refinances with a private buyer
→
Buyer's deposit and bank's reserves fall

In both programmes (2017–19 and from 2022) the Fed let bonds mature and stopped reinvesting principal up to a monthly cap. No bonds were sold into the market. Slow, scheduled, known in advance — meant to run quietly in the background while the policy rate does the work.

The catch: every dollar of runoff is a dollar less in reserves — and the corridor only holds rates down while reserves are ample.
Ample vs scarce

The line nobody can see

With ample reserves, the overnight rate sits on the floor the Fed pays on reserves (IORB — Lesson 1). Drain enough and, at an unknown point, a little less cash means a much higher repo rate.

It actually happened — September 2019

After QT1, on a mid-month day when corporate tax payments and Treasury coupon settlement both pulled cash out of banks, overnight repo spiked far above the Fed's range — SOFR printed above 5% against a range of 2.00–2.25%. The Fed ran its first overnight repo operations in more than a decade and in October began buying bills to rebuild reserves, insisting it was not QE.

It actually happened

Four episodes, three channels

Pick one

QE1. Up to $100bn of Fannie/Freddie debt and $500bn of agency MBS, aimed at mortgages. Expanded in March 2009 with up to $300bn of longer-term Treasuries; the 10-year fell about half a point that day.

Channel: portfolio balance and signalling, in a market where policy rates had run out of room.

The taper tantrum. In May 2013 Bernanke told Congress purchases could slow within a few meetings. Nothing was sold. The 10-year went from around 1.6% to near 3% by September; the actual taper began in January 2014.

Channel: signalling, in reverse.

Dash for cash. Treasuries stopped trading normally. 15 March: at least $500bn Treasuries + $200bn MBS. 23 March: "in the amounts needed". Balance sheet from a little over $4tn to past $7tn in about three months.

Channel: liquidity, in its purest form.

QT2 begins. 1 June 2022: caps of $30bn Treasuries + $17.5bn MBS a month, doubling after three months. Treasury cap slowed in June 2024 and again in 2025 to approach the ample–scarce line gradually.

Channel: the reverse of portfolio balance — and a slow drain on reserves.

The academic evidence for the yield links is real but debated: event studies (Gagnon, Raskin, Remache & Sack, 2011) found big announcement-day falls; Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2011) found several channels at once; whether the stock held or the flow of buying matters is still contested.

Question 1

The Fed buys $200bn of Treasuries from a pension fund. What happens to bank reserves?

The Fed pays by crediting the seller's bank with reserves; the bank credits the pension fund's deposit. Fed: securities +200, reserves +200. Bank: reserves +200, deposits +200.
Question 2

Why did long yields jump in the 2013 taper tantrum when nothing had been sold?

The yield is expected path plus term premium. A hint that QE would slow told markets rate hikes were closer — the signalling channel running backwards.
Question 3

How did QT shrink reserves in 2022 without the Fed selling bonds?

Runoff: Fed securities −X, reserves −X, private Treasuries +X. Same accounting as a sale, done quietly and on a schedule.
Question 4

SOFR prints 4bp over the floor on the 15th and the SRF is drawn. What does the desk's evidence say?

12 of 15 such episodes fall on the 14th–18th (tax and settlement), and the SRF is drawn on 31% of sessions. The one interval clear of zero points to a calmer tape — reported as measured, not as proof the backstop works.
Recap

What to carry forward

01

QE is a swap: Fed securities and bank reserves rise by the same X. A non-bank seller swaps a bond for a deposit. No one's wealth rises by X.

02

Three channels — portfolio balance (term premium), signalling (expected path), liquidity (market functioning). 2013 was signalling; March 2020 was liquidity.

03

QT is capped runoff, and it drains reserves toward a scarce line nobody can see in advance — September 2019.

04

QE → yields is untested on this desk; the academic evidence is real but debated (stock vs flow).

05

funding-stress is NULL: SOFR over the floor is the calendar, and a drawn SRF was followed by a calmer tape.
