Options & Vol-Surface Extensions 3 of 3

Two Results Hiding Inside One Set of Prices

A full strike-by-strike grid of option prices secretly contains the market's entire probability distribution for the underlying — and, using almost the same trick, a model-free fair price for realized variance. No volatility model required for either. This deck walks through both.

Section 1

One strike gives a probability. All strikes give a distribution.

From N(d₂) to a whole density
Black-Scholes: N(d₂)
=
Risk-neutral P(finish above ONE strike)
Breeden-Litzenberger: ∂²C/∂K² across EVERY strike
=
The entire risk-neutral probability density
Same underlying idea — risk-neutral probability read off option prices — just applied to the full strike grid instead of one strike at a time.
Section 2

A call spread is a bet on "above." A butterfly is a bet on "exactly here."

🦋

Butterfly = long-short-short-long calls

Price = C(K−h) − 2C(K) + C(K+h) — a tent shape centered at K, tapering to zero on both sides.

📐

Divide by h², shrink h to zero

That's the standard finite-difference second derivative. As h→0 it converges exactly to ∂²C/∂K² — a Dirac-delta payoff.

Section 3

The Breeden-Litzenberger formula

1978, model-free
f_Q(K)
=
e^(rT) · ∂²C/∂K²
The curvature of the call-price curve, in strike, IS the risk-neutral density — up to the discount factor. No model assumption anywhere in the derivation, only that the observed call prices are arbitrage-free.

Worked example: S₀=100, r=2%, T=0.5yr. C(95)=$8.9470, C(100)=$6.1207, C(105)=$3.9856 (h=5). Discrete second derivative ≈ 0.027648 → f_Q(100) ≈ e^0.01 × 0.027648 ≈ 0.027926. True lognormal density at K=100: 0.028209 — about 1% off, purely from finite h.

Section 5

Part 2 — pricing variance without forecasting it

The log contract
Payoff: −2·ln(S_T / F)
→
E[payoff] = expected realized variance
g(x) = −2ln(x/F) ⟹ g″(K) = 2/K²
→
Weight every OTM option by 2/K²
You can't trade the log payoff directly — but a strip of OTM puts and calls, weighted by 2/K², replicates it exactly (continuous-path assumptions). That strip's price today IS the fair variance-swap strike. No forecast, only today's prices.
Section 6

The strip: OTM options weighted by 1/K²

Bar height ∝ (ΔK/K²)·e^(rT)·Q(K) — each strike's contribution to the variance sum. Contribution peaks near the reference strike closest to the forward and shrinks moving further out-of-the-money, both because 1/K² shrinks and because deep-OTM prices themselves are small.

Section 8

Why these two results are actually the same idea

The shared mechanism

Any smooth payoff g(S_T) can be spanned by a continuum of options via its own second derivative, weighted across strikes. Part 1 applies this to a butterfly's limiting spike payoff → the density. Part 2 applies it to the log-contract payoff → expected variance. Same math, two targets.

The same object, elsewhere in this category

∂²C/∂K² also sits in the denominator of the sibling Dupire local-vol formula — not a coincidence. It's the arbitrage-free risk-neutral density, the shared ingredient both approaches depend on.

🧬

Genuinely model-free

Both results need only that observed prices are arbitrage-free — no commitment to any specific process for volatility.

⚙️

Bates and Dupire commit to a structure first

Bates: stochastic vol + jumps, calibrated. Dupire: vol as a deterministic function of price and time. Both build a specific machine before pricing anything — this lesson's two results don't.

Section 9

Where this quietly breaks down

🧩

Only finite strikes ever trade

Breeden-Litzenberger needs interpolation across the gaps between listed strikes — the same noise-sensitivity concern the sibling Dupire lesson raises about its own use of ∂²C/∂K².

🚩

Negative density = bad data, not a real result

Real densities are never negative. A negative number from real quotes flags noisy or arbitrageable input prices — fix the data, don't report the number.

✂️

Variance replication needs a truncation cutoff

The theoretical formula integrates strikes from 0 to ∞; real markets stop listing strikes at some point. CBOE's real VIX methodology has explicit published cutoff rules for exactly this.

💥

The log-contract trick assumes no jumps

Genuine price jumps (Bates territory) leave a residual jump-variance term the pure options-strip result doesn't fully capture.

A real, well-known fact

This variance-swap replication logic is the actual theoretical basis for how CBOE computes the VIX index — a discretized version of exactly this formula, run on real S&P 500 option quotes.

Section 10

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

What does Breeden-Litzenberger say the risk-neutral density equals?

f_Q(K) = e^(rT)·∂²C/∂K² — the first derivative gives a probability of finishing above K, not the density itself.
Question 2

Why is a butterfly spread the natural discrete version of this formula?

Divide the butterfly's price by h² and shrink h — that's precisely how a finite-difference second derivative is defined.
Question 3

What payoff does the "log contract" behind variance-swap replication pay?

Its second derivative, 2/K², is exactly the weighting scheme used in the options-strip replication.
Question 4

Why are the two results in this lesson described as "the same underlying idea"?

Part 1 uses a shrinking butterfly's limiting payoff; Part 2 uses the log-contract's payoff — same spanning machinery, different target.
Question 5

A discrete Breeden-Litzenberger calculation on real quotes returns a negative density. What does that mean?

A negative result is a data-quality red flag, not a valid finding — clean the inputs rather than report the number.
Question 6

How do this lesson's two results differ structurally from the sibling Bates and Dupire lessons?

Model-free here means: correct regardless of which model, if any, actually generated the observed arbitrage-free prices.
Section 11

What to carry forward

01

Breeden-Litzenberger: f_Q(K) = e^(rT)·∂²C/∂K². A shrinking butterfly spread's price converges exactly to the risk-neutral density — model-free, needing only arbitrage-free prices.

02

Variance swaps replicate via a strip of OTM options weighted by 2/K² — the log-contract result. Today's whole surface prices expected realized variance; no forecast required.

03

Both are the identical spanning principle applied to two different payoffs — and both share ∂²C/∂K² with the sibling Dupire formula, for a genuinely structural reason.

04

Real markets only ever supply finite strikes — interpolation risk for the density, truncation error for the variance strip. This is exactly what CBOE's real VIX methodology has published cutoff rules to manage.

What this now lets you do

Read an entire strike grid of option prices as more than a pricing table — as the market's own probability distribution, and as a live, model-free fair price for realized variance, both recoverable with nothing more than arithmetic on the quotes already in front of you.
