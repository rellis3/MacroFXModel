From a Volatility Forecast to a Position Size

GARCH says tomorrow's EUR/USD volatility is 0.40%. So what? Here's how that number becomes pips, a stop and a lot size, by two standard routes, and where both go wrong.

Section 1 · How it reached trading

From the Turtles' N to volatility targets

Turtle rules (1983); RiskMetrics (J.P. Morgan, 1994); GARCH (1986) and HAR (2009) as forecasts; volatility-targeted funds

How it reached trading

The Turtle rules defined N, an average of the daily true range. Each unit was sized so a one-N move cost 1% of the account, with the stop at 2N. Quiet market, more contracts; wild market, fewer; same dollars at risk.

RiskMetrics published a daily volatility for risk limits. GARCH and HAR sharpened the forecast. Managed-futures and risk-parity funds run whole books at a target volatility.

A HAR model forecasts variance: 0.16 (%²) → σ = √0.16 = 0.40%. A random walk's expected daily high-to-low is √(8/π)·σ ≈ 1.6σ. If that's far from the ATR, a unit is wrong.
Section 3

Longer trades: add variances, let shocks fade

GARCH pulls the forecast back toward its long-run level at the rate φ = α + β. Over h days, sum each day's variance.

Long-run only: √10 × 0.50%1.58%
A one-day σ for a three-week trade puts the stop inside the noise of the holding period. Match the forecast to how long you'll hold.
Section 4

Two routes to a size

A · per-trade risk

Stop = k·σ (pips). Lots = money at risk ÷ (stop × pip value). Caps the loss if the stop is hit.

B · volatility target

Annual σ = daily × √252. Notional = target vol × equity ÷ annual σ, under a leverage cap. Fixes the position's typical swing.

Use one, not both

A k·σ stop plus a risk budget that also shrinks with volatility gives size ∝ 1/σ². Volatility doubles and the position quarters.

Section 6 · Worked example

HAR forecast → 0.57 lots

HAR variance 0.16 → σ = 0.40% → 0.0040 × 1.0850 = 43.4 pips (1.6σ ≈ 69 ≈ ATR 65 ✓).
A: stop 2σ = 86.8 → 87 pips; $500 ÷ (87 × $10) = 0.575 → 0.57 lots ($495.90).
Volatility doubles to 0.80%: stop 174 pips → 0.28 lots ($487.20). Same budget, half the size.
B: 0.40% × √252 = 6.35%; 5% × $100,000 ÷ 6.35% = $78,743 ÷ $108,500 per lot → 0.72 lots.

A caps the loss at the stop. B sets a typical yearly swing of about $5,000. Stop-and-target trade → A. Held exposure with no natural stop → B.

Section 7

Where Kelly fits

Kelly leverage f* = μ / σ² → position vol f*·σ = μ/σ = Sharpe

Full Kelly is a volatility target equal to your Sharpe ratio. An honest Sharpe of 0.5 means 50% volatility at full Kelly, 25% at half. A 5–10% target is a small fraction, on purpose: Sharpe estimates run high, and past Kelly, risk rises while growth falls. More in the Kelly guide.

Section 8 · It actually happened

5 February 2018: "Volmageddon"

2017 was one of the calmest years on record for US stocks. Inverse-VIX products had grown large, and positions were sized from a long run of quiet trailing volatility.

The day

The VIX more than doubled in one session, its largest one-day percentage rise on record. Credit Suisse's XIV lost almost all its value and was terminated.

The lesson

Trailing-vol sizing is largest when the past is calmest, and learns of the shock after it lands. Cap leverage, floor the forecast, check implied.

What was tested here

Desk test: held upPace persists. A slow 15 minutes is followed by a slow hour, a fast one by a fast hour: monotonic in 68 of 68 cells across 17 pairs. That's why a volatility forecast is worth sizing on. Not a direction.

Section 12

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

HAR outputs 0.25 (%²). What σ goes into the sizer?

√0.25 = 0.50%. Using 0.25% halves the stop and doubles the size.
Question 2

0.80% daily volatility, annualised?

0.80 × √252 = 12.70%. Variance adds over days; volatility grows with the square root.
Question 3

Stop at 2σ, and you also halve the risk budget when volatility doubles. Volatility doubles. Size becomes…

Wider stop halves it; smaller budget halves it again. Size ∝ 1/σ², usually by accident.
Question 4

Honest Sharpe 0.4. A 10% volatility target is what fraction of Kelly?

Full-Kelly volatility = Sharpe = 40%. 10 ÷ 40 = a quarter.
