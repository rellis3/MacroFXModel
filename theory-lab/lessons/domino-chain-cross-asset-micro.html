How Markets Talk to Each Other · 1 of 5

The Domino Effect: One Shock Through the Whole Board

A hot inflation number does not hit one market. It knocks over the 2-year yield, which knocks over the dollar, gold, equities and credit — all priced off the same few shared inputs. This deck walks the chain, shows it biting in real crises, and then shows where the popular version was tested here and failed.

Section 1

Everything is priced with the same ruler

Bonds, currencies, gold and shares are all sums done with the same few numbers: expected policy rates, expected inflation, the real yield, the dollar.

📏

Change a shared input…

…and every price built on it must be redone at once. Not copying — arithmetic.

🔎

So the chain is a diagnostic

If the 2-year jumped, the dollar firmed and gold slipped in the same minute, the shared input was the expected policy rate.

⏱

…and only weakly a forecast

Whether the dominoes fall in sequence — so you could trade the later ones — is a separate claim. Hold that thought for slide 9.

Section 2

The textbook chain for a hot CPI print

ShockCPI prints hot
→
Rates tradersPrice fewer cuts; 2-year jumps
→
FX desksDollar firms, USD/JPY up
→
Gold & equitiesReal yield up: gold down, multiples down
→
OutcomeCredit wider, volatility up

Every arrow is somebody being forced to reprice: a bond holder whose fixed coupon is now worth less, a gold holder whose metal now costs more to carry, a shareholder whose future profits are discounted harder.

Section 3

The front end moves first — and which end moved more is the clue

ΔP/P ≈ −D · Δy

The 2-year is mostly a forecast of the policy rate over two years, so a surprise about this month's prices moves it a lot. The 10-year averages over far longer, so it moves less.

2-year up more

Bear flattening: a short-run inflation / policy shock.

10-year up more

Bear steepening: term premium, government supply, or doubts about inflation credibility.

Real yield up

Money got more expensive. Gold (pays nothing) costs more to hold. Future profits are discounted harder: P/E = 1/(k − g) falls as k rises.

Breakeven up

More inflation expected. That is gold's friend, and not a tightening at all.

Quick number

k = 8%, g = 3% → P/E = 1/0.05 = 20.0. Real yield +5bp → k = 8.05% → P/E = 19.80. About −1% with earnings unchanged. Small changes in k − g move multiples a lot.

Section 6 · It actually happened

UK gilts, Sept–Oct 2022: a link broke, then the chain became a loop

23 SeptUnfunded "mini-budget"
→
Gilt investorsLong yields spike
→
LDI fundsCollateral calls → forced gilt sales → yields up again
→
28 SeptBank of England buys long gilts

The broken link: higher yields should support a currency, but sterling fell to an all-time low against the dollar on 26 September. A yield rise driven by doubt about the government is a risk premium, not an attraction. Pension LDI funds' leveraged hedges then forced them to sell gilts for cash, pushing yields up further — until a buyer with no balance-sheet limit stepped in. The purchases ended on 14 October.

Section 7 · It actually happened

August 2024: the same links, run backwards, under leverage

Late Jul – 2 AugBoJ hikes; weak US jobs report
→
FXYen jumps; USD/JPY falls
→
Carry tradersYen-funded positions forced to cut
→
5 AugNikkei −12%+, VIX briefly above 60

Here US yields fell and equities fell with them — the mirror of the hot-CPI chain. The Nikkei's drop was its worst day since 1987, and it rebounded sharply the next session. Forced de-risking, not a new view on earnings.

Section 8 · The desk's verdict

The mechanism holds. The timing claims failed here.

Null"A 2-year shock means a volatile FX week" (front-end-shock). Null on EUR/USD, USD/JPY and GBP/USD (~211 shocks each). After a 2Y UP shock EUR/USD and GBP/USD ran calmer (−0.34 and −0.28 ATR, CIs clear of zero).
Null"Rates turn first, then equities" (rates-pivot-lead). 2,010 de-clustered events: Nasdaq −0.005% vs control [−0.021, +0.012]. Hit rate 54.1% against a 54.1% base rate. Rates and equities move in the same bar.
Null"Fear buys gold" (fear-gold). 58 VIX spikes: excess +0.07% [−0.42, +0.30] at five sessions, −1.19% [−2.30, −0.48] at twenty.
ContextStocks and bonds falling together (stock-bond-flip). 71 episodes since 2008, median 5 sessions, p75 17. Too few clean flips to test what follows.
Section 9

So what is the chain for?

Use it for
  • Naming which shared input moved
  • Reading which curve leg led
  • Spotting a broken link (yields up, currency down)
  • Knowing what would contradict your reading
Don't use it for
  • Pre-positioning in equities off a rates turn
  • Calling a wild FX week off a 2-year shock
  • Waiting for a "fear bid" in gold
  • Saying oil will reach breakevens "later"
The dominoes fall together, in the same bar, because every market reprices off the same input at once. The chain explains the five minutes; it does not hand you the next five days.
Section 10

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

CPI is hot; the 2-year rises 20bp and the 10-year 6bp. What does the curve say?

The front end is a forecast of the next couple of years of policy. It moving much more than the 10-year means the market repriced the central bank's next moves.
Question 2

The 10-year rises 10bp and the breakeven rises 10bp. What should gold's rates domino do?

Nominal = real + breakeven, so the real yield is unchanged. Gold's carrying cost didn't rise. (And "fear buys gold" tested null here.)
Question 3

In Sept 2022 UK yields soared and sterling fell. Why?

The usual link (higher rate → more attractive currency) assumes the rise reflects expected policy. A rise driven by doubt about the government's finances is a risk premium, and the link runs backwards.
Question 4

"The 2-year just broke higher — short Nasdaq before it catches up." What did the desk find?

rates-pivot-lead: 2,010 de-clustered events, Nasdaq −0.005% vs control. Null at 1h, 2h and 4h, in both directions.
Section 11

What to carry forward

01

Dominoes are real because every asset is priced off the same shared inputs: policy-rate path, inflation expectations, real yields, the dollar.

02

Split before you read: nominal = real + breakeven, and 2-year vs 10-year says whether it is a short-run or long-run story.

03

A broken link is the signal — yields up with the currency down meant "credibility shock" on day one in 2022.

04

Leverage turns a chain into a loop: LDI collateral calls in 2022, yen-funded carry in 2024.

05

Tested null here: rates leading equities, 2-year shocks meaning a wild FX week, fear buying gold. The dominoes fall together, not in a queue.
