Thinking in Expected Value

A hit rate on its own tells you almost nothing. Here is the one number that decides whether a trade is worth taking: what it pays on average, after costs. You'll also see how to get a probability out of an analysis that never gives you one.

Section 1 · How it reached trading

From the card table to the desk

How it reached trading

Edward Thorp's Beat the Dealer (1962) showed that blackjack's expected value changes from hand to hand, and that a player should bet more exactly when it turns positive. He took the same idea to markets: Beat the Market (1967) and then a hedge fund.

Discretionary traders learned it as "expectancy" in R-multiples (profit ÷ initial risk), a vocabulary popularised in the 1990s.

Section 2

40% can beat 70%

E = p·b − (1 − p) − c

Measured in R, where 1R is what you lose at the stop. b = average win ÷ average loss. c = cost per trade.

Trader A · right 70%

Wins pay 0.4R. E = 0.7×0.4 − 0.3 = −0.02R. After a 0.05R cost, −0.07R. Break-even win rate 71.4%.

Trader B · right 40%

Wins pay 2R. E = 0.4×2 − 0.6 = +0.20R. After a 0.05R cost, +0.15R. Break-even win rate 33.3%.

What matters is how far your win rate sits above the break-even rate for your payoff, not how high it is.

Section 4

Moving the target doesn't create an edge

In a market with no edge (a driftless random walk), the chance of hitting target T before stop S is

P(target first) = S / (S + T) = 1 / (1 + b)

That is exactly the break-even win rate. Every stop and target combination has EV = 0 before costs, and −c after. A 1R target hits 50% of the time, a 3R target 25%, and both average zero.

Use it

S/(S+T) is the base rate for any stop-and-target trade. Your analysis has to beat that number. Picking a different target can't do it for you.

Section 5

Your analysis didn't give a probability. Build one.

1

Base rate

No analysis at all: S/(S+T), or the long-run share of up-weeks.

2

Count

Past cases with the same reading, same exits: k wins of n. Use only what was knowable then.

3

Shrink

p̂ = (k + m·p₀) / (n + m). With small n you stay near the base rate.

4

Range → odds

Vol forecast σ and target d: P(close beyond) = 1 − Φ(d/σ); P(touch) ≈ twice that.

Section 7 · Worked example

Long EUR/USD 1.0850, stop 1.0810, target 1.0950

1 · S = 40 pips = 1R, T = 100 pips → b = 2.5; cost 1.5 pips → c = 0.0375R
2 · Base rate S/(S+T) = 40/140 = 28.6%
3 · Back-check: target first in 12 of 30 similar cases (40%)
4 · Shrink, m = 30: (12 + 8.57)/60 = 34.3% (raw SE ±8.9 pts)
5 · E = 0.343×2.5 − 0.657 − 0.0375 = +0.16R ≈ 6.5 pips a trade
6 · Break-even (1.0375)/3.5 = 29.6% → margin 4.6 pts, half an SE
7 · Decision: take it, reduced size, log it

With the raw 40% you'd have believed +0.36R, more than double. Most of the work is in getting the probability right.

Section 9 · It actually happened

February 2018: short volatility

Through the calm of 2017, being short volatility, often through notes that rose when the VIX fell, made a little money most days. The hit rate was high and the curve was smooth.

On 5 February 2018 the VIX more than doubled in one session. Credit Suisse's XIV note lost more than 90% of its value in a day and was terminated.

The EV reading

Many small wins tell you the win rate. The sign of EV depended on the rare loss: how big it was and how often it came, which was the number nobody had measured well.

Section 11 · The desk's verdict

What was tested here

Desk test: held upThe audited 2-year yield-spread FX sleeve, as EV: ~109 out-of-sample trades, 63% win, profit factor 2.2. So b ≈ 2.2×0.37/0.63 ≈ 1.29 and E = 0.37×(2.2−1) ≈ 0.44 average losses per trade. That is about 19 points above its ≈44% break-even. Backtest evidence; not forward-proven.
Desk data: base rateHow far a backtest win rate can fall: one Fibonacci-level system, 85.7% in backtest, 39.6% live. EV can't be better than its p.
Not tested here"A wider target makes the trade better." The maths says that with no edge it doesn't. For a real setup, whether it does has to be measured.
Section 12

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

45% win rate, average win 1.5R, average loss 1R, no cost. EV?

0.45×1.5 − 0.55 = 0.675 − 0.55 = +0.125R. Break-even is 1/2.5 = 40%.
Question 2

No-edge market. You move the target from 1R to 3R, same stop. EV before costs?

The hit rate drops from 50% to 25% (S/(S+T)). 0.5×1 − 0.5 = 0 and 0.25×3 − 0.75 = 0.
Question 3

A setup "worked 9 of 12 times". Base rate 50%, prior weight 12. What p goes into EV?

(9 + 12×0.5)/(12 + 12) = 15/24 = 62.5%. Twelve cases can't pin down 75%.
Question 4

Weekly σ 1.5%, target 3% away, no drift. Chance of closing the week beyond it?

d/σ = 2, and 1 − Φ(2) ≈ 2.3%. Touching it during the week is about twice that, ≈ 4.6%.
Section 13

What to carry forward

01

E = p·b − (1−p) − c, in R. A 40% win rate at 2R beats a 70% win rate at 0.4R.

02

Edge is the gap to break-even, (1+c)/(1+b), not the win rate.

03

With no edge, hit rate = S/(S+T) = break-even. Moving the target changes the hit rate, not EV.

04

Build p: base rate → count → shrink → range to odds.

05

EV needs hundreds of trades to show. Backtest win rates shrink live, as the desk's own records show.
