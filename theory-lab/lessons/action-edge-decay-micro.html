When an Edge Has Stopped Working

Every edge has losing stretches, and some edges really do die. Tell the two apart with rules set in advance — streak odds, a record test, a CUSUM alarm, and an honest count of how many trades it takes.

Section 1

Unlucky, or has the skill gone?

Page (1954) for CUSUM; Wald (1940s) for sequential testing; Philips, Yashchin & Stein (2003) for monitoring managers.

How it reached trading

Allocators have always asked: a bad year — fire the manager or hold? A year is too short, and checking every month quietly raises the false-alarm rate.

The tools came from factory quality control: Page's CUSUM chart, built on Wald's sequential test. Philips, Yashchin and Stein set out a CUSUM for active managers in 2003. Any trading rule with a tested record can be watched the same way.

Section 5

CUSUM: an alarm on every trade

S = max(0, S + score) · alarm at S ≥ h

55% tested vs 50% break-even: a loss scores +0.105, a win −0.095. The floor at zero forgets old good runs, so it reacts to a recent change.

Section 7 · Worked example

180 trades, 88 wins, seven losses in a row

EUR/USD system tested at 55%, payoff 1 : 1
Streak: run of 7+ in 180 trades = 30.6% — not evidence
Record: expected 99 ± 6.67 → z = −1.65 (p ≈ 0.05)
LR = (0.50/0.55)⁸⁸ (0.50/0.45)⁹² = 3.7
Prior 3 : 1 for the edge → chance it's gone ≈ 55%
Power at n = 180: only 38%; 80% needs ~615
CUSUM (h = 3): last 60 trades add +2.11 — near, not over
Decay verdict: AMBER — reviewHalve size, look for a named cause, let the rule decide: red at CUSUM 3 or z −2.33; green again above z −1.28.

Section 9 · It actually happened

Slow decay, sudden shocks

August 2007 · quant quake

Many quant equity funds lost heavily in the same few days while the market barely moved; much of it reversed soon after. Khandani and Lo blamed crowded positions being unwound — and found the stand-in strategy's returns had been falling for years as capital piled in. Slow erosion was real decay; the week itself was a shock.

2016 · publication

McLean and Pontiff studied 97 published return predictors: returns were 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication. Decay has a nameable cause — someone else found the edge — and is usually partial.

Section 10 · The desk's verdict

What was tested here

Not tested here"A losing streak means the edge has decayed." A streak can't settle it, and no desk strategy has run live long enough for a full decay test.
Desk test: held upThe 2-year yield-spread FX sleeve (~109 out-of-sample trades, 63% win, PF 2.2; paper-traded since 2026-07). Seeing a fall to 50% would take ~88 live trades — years, at its pace. Not forward-proven.
Desk data: base rateCheck the backtest first. A Fibonacci-level system: 85.7% backtest win rate vs 39.6% live. A gap like that from day one isn't decay — the backtest saw something live couldn't.
Section 11 · Template

The decay review

1

Before launch

Tested p₀, payoff, break-even, trades a year — and the trades needed to see a fall to break-even.

2

Set the alarm

Pick h for a false-alarm rate you accept. Write down the streak you should expect.

3

Every trade, every month

Update the CUSUM; record z and LR. Green / amber (half size) / red (stop).

4

Find a cause — never refit

Costs, data, code, regime, crowding. A re-launch needs a fresh out-of-sample test.

Section 12

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

Tested at 60%, live 105 wins of 200. Which band?

Expected 120 ± 6.93 → z = −15/6.93 = −2.17. Between −1.28 and −2.33: half size, review.
Question 2

Roughly how many trades to tell 55% from 50% (5% / 80%)?

A five-point fall is small against the noise of single trades. At 100 trades a year that's six years.
Question 3

You drop the CUSUM threshold from 4 to 2. What happens?

False alarms in 300 healthy trades go from ~0.5% to ~21%; median time to catch a real decay falls from ~475 to ~194 trades.
Question 4

Backtest 80% wins; live 42% from the first trade. Most likely?

A gap that size from day one points at look-ahead, survivorship or impossible fills. Replay the live period through the backtest before any decay test.
Section 13

What to carry forward

01

Streaks are not verdicts: at 55%, 7 in a row appears in 180 trades about 31% of the time.

02

Test the whole record against the tested edge (z), and let LR move your odds — no more.

03

Know the trades needed before launch: 55→50 needs ~615, 63→50 ~88.

04

CUSUM watches every trade with a known false-alarm rate. Choose h from the table, not your mood.

05

Amber halves size and looks for a cause; red stops and re-tests. Never refit on the losing stretch.
