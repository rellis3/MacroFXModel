Avellaneda-Stoikov, Where Should a Market Maker Actually Quote?

A market maker earns the spread by quoting both sides — but every fill leaves behind a position, and a position is a risk the market maker didn't necessarily choose. This deck walks through the first widely-used closed-form answer to what a market maker holding inventory should actually do about it.

Section 1

Symmetric quoting has no brake pedal

Post a fixed bid and ask around the mid, forever, and nothing stops inventory from running away.

The problem, in one picture
Fill on the bid
→
Now long +1
Symmetric quoter re-quotes the SAME distance either side
→
No pull back toward flat
Market keeps sending buyers
→
Long +2, +3, +4… no brake
Every fill changes inventory. A quoting rule that ignores current inventory has no mechanism at all to push a runaway position back toward flat.
Section 2

The reservation price is NOT the mid-price

The central formula
r(s,q,t)
=
s − q·γ·σ²·(T−t)
s = mid-price · q = signed inventory (+long / −short) · γ = risk aversion · σ² = variance · (T−t) = time left. When q=0, r=s exactly — a flat market maker has no reason to skew at all.
📉

Long (q>0) → r shifts DOWN

The subtracted term is positive, so r sits below the mid — the ask looks cheap (easy to sell into), the bid looks already-paid-up (harder to buy more).

📈

Short (q<0) → r shifts UP

Exact mirror image: the bid looks relatively attractive (easy to buy back), the ask looks relatively expensive (harder to sell more).

🌊

Same σ as the OU lesson

The model's mid-price follows ds = σ·dW — a driftless Brownian motion, exactly the θ=0 special case of the sibling Ornstein-Uhlenbeck lesson's own SDE.

⚖️

An indifference price, not a forecast

r is the price at which the market maker themselves is indifferent between their current position and flat — not a prediction of where the market is headed.

Section 4

The total spread — two different questions, added together

Inventory-risk term: γσ²(T−t)
  • Pure compensation for the risk of holding ANY position
  • Grows with risk aversion, variance, time remaining
  • Has nothing to do with getting filled at all
Profit term: (2/γ)ln(1+γ/k)
  • Balances "wider = more per fill" against "wider = fewer fills"
  • Driven by k — how fast fill-arrival decays with distance
  • As γ→0, converges to a pure 2/k, no risk aversion involved
A clean structural feature

The total spread's width has NO q in it anywhere — it never changes with inventory. Inventory skew is entirely a re-centering effect (moving where the fixed-width window sits), never a widening-one-side effect.

Section 5

Inventory skew, end to end

Long inventory skews BOTH quotes down
Ask, shifted down
→
Closer to mid — easier to sell into
Bid, shifted down
→
Farther from mid — harder to buy more
Symmetric quoting does neither of these — both quotes stay a fixed distance from s no matter what q is. This mechanism is the model's entire fix for runaway inventory.
Section 6

The horizon T: quotes "want" to end flat

As (T−t)→0, r converges back to the mid — but the spread does NOT converge to zero.

🎯

r → s at the horizon

The inventory shift qγσ²(T−t) shrinks to zero as (T−t)→0, regardless of q — the reservation price collapses back onto the plain mid.

💵

Spread does NOT go to zero

Only the inventory-risk term vanishes. The profit term (2/γ)ln(1+γ/k) has no (T−t) in it — the spread converges to that, not to zero.

🏁

A deliberate finite-horizon choice

The original 2008 model assumes a desk that wants to end the session flat — not a claim that every real desk should "close the book."

♾️

Later variants relax this, honestly

Infinite-horizon and rolling-horizon extensions exist in later literature — this deck won't invent details of a specific one it isn't certain of.

Section 8

One risk, priced honestly — not the only risk

🎭

No adverse selection here

Every arriving order is treated as equally likely to be informed — this model has no μ-style parameter the way Glosten-Milgrom and PIN do.

🔗

Order Flow Imbalance feeds it, doesn't replace it

OFI/the microprice, from the sibling deck, could refine the reference mid s this model just takes as a given exogenous input.

📋

Limit Order Books is the venue this quotes into

Reservation price and spread say WHERE to quote; queue position and priority (sibling deck) govern what happens once that order is resting.

🧩

A real desk needs both risks priced

Inventory risk (this deck) and adverse selection (Glosten-Milgrom/PIN) are genuinely different mechanisms — a real spread compensates for both at once.

This model is excellent at "how much do I already have, and how should that change my price?" It has nothing to say about "is the person trading with me right now secretly better informed than I am?" — that's a different lesson's entire job.
Section 9

Where this quietly stops matching reality

⏱️

Free, instant re-quoting assumed

Real venues have latency, and cancel-replace loses queue position entirely — none of that friction is in this model.

🎲

Constant σ and one fill-shape, assumed known

Real volatility clusters and regimes shift; real fill-arrival may not truly decay exponentially in distance the way λ(δ)=Ae^(−kδ) assumes.

🎭

No adverse-selection pricing at all

Every fill is treated as equally (un)informed — a genuinely toxic-flow environment isn't priced in by this model on its own.

🏁

"Flatten by T" isn't always desirable

A deliberate simplification for a finite-horizon desk — not automatically right for one that runs continuously with no closing time.

Section 10

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

Is the reservation price r the same thing as the mid-price s?

r is the mid shifted toward flat by an amount set by inventory, risk aversion, variance, and time remaining.
Question 2

A market maker is long (q>0). Which way does r shift, and why does that push inventory back toward flat?

r is an indifference price, not a forecast. Being long makes a market maker less eager to add more, hence a lower reservation price for doing so.
Question 3

What are the two components of the total optimal spread?

The first is pure compensation for holding any position; the second balances earning more per fill against getting filled less often as quotes widen.
Question 4

As (T−t)→0, what happens to r and to the spread?

Only the inventory-risk term (which carries (T−t)) vanishes — the profit term has no (T−t) in it and survives unchanged.
Question 5

Does this model price in adverse selection (informed-trader risk)?

This is an inventory-only model. A real desk's spread has to compensate for both inventory risk and adverse selection — this model only addresses the first.
Question 6

Does the total spread's WIDTH change as inventory q changes?

Inventory skew in this model is entirely a re-centering effect. The fixed-width bid-ask window just slides up or down with q.
Section 11

What to carry forward

01

Symmetric quoting has no mechanism to stop inventory from running away. The reservation price r(s,q,t) = s − qγσ²(T−t) is the fix: quotes center on r, not on the mid s.

02

Long inventory shifts r below the mid (skewing both quotes toward selling); short inventory shifts it above (skewing toward buying) — the entire inventory-skew mechanism.

03

The total spread splits into an inventory-risk term and a market-making profit term — and its WIDTH never depends on q, only where it's centered does.

04

As (T−t)→0, r converges to s but the spread converges to the profit term, not zero — a deliberate finite-horizon "flatten by close" modeling choice, not a universal prescription.

05

This model prices inventory risk only. It has no adverse-selection mechanism — that's Glosten-Milgrom and PIN's job — and it assumes free continuous re-quoting and a known, fixed fill-intensity shape that must be calibrated in practice.

What this now lets you do

Read a market maker's current inventory, risk aversion, and a volatility/depth estimate, and compute exactly where their bid and ask should sit relative to the mid — and explain precisely which real risk (adverse selection) this stylized model leaves for other lessons to cover.
