Market Depth & Order-Book Slope, What the Book Looks Like Before Anyone Trades It

Every earlier microstructure signal in this Theory Lab looks at trades that already happened, or at changes to the book over time. This deck asks a plainer question: forget what just happened — what does the resting book look like right now? Depth, slope, and depth imbalance answer that from a single snapshot, no trade history required.

Section 1

A snapshot statistic, not a flow statistic

How this deck's signals differ from everything else this Theory Lab has covered so far.

📸

Depth & slope (this deck)

Read directly off ONE book snapshot — how much size rests where, right now. Zero trade history needed.

🎞️

Order Flow Imbalance (sibling deck)

Built from CHANGES between consecutive book updates, summed over a window — inherently a time-series quantity.

📈

Kyle's λ (sibling deck)

A reduced-form number estimated statistically from a HISTORY of trades and price changes — not observable from a single snapshot at all.

🔗

Same book, three lenses

All three describe "how costly is size" from the same underlying market — just from genuinely different data and genuinely different math.

A photograph vs. a flip-book vs. a statistical estimate. All three are legitimate; none of them substitutes for the other two.
Section 2

Depth: level-1 vs. cumulative

The touch alone is rarely the whole story once an order is bigger than what's sitting right there.

Two ways to measure "how much is here"
Level-1 depth
=
Q(1) — size at the touch only
Cumulative depth D(n)
=
Q(1) + Q(2) + … + Q(n)
D(n) answers what level-1 depth alone can't: how much size would actually need to be consumed to walk the book out to n ticks from the touch?
Section 3

Order-book slope — two conventions, both real

Depth-vs-distance (this deck's convention)
  • slope = average per-tick growth of D(n) beyond the touch
  • LARGER slope = depth builds up fast = deep, liquid book
  • Næs & Skjeltorp (2006) construction
Walk-the-book (colloquial)
  • Plot price reached vs. quantity consumed — the inverse view
  • STEEP price curve = thin, dangerous book
  • The numerical opposite direction of the first convention
Same book, opposite word

"Steep" means a deep book under one convention and a thin book under the other. Neither usage is wrong — always check which axis a "slope" figure is actually computed against before deciding whether a large number is good news or bad news.

Section 4

Drag the depth, watch the book's shape change

Two levels per side. Slope here reduces to one number: how much extra depth level 2 adds beyond level 1.

Try it
Bid: D(1)=300, D(2)=550, slope=250 · Ask: D(1)=200, D(2)=350, slope=150 · DI(1)=0.200, DI(2)=0.222
Section 5

Depth imbalance is exactly QI — reused, not reinvented

Same formula, this deck's own notation
DI(1)
=
(Q_b(1) − Q_a(1)) / (Q_b(1) + Q_a(1))
=
QI
Because DI(1) = QI exactly, the OFI deck's own microprice identity plugs straight in: P_micro = M + (S/2)·QI. Nothing new is derived here — DI(1) is fed into a formula that already existed.

What's genuinely new: extending the same ratio to cumulative depth, DI(n) = (D_b(n) − D_a(n)) / (D_b(n) + D_a(n)) — useful once an order is larger than what's resting at the touch alone.

Section 6

Level-1 vs. cumulative — they can disagree

Same toy book throughout: bid 300/250/400 across 3 levels, ask 200/150/180.

Try it
Bid: 60%
Ask: 40%
DI(1) = (300−200)/(300+200) = 0.200 — mildly bid-heavy at the touch alone.
Bid: 64.2%
Ask: 35.8%
DI(3) = (950−530)/(950+530) = 0.284 — MORE bid-heavy than the level-1 reading, because the bid's extra depth further out outpaces the ask's.

Same book, same instant — only how many levels are included changes the answer. This is a real design choice, not a rounding difference.

Section 8

Where this quietly goes wrong

👻

Displayed ≠ committed

Icebergs hide true size; spoofing fabricates size that was never meant to trade. Every formula here takes displayed size at face value.

🧩

FX has no single book

Spot FX is OTC across fragmented ECNs — one venue's slope isn't "the market's" depth. This repo's own OANDA spot venue has no live top-of-book ladder at all.

⏱️

Snapshots go stale in milliseconds

Depth and imbalance can flip entirely on the very next book update — treat either as an instant read, never a forecast.

🔀

Check the slope convention

"Steep" means opposite things under the two conventions from Section 3 — confirm which axis before judging a number.

Section 9

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

What's the difference between level-1 depth and cumulative depth D(n)?

D(n) answers how much size must be consumed to walk the book to n ticks — a question level-1 depth alone can't answer.
Question 2

Under this deck's stated convention, does a LARGER slope value mean a deeper book or a thinner one?

The walk-the-book convention inverts this — a deep book gives a FLAT price curve there. Both usages exist; check which one a source means.
Question 3

How does DI(1) relate to QI from the Order Flow Imbalance deck?

DI(1) = (Q_b−Q_a)/(Q_b+Q_a) = QI exactly, so the OFI deck's microprice identity plugs in unchanged.
Question 4

Can DI(1) and DI(n) for a larger n disagree on the same book, at the same instant?

A book can be more (or less) lopsided a few ticks back than right at the touch — which n you choose is a real, consequential decision.
Question 5

Why can depth imbalance and Order Flow Imbalance point in opposite directions at the same moment?

A book can be bid-heavy right now while that same size has been shrinking over recent updates — a photograph and a flip-book, both true at once.
Question 6

How does order-book slope differ from Kyle's λ?

Related in spirit — both describe how costly size is — but built from entirely different data using entirely different methods.
Section 10

What to carry forward

01

Depth (level-1 and cumulative D(n)) and order-book slope describe the SHAPE of the resting book from a single snapshot — no trade history needed at all, unlike every other signal in this Theory Lab.

02

Slope's direction is genuinely convention-dependent: this deck's depth-vs-distance convention says larger = deeper; the walk-the-book convention says the opposite — always check which axis before calling a number "steep."

03

Depth imbalance at level 1 is exactly QI from the Order Flow Imbalance deck, feeding the same microprice identity unchanged — extended here to cumulative depth, DI(n), which can disagree with DI(1).

04

Depth imbalance (a resting snapshot) and OFI (a sequence of book changes) are genuinely different signals that can point opposite ways at the same instant — neither substitutes for the other.

05

Displayed depth can hide real size (icebergs) or fabricate it (spoofing), and FX's fragmented, OTC structure means no single book ever describes "the whole market" anyway.

What this now lets you do

Read a raw ladder of resting bid/ask sizes and turn it into cumulative depth, a slope estimate under a clearly-stated convention, and a depth imbalance that plugs directly into the microprice — and know exactly how that reading differs from Order Flow Imbalance and from Kyle's λ.
