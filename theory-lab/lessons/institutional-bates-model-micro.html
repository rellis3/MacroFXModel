Bates: One Model, Two Jobs

Heston's stochastic variance can shape a smile reasonably well — until you get to short-dated options, where real markets show a skew far steeper than smooth diffusion alone can build in time. Bates (1996) staples a Merton-style jump term straight onto Heston's own price process. No new machinery here: two models this Theory Lab already covers in full, combined into one.

Section 3

Independence makes the characteristic function multiply

Why Bates stays tractable
φ_Bates(u)
=
φ_Heston(u)
×
φ_jump(u)
The characteristic function of a SUM of independent random variables is the PRODUCT of their own characteristic functions. Because jumps are assumed independent of the Heston diffusion, Bates' combined characteristic function is available the moment both pieces' are — no new derivation needed.
This is exactly what makes Bates FFT-pricing-compatible: once φ_Bates(u) is known in closed form, the same Carr-Madan/FFT machinery from the sibling FFT Option Pricing lesson prices an entire strike strip in one call — it doesn't care which tractable characteristic function it's fed.

The precise mechanism: flat variance share, vanishing skew and kurtosis

Fixed jump parameters, four maturities
Jump share of variance
→
38.6% at EVERY horizon — flat
Skewness
decays like
1/√T
Excess kurtosis
decays like
1/T — fastest of all
1 week's excess kurtosis (7.744) is 52× the 1-year value (0.149) — exactly 52 weeks per year, exactly the 1/T scaling predicted. Same fixed jump parameters, dramatically different effect on smile shape depending on maturity.

Every compound-Poisson cumulant scales as λT — so variance's numerator and denominator both scale as T (ratio flat), but skewness divides by Var^1.5 (T^1.5) and kurtosis by Var² (T²), leaving T^−1/2 and T^−1 respectively. This is the same Central-Limit-Theorem logic behind any sum of independent shocks washing out toward normality as more of them pile up.

Section 7

The calibration trade-off, stated honestly

What 8 parameters buys you

κ, θ, ξ, ρ, v₀ (Heston's five) plus λ, μ_J, σ_J (Merton's three) — a genuinely richer set of achievable surface shapes, targeting the short-dated wings without distorting the longer end to compensate.

What it costs

A harder, less stable, more overfitting-prone optimization. Multiple, meaningfully different 8-parameter combinations can produce statistically similar-looking fits — a genuine identification problem, not a numerical bug.

This is not new to Bates — it's compounded

The Merton lesson already flags "separating small jumps from high diffusion vol from a few large jumps" as ill-conditioned on its own. Bates layers a second ambiguity on top: is the smile steep because ρ and ξ are large, or because λ and jump size are elevated? A good fit alone can't say.

Section 8

How practitioners actually use this

📐

Calibrate jointly, not slice by slice

Fit all 8 parameters to the whole maturity ladder at once — short maturities help pin down jump params, long maturities help pin down Heston's five.

⚡

Same FFT/Carr-Madan pricing path as Heston

Once φ_Bates(u) exists, the identical FFT machinery prices an entire strike strip per candidate parameter set — no new pricing infrastructure required.

🔧

Regularize, don't optimize blindly

Given the identification issue, add parameter bounds or stability-across-time penalties rather than trusting an unconstrained fit to land somewhere economically sensible.

🧭

A middle ground between Heston and local vol

More flexible than Heston alone at short maturities; still a cleaner, more parametric forward-looking story than a fully empirical local-vol surface fit.

Section 9

Where Bates-style thinking goes wrong

🎭

A good fit ≠ a correct parameter split

Two calibrations that both fit today's surface equally well can disagree meaningfully about how much of tomorrow's risk is vega-hedgeable versus jump risk.

🧨

Jump risk isn't perfectly hedgeable

Continuous delta-hedging needs a continuous path. A genuine discontinuity defeats that logic — jump risk generally can't be replicated by dynamic trading alone, and typically carries its own priced risk premium instead.

🌊

Calibration stability isn't guaranteed

Eight parameters can shift meaningfully between calibration dates — and some of that apparent instability may just be the optimizer landing in a different, similarly-good local fit.

🚫

More knobs ≠ validated edge

A tighter in-sample fit with more degrees of freedom says nothing, by itself, about real out-of-sample trading edge.

Section 10

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

What is Bates' model specification, in one sentence?

Bates re-derives nothing — it staples Merton's jump term onto Heston's price process, leaving Heston's variance SDE exactly as that lesson left it.
Question 2

Why does pure Heston struggle with a steep one-week implied-vol skew?

v_t needs time to wander from v_0, and ρ's leverage effect needs calendar time to accumulate — both mechanisms are gradual by construction.
Question 3

Why does φ_Bates(u) = φ_Heston(u) × φ_jump(u)?

A sum of independent random variables has a characteristic function equal to the product of the parts' own characteristic functions — exactly why Bates stays FFT-tractable.
Question 4

Fixed jump parameters: why does excess kurtosis fall 52× from 1 week to 1 year while the jump variance SHARE stays exactly the same?

Variance's own ratio is T/T = flat. Kurtosis is T/T² = 1/T. 1 year is 52 weeks, so kurtosis at 1 week is ~52× the 1-year value — exact CLT-style scaling.
Question 5

A calibration fits the observed surface well with 8 Bates parameters. Does that prove the fitted λ, μ_J, σ_J are the "true" jump parameters?

Fit quality shows the combined model works — it can't distinguish between competing parameter splits that all fit about equally well.
Question 6

Why is jump risk not "perfectly hedgeable" the way pure diffusion risk is under Black-Scholes-style replication?

A real market-incompleteness subtlety: jump risk generally can't be replicated by dynamic trading in the underlying alone, so it typically carries its own priced risk premium instead.
Section 11

What to carry forward

01

Bates = Heston's two SDEs + Merton's jump term, stapled onto the price process — neither piece re-derived, both reused verbatim.

02

The complementarity is the point: jumps own the short-dated tails, stochastic vol owns the longer-dated shape and clustering.

03

Independence makes φ_Bates = φ_Heston × φ_jump — why the same Carr-Madan/FFT pricing path still works.

04

Jump variance share is flat across horizons; skew decays as 1/√T and excess kurtosis as 1/T — the precise reason jumps matter most at the short end.

05

Eight parameters buy flexibility at the cost of a real, honest identification problem — multiple combinations can fit similarly well.

06

Jump risk isn't perfectly hedgeable by continuous delta-hedging — an incomplete-markets subtlety worth carrying forward honestly.
