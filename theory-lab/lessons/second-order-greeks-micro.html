Second-Order Greeks, One Derivative Deeper

Gamma already asks how Delta responds to price. Six more named Greeks ask the same kind of question for every other pair of inputs a Black-Scholes price depends on — and two of them, Vanna and Charm, generalize the exact dealer-hedging mechanism from the gamma lesson into a vol-driven flow and a pure-time-driven flow. The other four are covered honestly, including how rarely they come up.

Section 1

One derivative deeper than Gamma

Gamma is already Δ's sensitivity to price — a second derivative. These six ask the same kind of question about every other pair of Black-Scholes inputs.

Delta's own σ-derivative and Vega's own S-derivative land on the same Vanna box — that isn't a drawing coincidence, it's the point of the next few slides. Gamma feeds all three niche Greeks; Theta and Rho have lesser-known second derivatives of their own (sometimes called Veta and Vera) that this lesson leaves out of scope.

Section 2

All six, at a glance

Every one of these is a standard published closed form for a non-dividend European option — none derived from first principles here.

🔄

Vanna — ∂Δ/∂σ = ∂ν/∂S

How much Delta shifts per point of vol, or equivalently how much Vega shifts per $1 of spot. One of the two that matter in practice.

⏳

Charm — ∂Δ/∂t

How much Delta drifts purely from a day passing. The other one that matters in practice — largest in the last few days before expiry.

📈

Vomma — ∂ν/∂σ

Vega's own convexity — the formal basis of "volga" vol-of-vol trades. Real, but a step removed from daily dealer-flow reading.

🚀

Speed — ∂Γ/∂S

Gamma's own rate of change with price — a third derivative. Niche: matters for very large gamma books or gap moves.

🎨

Color — ∂Γ/∂t

Gamma's decay over calendar time. Niche: useful for planning a multi-day gamma-scalp, rarely quoted otherwise.

🌀

Zomma — ∂Γ/∂σ

Gamma's sensitivity to vol. Niche: relevant mainly in a fast vol-regime shift.

Section 3

Vanna, Charm, and Vomma — the closed forms

All reuse d₁, d₂ from the Black-Scholes lesson. φ(·) is the standard normal density — not N(·), the CDF used for Delta itself.

Try it
Vanna = −φ(d₁)·(d₂/σ)
Also equals ∂²C/∂S∂σ = ∂²C/∂σ∂S — Delta's vol-sensitivity and Vega's price-sensitivity are the exact same number, computed two different ways. A delta hedge can drift out of true purely from a vol move, with the underlying not having moved at all.
Charm = −φ(d₁)·[(2rt − d₂σ√τ) / (2τσ√τ)]
How much Delta drifts from one day passing, all else fixed. Mirrors Gamma's own late-life acceleration — especially large in the final days before expiry, since both are driven by T shrinking toward zero.
Vomma = ν · (d₁d₂ / σ)
Vega's own sensitivity to vol — Vega's convexity. A long strangle carries positive Vomma: it gains Vega disproportionately as vol itself rises. The formal basis of "volga" vol-of-vol positioning.
Speed, Color, and Zomma get their own honest treatment later in this deck — they're real, but genuinely more niche than these three.
Section 4

Vanna's symmetry — elegant, and guaranteed

Vanna is defined two different ways above. They read like two different quantities. They are not.

∂(∂C/∂S)/∂σ
shift Δ, watch it move with vol
=
∂(∂C/∂σ)/∂S
shift ν, watch it move with price
Why this is guaranteed, not lucky

Clairaut's theorem (also called Schwarz's theorem) states that for any function with continuous second partial derivatives, mixed partial derivatives are equal regardless of differentiation order. The Black-Scholes price C(S,σ,t) is smooth enough for the theorem to apply — so ∂²C/∂S∂σ and ∂²C/∂σ∂S must agree, not merely happen to in one worked example. Compute Vanna by asking "how does my hedge ratio move if vol jumps" or "how does my vol exposure move if spot jumps a dollar," and the two routes are guaranteed to land on the same number.

If you climb a smooth hill, how your northward slope changes as you walk east always equals how your eastward slope changes as you walk north. Vanna is exactly that fact, applied to an option price.

Deep in the money (low K) Vanna runs negative; out of the money it runs positive.
Section 7

Two flows, two different triggers

Vanna and Charm generalize the exact gamma-hedging mechanism into a vol-driven flow and a pure-time-driven flow.

Vanna flow

Trigger: implied vol moves, spot doesn't. If dealer OI carries positive Vanna, a vol rise alone raises customers' aggregate Delta, forcing the dealer's hedge to grow — real buying on a day price barely moved.

Charm flow

Trigger: a day passes, nothing else moves. Aggregate Delta shifts purely from time decay, forcing predictable rebalancing — no price or vol move required at all.

A vanna-flow scenario
IV climbs into a macro event
spot drifts in a tight range
→
Dealer's long-delta grows
desk sells to stay flat
Reading that sell flow as "someone thinks price is topping" would be wrong — it's a vanna flow, mechanically triggered by vol repricing, not a directional call. After the event, vol crush runs the same mechanism in reverse.
Section 8

Turning Vanna and Charm into dollars

Same aggregation logic as dealer-GEX — the difference is which market variable is assumed to move.

Dollar Vanna exposure

VannaExpk = Vannak × OIk × 100 × S

Shares' worth of delta-shift per unit σ, converted to dollars by one factor of S — mirroring GEX's own ×S step. Per 1.00 of σ raw; ×0.01 rescales to "per 1 vol point."

Dollar Charm exposure

CharmExpk = Charmk × OIk × 100

Dollar delta-shift per day — no ×S needed, since this is a pure time effect, not a price-scaled one. Charm's raw output is annualized; ÷365 converts to "per calendar day."

Under the standard "dealer short the OI they wrote" assumption, positive Vanna at a strike means a vol rise alone grows the dealer's required hedge — genuine buying with no price move behind it. Charm's mechanism is identical in spirit, driven by the calendar instead.

Section 10

A genuinely smaller flow, and why

Set the toy book above against the dealer-GEX lesson's own three-strike toy example.

Read this plainly, not as a trading claim

Vanna and Charm flows are directionally real and mechanically sound — same aggregation logic as dealer-GEX, over the same open interest, under the same dealer-sign assumption. But dollar Vanna exposure scales with S, while dollar Gamma exposure (GEX) scales with S² — one fewer power of S is a mechanical reason vanna flows run structurally smaller in typical dollar magnitude, not merely an assertion. This lesson explains the mechanism, not a specific tradeable edge.

Section 11

Speed, Color, Zomma — honestly niche

🚀

Speed = ∂Γ/∂S = −(Γ/S)·(d₁/(σ√τ) + 1)

Gamma's own rate of change with price — a third derivative of the option price. Rarely quoted; matters mainly for very large gamma books or extreme gap moves.

🎨

Color = ∂Γ/∂t

Gamma's decay over calendar time. Tells a gamma-scalper how their gamma will evolve day to day with no price move — useful for planning a multi-day scalp, rarely tracked otherwise.

🌀

Zomma = ∂Γ/∂σ

Gamma's sensitivity to vol. Relevant when vol itself is moving fast — the Gamma a delta-hedger thought they had can shift before any price move, purely from IV repricing.

All three are genuinely valid, correctly derivable Greeks — not wrong or made up. The honest distinction: most trading desks and retail options-flow tools don't quote or monitor them day to day, unlike Vanna and Charm, which show up regularly in real dealer-flow commentary.

Section 12

Checking the lesson's own claim against this repo

The full lesson's meta badge says

"CONCEPT — explained here, not built or tested anywhere in this repo." That's stale for Vanna and Charm specifically. This codebase has real, tested implementations: js/gammaGreeks.js exports bsVanna (the identical −φ(d₁)·d₂/σ closed form) and bsCharm, plus charmVannaExposure aggregating them across a chain exactly the way this deck's toy book does. They're wired into js/oi.js, server.js, js/oiLevelExport.js, and js/bot-config.js's conditioners, and covered by passing tests in gammaGreeks.test.mjs, ivMetrics.test.mjs, and oiZones.test.mjs.

The claim still holds, however, for Vomma, Speed, Color, and Zomma — a repo-wide search turns up no implementation of any of the four anywhere in this codebase. Those four are genuinely concept-only here, exactly as the lesson states.

Standing pitfall

Every one of these dollar figures is a description of mechanical hedging pressure, not a forecast of direction. "Large positive dealer Vanna exposure" says a vol move at that strike will force a large hedging flow — it says nothing about which way the market expects price to go. Confusing the two is the exact same category error the dealer-GEX lesson flags for Gamma.

Section 13

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

Why must ∂Δ/∂σ and ∂ν/∂S give the same number for Vanna, not just happen to agree?

C(S,σ,t) is smooth enough for Clairaut's/Schwarz's theorem to apply, so ∂²C/∂S∂σ = ∂²C/∂σ∂S is guaranteed — any correctly computed Vanna, from either direction, must agree.
Question 2

In the worked example (S=100, K=105), Vanna comes out positive. Why does that make economic sense?

K=105 > S=100, so this call is OTM. A vol rise increases the probability of finishing ITM, raising Delta — exactly what a positive Vanna says.
Question 3

What actually triggers a "charm flow," as distinct from a gamma flow or a vanna flow?

Charm = ∂Δ/∂t: aggregate dealer Delta shifts purely from time passing. Gamma flows need a price move; vanna flows need a vol move; charm flows need neither.
Question 4

Why does dollar Vanna exposure scale with S while dollar Gamma exposure (GEX) scales with S²?

GEX answers "dollars per 1% move," which needs S twice (shares→dollars, then $1-move→1%-move). VannaExp answers "dollars per 1 vol point," where a vol point isn't itself scaled by S — so only one factor of S appears.
Question 5

A commentator claims "large positive dealer Vanna exposure at a strike means the market is bullish." What's wrong with that?

Dealer Vanna exposure describes how much rebalancing a vol move will force, given current OI and an assumed dealer sign — it says nothing about which direction price is expected to move.
Section 14

What to carry forward

01

Second-order Greeks ask how a first-order Greek itself responds to price, vol, or time — Gamma already did this for Delta-vs-price; these six extend it to every other pair.

02

Vanna and Charm are the two that matter most — a vol-triggered flow and a pure-time-triggered flow, each real and mechanically distinct from price-triggered gamma hedging.

03

Vanna's two definitions are the same mixed partial, guaranteed equal by Clairaut's/Schwarz's theorem — not a coincidence, a theorem.

04

Dollar Vanna exposure scales with S; dollar Gamma exposure scales with S² — the mechanical reason vanna flows run roughly six orders of magnitude smaller than gamma flows in the lesson's own toy comparison.

05

Speed, Color, and Zomma are genuinely niche — real and correctly derived, but rarely tracked day to day, and (unlike Vanna/Charm) still unimplemented anywhere in this repo.

06

Every one of these flows is a mechanical hedging description, never a directional signal — confusing the two repeats the same mistake the dealer-GEX lesson already warns against.
