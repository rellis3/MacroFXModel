Lesson 6 · Market Microstructure

FX Fixings, or The Busiest Five Minutes in Currency Trading

FX has no closing auction, so funds, indices and custodians agree to use one published rate: the WM/Reuters 4pm London fix. That makes 4pm the moment a huge amount of money wants to trade. This guide covers how the rate is calculated, how banks fill the orders that target it, why month-end moves it, and how it was rigged until 2013.

The whole idea
🎧 Listen · the four-minute version

A trader and a quant talk the lesson through. No symbols, just the idea and one worked number, so it works on a walk or a commute.

Every month-end someone tells me to watch the four o'clock fix. What actually is it?

It's one exchange rate, published for four in the afternoon London time. Index providers value their indices at it. Fund administrators strike prices at it. Custodians convert at it. So a lot of money wants to trade at exactly that rate.

How do they work out one number for the whole market?

Every second for five minutes, from two and a half minutes before four to two and a half minutes after, they take a snapshot of trades and best prices on the main platforms. Then they take the median. Since February twenty fifteen it's been five minutes. Before that it was one.

Why the median and not the average?

Because one huge trade can drag an average, but it barely moves a median. To shift a median you have to keep the price where you want it for close to half the window. With one minute, that was about thirty seconds of pushing. With five minutes, it's about two and a half minutes. Much harder, much more expensive.

And the banks?

A fund says, buy me five hundred million euros at the fix. The bank promises a rate it doesn't know yet. If another client is selling two hundred million, the bank nets them and only has to buy three hundred million in the market. On three hundred million euros, every pip is thirty thousand dollars.

So where's the temptation?

The bank knows the flow before anyone else. If it buys early, cheap, and its own buying lifts the fix, it sells to the client at a higher rate and keeps the gap. Two pips on that order is sixty thousand dollars, paid by the client.

And that's what the scandal was.

Partly. Regulators found traders at different banks in chat groups, sharing what their clients were doing and trying to move the fixes together. On the twelfth of November twenty fourteen, British, American and Swiss regulators fined six banks about four point three billion dollars in total. The next May, four banks pleaded guilty in the United States.

What about month-end?

Funds that own foreign stocks and hedge the currency have to top up the hedge when the stocks rise. Say foreign investors hold six hundred billion dollars of hedged American shares and the market is up four percent. They sell twenty four billion more dollars. If Americans hold four hundred billion of hedged foreign shares that rose one percent, they buy four billion back. Net, about twenty billion dollars to sell.

So I just sell dollars into every month-end?

No. Everyone runs the same sum, so some of it is traded before the fix and priced in. The size estimate is rough. And research finds the dollar tends to firm into the big fixes and give it back afterwards, by a couple of basis points on a normal day. Use the sign as a warning, not a free trade.

The one-line version?

The fix is a five-minute median that the whole buy side marks to. Know when it is, expect a crowd and a jump, and never trade on what you know about someone else's order.

Section 1

One agreed rate, and everyone who needs it

A fix is a single published exchange rate for a stated moment, from a published method. WM/Reuters began publishing in 1994; the service is now called WMR and run by an LSEG company.

📈

Index providers

Multi-currency indices need one rate per currency per day. The administrator names MSCI, FTSE Russell and S&P among its users.

🧺

Passive funds and ETFs

Valued at the same rate as their index, so trading FX at the fix removes currency tracking error.

🏦

Custodians and administrators

Convert income and strike fund values at a rate clients and auditors can check.

🔁

Managers and corporates

Mandate transfers, hedge rolls and month-end rebalancing often go through the fix.

If you are valued at the 4pm rate, you want to trade at the 4pm rate. That is why the crowd forms.

One wild trade can drag an average as far as you like. A median only moves if you change which sample sits in the middle, so you must hold the price for a large part of the window.

Less-traded currencies use quotes sampled every 15 seconds over their own window. The FSB describes that quote-based fix as the average of the median bid and the median offer.

Section 3

How long must you hold the price to move a median?

To drag the median Δ pips above the clean level you need half the samples there. With ordinary prices scattering σ pips: T = N·(½ − q)/(1 − q), where q = 1 − Φ(Δ/σ).

Push far beyond the normal scatter and the answer tends to half the window: about 30 seconds before 2015, about 150 seconds after. The reform did not make manipulation impossible. It made it five times slower, costlier and more visible.

Honest fill
  • Buy the €300m evenly through the window, to track the median
  • Average 1.10012 vs fix 1.10010: −0.2 pip = −$6,000
  • The agreed, disclosed fee pays the bank for that risk
Conflicted fill
  • Buy it all before the window at 1.09990
  • The buying lifts the price; the fix prints 1.10010
  • The bank keeps 2 pips = $60,000, paid by its client
The FX Global Code allows working an order before, during or after the window, but not trading more than the client's interest to inflate the price against the client. The line is intent.

Section 5

Month-end: the currency of the market that rose most gets sold

Hedged equity funds top up their currency hedges at the month-end fix. Flow in USD = B·r_F − A·r_US.

Melvin and Prins (2015) found that equity gains over the month predict that market's currency weakening before the month-end 4pm fix. Read the answer as a sign and an order of magnitude: holdings, hedge ratios and timing are not public, and part of the flow trades in the days before.

Section 6

What the minutes around 4pm look like

From the FSB's 2014 report and the administrator's own 2020–21 analysis.

📊

Activity

Rises gently from about 15:55, jumps when the window opens at 15:57:30, stays high, tails off in the last 30 to 60 seconds and drops straight back after.

〰

Volatility

Rises slightly and peaks in the first 30 seconds of the window.

↔

Spreads

Tighten inside the window, often to their lowest of the day: the crowd brings liquidity as well as pressure.

📅

Month-ends

Consistently busier, and the net imbalance tends to be bigger at month-end and quarter-end.

One real number for scale

In WMR's analysis of EUR/USD from Q1 2020 to Q1 2021, the average absolute move from the 3pm rate to the 4pm rate was 0.1209%, against 0.0810% from 4pm to 5pm.

Section 7

The other fixes on the clock

🇯🇵

Tokyo, 9:55am

Each Japanese bank sets its own customer rate from prices at about 9:55. Busiest on payment days ending in 5 or 0 ("gotobi") and at month-end.

🇪🇺

ECB, about 14:10 CET

A concertation between central banks (14:15 CET in the scandal years), published around 16:00 CET since 1 July 2016, for information only.

🇬🇧

WMR, 4pm London

The closing rate for 150+ currencies, plus intraday rates, a 2pm CET fix and a noon New York fix.

🔄

The pattern

The dollar tends to rise into the Tokyo, ECB and London fixes and fall back after: about 2.5 basis points each way a day, 1999–2019 (Krohn, Mueller and Whelan).

Section 8 · It actually happened

The fixing scandal, 2013–2015

It actually happened

In June 2013 Bloomberg reported traders pushing trades before and during the 60-second window. On 12 November 2014 the FCA fined Citibank, HSBC, JPMorgan Chase, RBS and UBS £1.11bn; the CFTC fined the same five $1.475bn; the OCC fined Bank of America, Citibank and JPMorgan $950m; FINMA ordered UBS to pay CHF 134m. About $4.3bn, six banks, one day. On 20 May 2015 Citicorp, JPMorgan, Barclays and RBS agreed to plead guilty in the US over a chat room whose members called themselves "The Cartel", and the Fed fined six banks more than $1.8bn.

Section 9

Where this needs qualifying

Where the textbook version breaks

The median argument assumes the ordinary samples are independent of whoever is pushing. Collusion breaks that: when several banks trade together, or hold back the other side, the "ordinary" samples move too. The DOJ described exactly that: traders agreeing to withhold bids or offers so as not to move the rate against each other. The deeper fix was behavioural: separate desks, no sharing of client flow, transparent fees.

Where this needs qualifying

The hold-time formula is a simple model: it ignores that holding a price drags the seconds after it. The month-end model gives a sign, not a reliable size. And most price moves in the window are just genuine supply and demand meeting, not manipulation.

Section 10

What to do, and not do, around a fix

Do
  • Keep a fix calendar: 4pm London, month-end and quarter-end, ECB, Tokyo 9:55
  • Sign the month-end flow from relative equity performance
  • Decide stops and size before 15:57:30
  • Choose consciously between the fix and an algorithm over a longer period
Don't
  • Share or act on anyone's fixing orders or flows
  • Trade extra size into the window to move the print
  • Treat a few basis points of pre-fix drift as a free trade
  • Leave a tight stop in the window on a big month-end
Section 11

Quick check

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

Since 15 February 2015, what is the WMR 4pm rate for EUR/USD?

A five-minute window centred on 4pm, 301 samples, median. Before the reform it was one minute, 15:59:30 to 16:00:30.
Question 2

Why does a median make the fix harder to push than an average?

One outlier moves an average by its distance divided by N, but moves a median at most one place. To move it beyond the natural range you need about half the samples.
Question 3

A bank holds fix orders to buy €500m and sell €200m. What is a pip worth on what it must trade?

It crosses €200m internally and buys only €300m. 300,000,000 × 0.0001 = $30,000 per pip.
Question 4

US stocks rose 4% this month and foreign stocks 1%. Which way should hedge rebalancing push the dollar at the fix?

Foreign holders of hedged US shares must sell more dollars to match the bigger holding. With A = 600 and B = 400 ($bn): 4 − 24 = −$20bn.
Question 5

What happened on 12 November 2014?

FCA £1.11bn, CFTC $1.475bn, OCC $950m, FINMA CHF 134m. The window changed on 15 February 2015; the Global Code came in May 2017.

Scenario drill · 12 November 2014

A chat message the day the fines landed

You run the G10 spot desk at a bank. For tomorrow's 4pm fix you hold a client order to buy €400m and another client's order to sell €150m, both at the fix plus an agreed fee. A trader you have known for years at another bank messages you in a chat group: "Big euro buyer at the fix tomorrow? Me too. Pass yours to me and I'll make sure it goes our way." What do you do?

  1. Tell him your net and agree to pool the orders so one of you dominates the window.
    This is the conduct regulators fined: sharing client order information between banks and coordinating to move a fix. With a median, pooling orders is exactly what makes a push possible. It is also how the US criminal case against four banks was built.
  2. Say nothing, but buy an extra €250m of your own just before the window, then sell it back during the window.
    Trading more than the client's interest just before the window to inflate the price against the client is a listed unacceptable practice. Doing it alone instead of with a friend does not change what it is.
  3. Decline to discuss client business, cross the €150m internally and work the €250m residual through the window under your documented procedure.
    This is what the FSB's September 2014 recommendations ask for, and what the FX Global Code later wrote down: no sharing of flows, netting client interest, executing the net amount in a way meant to track the fix, paid by a transparent fee.
  4. Stop accepting fixing orders at all.
    You can't misuse what you don't hold, so it is safe. But clients still need the service, and the FSB chose not to ban fix trading for that reason. Handling the orders properly serves them better than refusing them.

What happened. That day the FCA fined Citibank, HSBC, JPMorgan Chase, RBS and UBS a combined £1.11bn, after finding that traders in groups with names like "the players" and "the A-team" had shared client order information and tried to manipulate the 4pm WM/Reuters and ECB fixes. The CFTC fined the same five banks $1.475bn, the OCC fined Bank of America, Citibank and JPMorgan $950m, and FINMA ordered UBS to pay CHF 134m: about $4.3bn in total. On 20 May 2015 Citicorp, JPMorgan, Barclays and RBS agreed to plead guilty in the US over a euro-dollar chat room whose members called themselves "The Cartel", and the Federal Reserve fined six banks more than $1.8bn.

Section 12

What to carry forward

01

A fix is one agreed rate for one moment. The WMR 4pm London rate is what indices, fund values and custodians use, so benchmark-linked FX crowds into it.

02

Since 15 February 2015 it is the median of one-second snapshots from 15:57:30 to 16:02:30; before, a one-minute window.

03

Moving a median means holding the price for up to half the window: about 30 seconds before the reform, about 150 seconds now.

04

Banks net fixing orders and trade the residual; on €300m a pip is $30,000. Pricing must be transparent and client flow is never the bank's signal.

05

Month-end: hedge top-ups sell the currency of the stock market that rose most. Use the sign, distrust the size.

06

It actually happened: about $4.3bn of fines on 12 November 2014, guilty pleas on 20 May 2015, and a wider window in between.

What this now lets you do

Plan around the busiest scheduled minutes of the FX day: know when each fix is, estimate the month-end direction from equity performance, set stops and execution before the window opens, and recognise the line between handling a client's fixing order and trading against it.
