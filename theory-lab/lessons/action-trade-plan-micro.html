From a View to a Trade Plan

"EUR/USD looks cheap" is a view. A trade is five numbers written before you click: entry, invalidation, stop, target, size. The most important one, invalidation, comes from your analysis, not from how much you can stand to lose.

The whole idea
🎧 Listen · the four-minute version

A trader and a quant talk the lesson through. No symbols, just the idea and one worked number, so it works on a walk or a commute.

EUR/USD looks cheap. The rate spread says it should recover. I'm buying.

That's a view. Where do you get in, where are you wrong, where do you take profit, and how big is it?

In at ten eight fifty. I'll risk twenty pips. That's what I'm comfortable losing.

Twenty pips is a pain limit, not a reason. Does anything about your thesis change at ten eight thirty?

Not really. The spread model is just as right there.

Then the stop is in the wrong place. A normal day moves about sixty-five pips from high to low. Twenty pips is about a third of that. A quiet morning can stop you out, and then the trade does exactly what you said it would.

So where should it go?

Start from the reason. What price proves you wrong?

The March low at ten seven eighty held even when the spread was at its widest. A close below it means the market sees something my model doesn't.

That's your invalidation. Put the stop a little beyond it, a quarter of a day's range, so sixteen pips. Stop at ten seven sixty-four. That's eighty-six pips away.

Eighty-six pips? That's four times what I wanted to lose.

No. That's where the fear belongs: in the size, not the stop. Decide the money first. Half a percent of a hundred thousand is five hundred dollars. Divide by eighty-six pips at ten dollars a pip. That's point five eight lots. Wrong costs you five hundred dollars either way.

And the target?

From the analysis too. Eleven hundred is a hundred and fifty pips away. That's one point seven four times your risk, so you break even if you win about thirty-six percent of the time, before costs.

And if the stop just works, I'm safe.

A stop is an order, not a guarantee. In January twenty fifteen the Swiss central bank dropped its floor under the franc without warning. Stops just under one twenty filled far lower. Some traders lost many times what they planned, and a broker went under. Around pegs, events and weekends, size for the gap.

The one-line version?

Find where the reason breaks, put the stop just past it, and let that distance set your size.

Section 1 · How it reached trading

Rules you can write down

Trading practice · the Turtle experiment (1983–84); R-multiples (Van Tharp, 1990s); triple-barrier labels (López de Prado, 2018)

How it reached trading

In 1983 Richard Dennis and William Eckhardt trained novice "Turtles" on a fully written rule set: entry, a stop set as a multiple of an average true range, and a position size that risked a fixed slice of the account.

Van Tharp later made R, the amount risked, the unit of every result. López de Prado's triple-barrier labels use the same three exits: target, stop, time limit.

Section 3

Invalidation comes from the reason

From the analysis

"The March low at 1.0780 held at the spread's widest point. A close below it means the market sees something my model doesn't."

From pain

"I'll risk 20 pips because that's comfortable." The market doesn't know your comfort level. Nothing about the thesis changes at that price.

Pain is a real input, but it belongs in the size. If the honest stop is far away, trade smaller. Don't move the stop closer.
Section 4

The formulas

stop = invalidation ∓ buffer × ATR
R = target distance ÷ stop distance · break-even = 1 ÷ (1 + R)
lots = money at risk ÷ (stop pips × pip value)

Check the stop in ATRs. One ATR is an ordinary day's high-to-low. A stop well under half an ATR from entry gets hit by noise, so wait for a better entry rather than tightening it.

Section 6 · Worked example

EUR/USD, view to plan

View: spread says EUR/USD cheap; aim 1.1000 within ~3 weeks. Price 1.0850, ATR 65 pips.
Invalidation: March low 1.0780 (the reason fails below it).
Stop: buffer 0.25 × 65 ≈ 16 → 1.0764; distance 86 pips = 1.32 ATR.
Target: 150 pips → R = 1.74; break-even 36.4%.
Size: $500 ÷ (86 × $10) = 0.581 → 0.58 lots (risk $498.80; +$870 at target).
Time stop: 15 sessions. Gap line: open at 1.0700 → loss $870.
Same view, pain stop

A 20-pip stop at 1.0830 is 0.31 ATR, inside a quiet morning's wiggle. It shows 7.5R on paper, but the hit rate collapses because most "losses" are noise.

Section 7 · It actually happened

15 January 2015: the franc floor goes

From 2011 the Swiss National Bank held EUR/CHF above 1.20. Traders went long near 1.20 with stops just below. On their analysis the floor could not break.

The invalidation

Correct. Below 1.20 the thesis was dead.

The exit

The SNB dropped the floor without warning. EUR/CHF briefly traded well below parity, and stops filled far lower. Alpari UK went insolvent; FXCM needed a rescue loan.

The lesson

A stop is an order, not a guarantee. Around pegs, events and weekends, add a gap line and size for it.

Thesis

One sentence: instrument, direction, reason, horizon.

Invalidation + why

If the "why" is "it's what I can afford", start again.

Stop, in ATR

Target + time stop

From the analysis, with an expiry date.

R and break-even

1/(1+R), then costs on top.

Size + gap line

Section 11

Check yourself

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

Your honest invalidation is 150 pips away and that scares you. What changes?

Pain goes into size. A 50-pip stop exits while the thesis is still alive.
Question 2

Stop 40 pips, target 60 pips. Break-even win rate before costs?

R = 1.5, so 1/(1+1.5) = 40%. A 2-pip round-trip cost pushes it to 42%.
Question 3

$500 at risk, stop 100 pips, $10 per pip per lot. Size?

500 ÷ (100 × 10) = 0.5 lots. A loss at the stop costs exactly $500.
Question 4

Why did EUR/CHF stops just under 1.20 fail in January 2015?

The level was right. The exit assumption was wrong. Size for the gap when one is possible.
Your call
Scenario drill · 14 January 2015

Long EUR/CHF just above the 1.20 floor

Since September 2011 the Swiss National Bank has held a minimum rate of 1.20 francs per euro, promising unlimited purchases to defend it. For more than three years EUR/CHF has sat just above 1.20. You are long EUR/CHF near the floor with a stop a little below it. Your analysis says the floor is the invalidation level: below 1.20, the thesis is dead. How do you size the trade?

  1. Size it to the short stop as usual. The stop is close, so a big position only risks a small amount.
    This assumes the stop fills near its level. A stop is an order, not a guarantee. If the floor goes, there may be no prices between 1.20 and wherever the market lands, and a big position turns a small planned loss into a huge one.
  2. Keep the floor as invalidation, but add a gap line: the loss if price jumps far past the stop. Size so that loss is one you can survive.
    The invalidation is right; the exit assumption is the weak point. When a thesis rests on a policy or a peg, the plan sizes for the gap, not for the stop. That usually means a much smaller position.
  3. Skip the trade. A peg can break at any moment.
    Safe, and sometimes right. But the thesis may be sound. The problem is size, and a small position with a written gap line lets you take the trade without betting the account on the stop.
  4. Remove the stop. The central bank has promised unlimited purchases, so it cannot break.
    This turns an analysis into a belief. A plan with no exit for being wrong has no maximum loss at all.

What happened. On the morning of 15 January 2015 the SNB abandoned the floor without warning. The franc jumped in minutes and EUR/CHF briefly traded well below parity. Stops placed just under 1.20 were filled far lower, wherever the next real price was. Losses were many times what plans had allowed, some retail clients ended with negative balances, the UK arm of the broker Alpari went into insolvency, and FXCM needed an emergency rescue loan.

Sources: Thomas Jordan, SNB press conference remarks on discontinuing the minimum exchange rate, 15 January 2015 (BIS reprint); Finance Magnates, "Leucadia Hands FXCM $300 Mln Life-Line", 16 January 2015.
