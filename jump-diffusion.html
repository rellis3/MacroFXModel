Two days can print the same range and be completely different events: one ground there steadily, the other sat flat and gapped on a data release. This page splits realised variance into the part that came from smooth diffusion and the part that came from discrete jumps. Every number is descriptive state — it describes how today moved, and deliberately claims nothing about tomorrow. Nothing here feeds the live bots, same discipline as Vol Intelligence.

What this measures bipower variation

Realised variance RV = Σr² counts everything. Bipower variation BV = (π/2)·Σ|rt||rt-1| is jump-robust — a lone jump enters only one product on each side, so it washes out. The difference is the jump part: jump share = max(RV − BV, 0) / RV. Measured on 5-minute returns over the London day, with returns that span a session break dropped (an archive hole would otherwise read as a jump). Jump times come from a Lee-Mykland test with a stated 1%-per-day false-positive rate, after removing the ~3× intraday volatility cycle.

This is a continuous share, not a jump/no-jump verdict. max(…,0) floors the estimate because bipower is itself noisy and the difference goes negative on plenty of genuinely quiet days. Read “today is 18% jump-driven” as a description of character, never as “there were N jumps” — and always against the pair’s own percentiles below, since 18% is ordinary for one pair and a 95th-percentile day for another.

Why there is no forecast on this page three failed tests

The obvious idea is that a jump-inflated volatility reading overstates tomorrow, so stripping the jump out should forecast better. The reasoning is sound and the underlying effect is real — a jumpier day is followed by a quieter one at a matched volatility level. It still does not produce a better forecast, tested three separate ways:

So the page describes today and stops there. A “treat tomorrow’s cone with wider error bars” chip would be exactly the claim these three tests failed to support. The honest read: the jump share tells you what kind of day this was, and that is genuinely useful context — it just is not a forward signal, and labelling it as one would be the easiest way to make this page worse than nothing.
