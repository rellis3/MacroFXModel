Moves are from the instant of the release: SOFR, Euribor and their line in bp of rate (positive = rates up); the 2y and 10y are note futures in % of price, so a negative number there means yields rose; the dollar and Nasdaq in %. The 2y, 10y and dollar are 15-minute CFD or short-history before September 2026 (see the data checks), so their 1- and 5-minute cells are blank for older releases rather than guessed. Agreement counts the SOFR leg, Euribor leg, 2y and 10y that moved in the same rates direction over the first 30 minutes. Asset filters only change which columns show: EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, gold and the S&P 500 are OANDA 1-minute CFDs up to 19 Oct 2026 and CME/COMEX futures after; VIX and VXN have 1-minute history only from 24 Aug 2026, so older releases show their daily change only (points); VXN also prints only in the New York cash session, so only afternoon releases (the Fed decision) have a minute reading for it. Surprise is the raw actual minus consensus in the release's own unit (payrolls in thousands); modelling it is Layer 2.

Days view: daily closes, measured from the close of the day before the release. The 2y and 10y yields, 10y real yield and 10y breakeven (US Treasury curves) and the Euribor and SOFR legs are in bp, positive = rates up (a rise in the real yield is a tightening, a rise in the breakeven is more inflation priced in); dollar and Nasdaq-100 in %. Release day is the whole day's move to the close, so it also contains the hours before the release; run-up is the 5 trading days up to the pre-release close, signed as the move into the release. Euribor stops trading before a US evening decision, so its release-day cell is blank for FOMC.

Typical shape by release

Median absolute move from the release instant, over the releases in each family (1-minute assets only). It shows how fast and how large a typical reaction is, not which way it goes. Cells show the median, with how many releases moved at all in brackets.

Typical over days, by release

Median absolute move (the size of a typical response, not its direction) over the releases in each family, from the pre-release close: the 5 days before, the release day, then 1, 2 and 5 days after. The last column is the share of releases that were up at +1 day. Follows the release tabs above.

Exploratory and descriptive, not pre-registered; it predicts nothing. Built from C.OG's layered frame (Discord, 19 Oct 2026): Layer 1 is what happened, Layers 2 (why) and 3 (how unusual) come next, one at a time. The laptop pushes new bars every 5 minutes around a scheduled release and the page re-reads every 30 seconds, so a release forms here as it happens, at the delay shown in the freshness chip. If the laptop or the Gateway is off, the chip says so and the numbers stop where the data stops. Code: scripts/release_layer1/.
