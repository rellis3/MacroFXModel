Appendix C — Master reference: the daily scans (as taught)

Risk dashboard (L2): VIX level & term structure → CDX HY day change → JPY vs high-beta FX → stock-bond correlation check → if 3+ warn, cut gross first.

Fixed income scan, 5 min (L4): 10Y level/change → 2s10s → 2Y → HY spreads → MOVE → 10Y TIPS → key rate differentials.

FX scan, 5 min (L5): DXY direction & why → USD/JPY (JPY strength = risk-off) → EUR/USD → AUD/USD (risk + China) → EM FX breadth → cross-currency basis if available.

Pre-trade checks: levels above you in the hierarchy (L2) → positioning (who's in, what forces them out, am I early or late, what's my edge) (L2) → bond-first framework (L4) → regime & coherence context (L6).

Section 04

Key Takeaways

✓Systematic validation decouples time from capital. Hypothesis → in-sample → out-of-sample → walk-forward → small live size fails most ideas before any money is at risk; ad-hoc/intuition-based trading fails ideas with capital already deployed — the "double negative" of lost time and lost capital (Lesson 1).
✓Check the levels above you before any position. Policy cascades central banks → government bonds → currencies → equities → credit, with information degrading and lag increasing at each step down; most retail activity happens at the noisiest, most crowded bottom rungs (Lesson 2).
✓Fed communication is precise, not vague — but it's conditional, not a commitment. Statement word-diffs, press-conference tone, and dot-plot shifts telegraph policy before it happens; the dot plot itself has a documented poor track record and should never be read as a promise (Lesson 3).
✓Bond math is mechanical and it leads the other asset classes. Price and yield move inversely by definition; duration converts yield moves into price moves; an inverted yield curve has preceded every US recession in 50+ years (6–24 month variable lead); credit spreads and real yields (TIPS) often explain equity/gold/FX moves before the equity tape does (Lesson 4).
⚠Rate differentials drive FX medium-term, but risk sentiment overrides in stress. Haven flows (JPY, CHF, USD) and carry-trade unwinds can swamp the rate-differential logic entirely — a sharp JPY rally on a "risk-off" day is a mechanical unwind signal to read, not a rate-based forecast to fight (Lesson 5).
⚠Understanding the cross-asset framework is not the same as having an edge. The lesson material's own verdict: markets are efficient at short horizons, and daily discretionary macro trading is documented to be extremely difficult even for professionals — the institutional edge is data infrastructure, systematic implementation, longer time horizons, and the discipline to test before trading, not discretionary daily macro calls (Lesson 6).
✓These are course notes, not a validated trading system. The specific correlations, percentages and figures throughout are exemplar data representing typical historical ranges, as the lesson material itself states — the mechanisms are well-established, but exact numbers should be treated as illustrative until verified against current data, and none of this framework has been backtested or proven out-of-sample on this platform.

Appendix D — Topics flagged for future investigation

These are open threads named in the lesson material itself — testable ideas the course points at but doesn't resolve, not conclusions to trade on as given.

  • Lead-lag measurement: cross-correlation by lag, Granger causality, VAR, rolling-window stability (L6 notes these are strongest at regime turns).
  • Regime detection methods: rule-based thresholds vs HMM vs clustering; robustness across methods (L6).
  • Coherence scoring: cross-asset correlation vs regime norms as a conviction/size input; behaviour of trend vs mean-reversion under high/low coherence (L6).
  • Correlation methodology: rolling vs EWMA (λ=0.94), regime-conditional estimates, stress correlations (L6).
  • Carry strategy literature: UIP failure, long-run carry returns and crash risk (L5).
  • Pre-FOMC drift and the two-phase FOMC reaction; post-event drift over days 1–5 (L3).
  • Dot-plot vs market-pricing gaps as surprise-direction indicator (L3).
  • Net-liquidity ↔ risk-asset relationship ("a condition, not a signal") (L3/L6).
  • Calendar-flow effects: month-end rebalancing pressure, OpEx pinning/gamma, September/year-end seasonality (L2).
  • Positioning extremes (COT percentile, short interest, put/call) as reversal conditioning (L2).
  • Yield-curve inversion lead times and the un-inversion "final warning" (L4).
  • Real-yield relationships with gold and growth equities; breakeven decomposition of nominal moves (L4).
  • Cross-currency basis as a funding-stress indicator; quarter-end seasonality; Fed swap lines (L5).
  • Intervention episodes and their limits (Japan 2022–24) (L5).
  • Vol-adjusted sizing and expression selection across the capital structure (L6).
