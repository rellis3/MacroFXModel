Central Bank Policy, or Trade the Gap, Not the Decision

How the Fed decides, how it talks, what its dots and balance sheet really say, and why the tradeable object is always the difference between what the Fed signals and what the market has already priced. Course framework, not a backtested strategy.

The whole idea
Section 1

Markets trade changes in expectations

Edge, if there is any, is seeing where the Fed is heading before it is fully priced.

📥

1. What it watches

The inputs: inflation (PCE), jobs, financial conditions, global factors.

🧠

2. How members think

Hawks, doves, the Chair, the voters of the year, the dissents.

💬

3. How they talk

Statement, press conference, minutes, dots, speeches: the words reveal intent.

⚖️

4. Versus the market

Futures, OIS and FedWatch show what is already priced. Only the gap is news.

The thermostat announces where it plans to move the dial. The market adjusts before the dial moves, so the dial turning is rarely the news.

Section 3

The Fed's reaction function

Given these inputs, here is the likely response, and how markets tend to read it.

Inputs and response
  • Inflation (PCE): above 2% tighten; below, ease. Hot CPI: yields up.
  • Employment: tight labor tighten; rising unemployment ease. Late-cycle, bad news can be good news.
  • Financial conditions: loose can tighten more; tight pauses or eases. Selloffs slow the Fed.
  • Global factors: a strong USD is already tightening; global stress makes them cautious.
Fed-watcher scorecard
  • Inflation check: core PCE vs 2%, trend.
  • Labor check: unemployment direction, wages.
  • Financial conditions: spreads, equities.
  • Committee: who votes this year, net tilt.
  • Recent Fedspeak: tone shifts.

Dots are conditional ("if the economy evolves as I expect"). The shift versus the previous SEP is the signal, not the level. Dispersion shows how much weight the median deserves. Illustrative numbers; the reaction line is the course framework, not a tested rule.

Section 8 · It actually happened

Language first, action later: 2021-22

It actually happened

For months in 2021 the Fed called inflation transitory. On 30 November 2021 Chair Powell said it was "probably a good time to retire that word". The 15 December statement spoke of "elevated levels of inflation", sped up the taper, and the median dot showed hikes in 2022. On 26 January 2022 the Committee said it "expects it will soon be appropriate to raise the target range". On 16 March 2022 it raised the range to 0.25-0.50%, the first hike since 2018.

Also in the Fedspeak toolkit: the "data dependent" decoder (ask which data), trial balloons (a lesser voice or a journalist floats an idea), and clustering (several officials using the same new phrase).

Section 9

The balance sheet is liquidity

From about $900 billion before 2008 to nearly $9 trillion at the 2022 peak.

QE: reserves created
  • Announce purchases (e.g. $120B a month)
  • Buy from dealers, crediting their reserves
  • Sellers rebalance into credit and equities
  • Asset prices rise; effects are gradual
QT: reserves drained
  • Bonds mature without replacement, under a monthly cap (e.g. $60B Treasuries + $35B MBS)
  • Treasury reissues; the private sector pays with cash from markets
  • Reserves drain: a headwind for risk assets
  • Can turn into sudden stress when reserves get scarce
Net liquidity ≈ Fed balance sheet − TGA − RRP. Even during QT, a TGA drawdown or a falling RRP can mean it is rising: track the composite, weekly, via H.4.1 each Thursday.

Section 11

Other central banks, and policy divergence

🇪🇺

ECB

Single mandate (price stability); manages fragmentation risk across about 20 economies.

🇯🇵

BoJ

As taught: Yield Curve Control, structurally dovish, owns about half of JGBs. The wildcard. (YCC ended in March 2024; the mechanism is the lesson.)

🇬🇧

BoE

Stagflation risk: high inflation plus weak growth limits its options.

🇨🇳

PBoC

Different toolkit: RRR, administered rates, FX management; moves often outside meetings.

Divergence scenarios
  • Fed hawkish, ECB dovish: EUR/USD falls (2022: to parity)
  • Fed hawkish, BoJ dovish: USD/JPY rises (115 to 150+)
  • Fed pauses, others catch up: USD weakens broadly
  • Synchronized easing: FX stable, risk assets rally

Section 15

Where this needs qualifying

Where this needs qualifying

These are framework claims from a course, not validated trading rules. Committee members, stances and whole regimes change (the BoJ ended YCC in 2024), so the mechanics last and the specifics do not. Everyone watches the Fed: understanding it is the entry ticket, and any edge is in a differentiated view of the gap, in sizing, or in patience.

Where the textbook version breaks

On 22 September 2022 the policy-divergence framework was right about direction and still hurt: Japan's Ministry of Finance intervened for the first time since 1998 and USD/JPY dropped about five yen in a short burst before the gap pulled it back up. Divergence gives direction, not a smooth path.

Section 16

Quick check

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

A futures contract trades at 95.20 and the current effective rate is 4.58%. What does it price?

Implied rate = 100 − 95.20 = 4.80%, 22bp above 4.58%. 22 / 25 = 0.88.
Question 2

What is the right way to use the dot plot?

Dots say where each member thinks rates should go if the economy evolves as they expect. When it surprises, they move. The 2020-21 dots showed rates near zero for years; the range reached 4.25-4.50% by the end of 2022.
Question 3

The Fed's balance sheet is shrinking under QT, yet net liquidity rises. How?

Net liquidity = balance sheet − TGA − RRP. Falling TGA and RRP reduce the subtracted terms and can outweigh the runoff.
Question 4

What does the first market move after the statement tell you?

Phase 1 (2:00-2:05) is keyword scanning, high volatility and low signal. Phase 2 is human digestion; true repositioning by funds takes days.
Question 5

The Fed hikes while the BoJ holds. What does the divergence framework predict, and what is the catch?

A widening rate gap supports the dollar against the yen (115 to 150+ in 2022-24), but authorities can move the pair several yen in minutes. Direction is not a smooth path.
Your call
Scenario drill · 22 September 2022

The Fed hiked 75bp yesterday, and the BoJ has just held

You have been long USD/JPY on the policy-divergence theme since the spring: the Fed is hiking fast, and the Bank of Japan keeps its policy rate and its 10-year yield control unchanged. Yesterday the Fed raised its target range by 75bp to 3.00-3.25% and signalled "ongoing increases". Today the Bank of Japan has just left policy unchanged, and USD/JPY has jumped to about 146, the highest in 24 years. Japan's Ministry of Finance has been warning about the yen's moves. What do you do with the position?

  1. Add. The divergence just widened again, so the trend is your friend.
    The thesis is intact, but the risk is not symmetric. The course calls the BoJ and Japanese authorities the wildcard; a government that has been warning about the currency can move it by several yen in minutes, and adding size at the 24-year high is the largest exposure to exactly that tail.
  2. Keep the thesis, cut the size, and plan for a sharp one-way gap against you.
    Divergence describes the direction of travel but not the path. A smaller position that survives an intervention gap lets you stay in a thesis you still believe. This matches the course's "size for the volatility" and "sitting out is valid" rules for event risk.
  3. Reverse to short: intervention is obviously coming, so fade the move.
    Fading a trend because a government might act is a timing bet against the policy gap, with no trigger. The pair can be pushed down on the day and still go back up if the divergence is unchanged, so a full reversal risks being right about the event and wrong about the aftermath.
  4. Hold at the same size and wait for the Bank of Japan's next meeting.
    Reasonable if you already sized for a tail like this, but nothing in the setup tells you that the next BoJ meeting is when the risk lands. The Ministry of Finance does not wait for meetings.

What happened. Later on 22 September, Japan's Ministry of Finance intervened to buy yen, for the first time since 1998, and USD/JPY fell by about five yen, from roughly 146 to roughly 140, in a short burst. The pair then drifted back up as the policy gap persisted, reaching about 152 in October, when Japan intervened again. In December 2022 the Bank of Japan widened the band around its 10-year yield target, a de facto tightening, and the yen jumped by more than 3% in a day. The policy divergence was real and persistent, and it came with a policy-driven path risk the textbook framework does not price.

Sources: Federal Reserve, FOMC statement, 21 September 2022; Ministry of Finance, Japan, foreign exchange intervention operations. Yen levels are rounded; the Ministry's data gives the exact intervention amounts.

Section 17

What to carry forward

01

Markets move on changes in expectations. The tradeable object is the gap between the Fed's signalled path and the priced path.

02

Know the players: dual mandate, 12 voters of 19, hawk-dove tilt, dissents. The Chair dominates; a unified committee is more predictable.

03

The cycle is blackout, statement (2:00 pm ET), presser (2:30 pm ET), minutes three weeks later. Language precedes action: diff the statements.

04

Dots are conditional projections, not commitments. Compare with futures-implied pricing (implied = 100 − price), the shift from the last SEP and the dispersion.

05

Balance sheet = liquidity: net liquidity ≈ balance sheet − TGA − RRP, tracked weekly. QT can turn sudden when reserves get scarce (September 2019).

06

Divergence drives FX, the BoJ is the wildcard, and patience is edge: do not chase the first spike, and size for event risk. Only the post-meeting dollar base rate has survived this desk's tests.

What this now lets you do

Read a Fed meeting as a set of differences: this statement against the last, these dots against the futures, this balance sheet against its liquidity components, this central bank against that one. Prepare scenarios and sizes before 2:00 pm, and treat sitting out as a valid decision.
