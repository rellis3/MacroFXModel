What Moves Markets, Part 1: The Flow Hierarchy and Rates

Policy shifts cascade through markets in a fixed order, and interest-rate gaps pull money across borders. Part 1 of 2: who moves first, why the carry trade pays little and loses much, and what rate cycles do to everything else. A course-notes framework, not a backtested strategy.

Section 4

Rates are the price of money: the carry pull

$1M earns more in one country than another and flows there. A carry trade borrows the cheap currency and holds the dear one: R = (1 + iH)/(1 + x) − (1 + iF), where x is the funding currency's rally.

Try it
High-yield rate iH (e.g. USD)5.0%
Funding rate iF (e.g. JPY)0.1%
Funding currency's move x (+ = it rallies)+0%
Carry 4.90% · exchange-rate effect 0.00% · net 4.90%
Years of carry erased by the move (x ÷ carry): 0.00 · break-even rally x*: 4.90%
The pull on $1M: $50,000 at iH versus $1,000 at iF, a gap of $49,000

Set the yen to +10%: the net is −4.65%, about two years of carry gone in days. That is "picking up pennies in front of a steamroller".

Rate-cycle playbook
Domestic currency strengthens · bond prices fall · growth stocks underperform (higher discount rate) · value and banks outperform (margins widen) · gold pressured · EM outflows.
The mirror: currency weakens · bonds rally · growth outperforms · value and banks underperform (margin compression) · gold supported · EM inflows.
Weeks 1–4: 2Y yield 0.25% → 1.5%. Months 1–3: DXY +15%, EUR/USD 1.15 → parity. Months 3–9: Nasdaq −35%. Months 9–18: HY spreads +250 bps. Those watching bonds exited growth stocks 3–6 months before the bottom fell out. Figures are the course's, rounded; one cycle, in hindsight.
Gold nuance

Gold follows real rates (nominal minus inflation). If inflation rises faster than nominal rates, gold can rally even while the Fed hikes. Watch TIPS yields.

Section 7

Quick check

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

Why check bond yields and credit spreads before trading an equity index?

Policy reaches bonds in hours, FX in days to weeks, equities in weeks to months and credit in months to quarters. Each step down adds lag, noise and crowding.
Question 2

A carry trade earns 4.9% a year and the funding currency rallies 10% in days. What happens?

1.05/1.10 − 1.001 = −4.65%. The 10% move is 10 ÷ 4.9 = 2.04 years of carry, which is why an unwind is called picking up pennies in front of a steamroller.
Question 3

The dollar pays 5% and the yen 0.1%. What yen rally over the year makes the carry trade break even?

The break-even is x* = (1 + iH)/(1 + iF) − 1 = 4.90%. A bigger rally turns a year of carry into a loss, which is why a 10% move costs about two years of it.
Question 4

The Fed hikes while inflation rises even faster. Why might gold rally anyway?

Higher real rates raise the opportunity cost of holding gold; falling real rates lower it. Watch TIPS yields, not only nominal ones.
Section 8

What to carry forward

01

The hierarchy is not optional: central banks, bonds, FX, equities, credit. Information degrades at each level, so check the levels above before any position.

02

Rates drive cross-border flow; watch the 2-year. A carry trade earns steadily and gives it back in a rush: a 10% yen rally against 4.9% carry is about two years gone.

03

On this desk's data, rates leading equities intraday and yields predicting FX both came back null. The mechanisms are real; the trades are not shown.

What this now lets you do

Before acting on a headline or a chart, ask where in the queue your market sits and what the rate gap is paying money to do. Part 2 adds the regime, the liquidity tide, the calendar and positioning.
