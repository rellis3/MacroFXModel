Cross-Asset Synthesis, Part 1: The Framework

Rates, credit, currencies, equities and volatility all react to the same macro story, at different speeds and with different amounts of noise. This first of two parts is the course framework for reading them together: regimes, the data dashboard, lead-lag and correlation. Part 2 tests it against three real episodes. It is a framework as taught, not a backtested strategy.

The whole idea
Section 1

One view, many expressions

"The Fed pivots dovish earlier than expected." A macro fund asks five questions before it trades that.

🎯

Highest-conviction expression?

Perhaps 2-year rates, not equities.

⚖

Best risk-adjusted expression?

Perhaps rate options, for convexity.

✅

What should confirm it?

Credit tightening, a weaker dollar.

🚫

What invalidates it?

Inflation re-accelerating.

⏱

What is the horizon?

Weeks, months or quarters.

Watching only equities is watching one character of a film. Equities are noisy, narrative-driven and often the last to price a macro shift; the rates, credit and FX moves that told the story earlier go unseen.
Section 2

Coherence: do the layers agree?

High coherence
  • Every asset class prices the same scenario
  • Trends tend to persist; trend-following tends to work
  • More conviction, bigger size is defensible
Low coherence
  • Conflicting stories: someone is wrong (opportunity), or markets are in transition (wait), or it is genuinely uncertain (reduce risk)
  • A divergence does not say what to do. It says investigate
Where this needs qualifying

The course states the cascade order two ways (Rates, Credit, FX, Equities, and Rates, FX, Credit, Equities), and its own case studies differ: late 2023 went rates, credit, FX, equities; March 2023 started in credit. The order is a hypothesis to test, not a rule. Understanding this is not an edge: markets are efficient at short horizons.

Section 4

Transitions are where the money is made

Where this needs qualifying

Rule-based thresholds are interpretable but arbitrary; Hidden Markov Models are elegant but can overfit; clustering is another route. Robustness means testing across methods. Early transition detection is the hard, valuable part.

Section 6

Lead-lag: slide one series along the other

A simulation: the follower is the leader delayed by 5 days, plus noise. Find the delay.

Try it · simulation, not market data
Lag tested (days the leader is shifted forward)k = 0
—

The tool is the cross-correlation ρ(k) = corr(Xt, Yt+k): scan k, find the peak. The course also names Granger causality, VAR models and rolling-window stability tests. Its critical finding: leads are strongest at regime turning points and weakest in stable trends, so cherry-picked episodes flatter them.

Section 7

Divergences say "investigate", not "trade"

A simulation: equities and credit agree for 240 days, then credit cracks. Move the alarm threshold.

Try it · simulation, not market data
Flag when the gap exceeds1.50 σ
—

The course's examples: equities rallying while credit widens (credit is often right at turning points); yields falling while equities stay flat (bonds see a growth fear); JPY strengthening while equities rally (haven flows against the risk narrative). The threshold trades early detection against false alarms, and there is no free setting.

Section 8

Stock-bond correlation is a variable

Growth news pushes stocks and bond prices apart; inflation news pushes them together. The mix decides the sign.

Try it · simulation, not market data
Inflation-shock volatility, second half (× growth shocks)2.00×
—

In this made-up two-shock model cov(S,B) = −c·σg² + b·d·σπ², which changes sign when σπ/σg = √(c/(bd)), about 1.44 here. 2022 was the real-world version: the Fed hiked into weakness and 60/40 had one of its worst years in decades.

March 2020 is the course's example. To monitor it: rolling windows of 20 to 60 days (tactical) or 120 to 252 days (strategic), or an EWMA correlation with decay λ ≈ 0.94, which weights today most and fades old data. Its effective window is about 1/(1−λ), roughly 17 days.

Section 10

Quick check

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

Stocks and bonds both fell in 2022. What was driving markets?

Inflation-driven regimes make the stock-bond correlation positive. Growth-driven regimes make it negative. The sign depends on which fear dominates.
Question 2

Equities rally while high-yield spreads widen. The framework says:

A divergence does not say what to do; it says to find out why the layers disagree and what would resolve it.
Question 3

In the simple two-shock model, when does the stock-bond covariance turn positive?

cov = −c·σg² + b·d·σπ². It flips when σπ/σg exceeds √(c/(bd)). The mix of news changes the sign without either asset changing its behaviour.
Question 4

In the lead-lag explorer the correlation bars peak at k = +5. What does that say, and what must you check in real data?

A peak at a positive k is evidence that X leads Y by about k periods. A peak at k = 0 means they just move together. A lead that exists in one sample and vanishes in the next is not a lead, so test it across windows.
Section 11

What to carry forward

01

Trade the capital structure, not single assets: one view, many expressions, different risk and reward. The cleanest is most directly tied to the thesis.

02

Information tends to flow rates, credit, FX, equities, but the order changes by episode. Treat it as a hypothesis to test.

03

Growth × inflation gives four regimes; the opportunity is in detecting transitions early, not labelling the regime after the fact.

04

Correlations are variables. Stock-bond correlation flips between growth-driven and inflation-driven regimes; in a liquidity crisis, everything heads towards 1.

05

Coherence builds conviction; divergence means investigate. Credit often leads at turning points, and crisis signals need real-time alerts.

What this now lets you do

Read a market move as one layer of a larger story, ask what should confirm it and what would invalidate it, and recognise when a diversification assumption has broken. Part 2 tests this on real history and is honest that the framework gives context and discipline rather than a signal.
