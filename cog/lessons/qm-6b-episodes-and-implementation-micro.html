Cross-Asset Synthesis, Part 2: Episodes and Implementation

The framework from part 1 meets real history: the 2022 bear market, the Q4 2023 everything rally and March 2023. Then how one view is expressed and sized, and where edge actually lives. It is a framework as taught, not a backtested strategy.

Part 1 in brief

What part 1 gave you

01

The layers (rates, credit, FX, equities, volatility) price one macro story at different speeds. Agreeing layers are coherent; disagreeing layers mean investigate. The order is a hypothesis.

02

Growth × inflation gives four regimes; the money is in detecting transitions early. Dashboards suit weeks to months, with real-time alerts for stress signals only.

03

Correlations are variables: stock-bond is negative when growth fears dominate and positive when inflation fears do, and heads towards 1 in a liquidity crisis.

Section 1 · It actually happened

2022: every signal visible, equities last

It actually happened

Powell retired "transitory" on 30 November 2021. The 2-year yield rose, real yields climbed from about −1% towards zero, the Nasdaq corrected in January, Russia invaded Ukraine on 24 February, the Fed's first hike came on 16 March, bigger hikes followed in May and June, and the S&P 500 ended about a quarter off its peak by October. Figures: the course's, approximate.

→
Jan to Mar 2022Real yields up; growth falls; hikes begin
→
★ Stock-bond correlation flipsPositive: the hedge breaks
→
Oct 20222Y ~4.5%, DXY above 114, gilt turmoil

Worked: short duration, commodities early, long USD, long volatility, value over growth, cash. Failed: 60/40, risk parity, growth stocks, EM. The edge was not prediction but systematic response to observable signals. The caveat: this is one episode told with hindsight.

Section 2 · It actually happened

Q4 2023: peak coherence

It actually happened

In October 2023 the 10-year hit about 5% and the S&P fell about 10% from July, yet credit spreads barely widened: a rates-driven sell-off that credit did not confirm. After a soft inflation read, a Fed hold on 1 November with Powell less hawkish than feared, and weak payrolls on 3 November, yields fell and everything rallied.

→
27 Oct to 3 NovSoft inflation; Fed hold; weak jobs
→
★ CoherenceCredit, dollar, volatility all agree
→
Year-end10Y ~3.9%, HY ~330 bps, VIX ~12

Sequencing as taught: rates first, credit confirmed, FX confirmed, equities followed. Waiting for "certainty" missed the move; tracking coherence gave early confirmation. Caveat: the table is read from the year-end, and the lesson does not say how often waiting for coherence costs a trader.

Section 3 · It actually happened

March 2023: credit led, and a weekly review missed it

It actually happened

Silvergate announced its liquidation on 8 March; after that day's close Silicon Valley Bank disclosed a $1.8 billion securities loss and a capital raise. Its stock fell about 60% on 9 March, with about $42 billion of withdrawal attempts in a day. The FDIC took SVB on 10 March. On Sunday 12 March Signature was closed and the Fed announced the Bank Term Funding Program. The Fed still hiked 25 bps on 22 March.

→
★ MOVE leads VIXA rates crisis; the 2Y collapses ~100 bps
→
10 to 12 MarFDIC seizure; BTFP firewall
→
13 to 22 MarStabilise; 2Y whipsaws

Lesson: hiking to emergency cuts priced in about 48 hours; crisis signals need real-time alerts on credit stress, rate volatility and inversion. The 2Y level figures (5.05% on 8 March, 4.03% on 13 March, 3.92% on 20 March) are FRED DGS2 closes.

Section 4

Express the view, then size it

Cleanest vs noisier
  • Inflation higher: long breakevens (cleanest) vs short bonds, long commodities, long value
  • Credit stress: long CDX HY protection vs short equities, long VIX
  • Dollar weaker: short DXY / long EUR/USD vs long EM equities, long gold
Choosing the expression
  • High conviction, short horizon: direct 2Y or front-end options
  • Moderate conviction, longer horizon: steepener or gold
  • Options for defined risk; can you afford negative carry while waiting?
  • Equities are the noisiest expression
Position size = Target risk / (Instrument volatility × Correlation to view). Target risk 1%, volatility 10%, correlation 1 gives 10% of capital; correlation 0.5 gives 20%.
Where this needs qualifying

Dividing by the correlation makes the size explode as the correlation falls towards zero. It is the course's heuristic, not a derived result; it needs a hard size cap.

Section 5

Implementation reality: where edge actually lives

Why daily discretionary macro is hard
  • Information is priced fast
  • Noise dominates short horizons
  • Behavioural biases compound
  • Costs erode returns with frequency
  • A post-hoc story is not tradeable
Where edge can exist
  • Longer horizons (weeks to months)
  • Systematic rules and discipline
  • Better data and infrastructure
  • Capacity to hold through drawdowns
  • The retail edge: no benchmark, no redemptions, no career risk

Section 6

Quick check

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

In October 2023 the 10-year hit about 5% and equities fell about 10%, but high-yield spreads barely widened. What did that suggest?

Credit not confirming meant it was not a hard-landing signal. Later every layer agreed, which is peak coherence, but the table is read from the year-end: it shows how to use coherence, not that coherence predicts.
Question 2

What did March 2023 show about monitoring cadence?

The move from hikes priced to emergency cuts priced took about 48 hours. Slow tiers can be reviewed weekly; stress signals cannot.
Question 3

Target risk 1%, instrument volatility 10%, correlation to the view 0.5. What is the position size, and what is the trap?

Size = 0.01 / (0.10 × 0.5) = 20%. At a correlation of 0.1 it would be 100% of capital. It is the course's heuristic, not a derived result.
Question 4

The course says understanding this framework is not an edge. Where does it point to edge instead?

Markets are efficient at short horizons. Even the sources of edge the course names must be tested before they are trusted.

Scenario drill · 10 March 2023

A bank has just failed, and you are short the front end

The Fed is in the middle of a hiking cycle, and the market expected a 25 to 50 basis point hike on 22 March. You hold a short position in the 2-year Treasury, sized in calm conditions for a market that is still pricing hikes. Silicon Valley Bank was taken over by the FDIC this morning. The 2-year yield has dropped sharply, regional-bank credit has been under stress since Thursday, and rate volatility is jumping. It is Friday close and the weekend is ahead. What do you do?

  1. Hold the short. One bank is not the system, and the Fed's hiking path is still intact.
    Credit moved first and rate volatility is spiking, which is the lesson's signal that the regime is shifting from "hiking" towards "emergency cuts priced". A position sized for calm rates volatility is now much too large for the new volatility, and the weekend is a gap risk in which policy can change the picture.
  2. Add to the short: yields have fallen too far, too fast and will snap back.
    It is a fade of a move in a stress event with a policy response not yet known. If the authorities stabilise things the yields may bounce, but if they do not you are adding to a loser in the fastest market in years. Adding is a view on timing you cannot support.
  3. Cut the position well down before the weekend, treat it as a possible regime break, and rebuild only when the policy response and credit signals confirm.
    This respects both lessons of the episode: credit and rate volatility led, and a regime shift can arrive in 48 hours. Reducing is not a bet on the new direction; it is admitting your sizing assumed a world that may have just ended. You can re-enter once more layers agree.
  4. Flip to a large long in 2-year Treasuries to ride the flight to safety.
    The direction of the next few days was plausible, but it is a full-size bet on one outcome in a gap-prone weekend, and the 2-year whipsawed afterwards. Right direction for the wrong reason can still be wrongly sized.

What happened. On Sunday 12 March regulators closed Signature Bank, and the Federal Reserve announced the Bank Term Funding Program, lending to banks against Treasuries and other eligible securities valued at par; depositors at the failed banks were protected. The 2-year yield fell much further on Monday 13 March and stayed very volatile, and the Fed raised rates by 25 basis points on 22 March.

Sources: Federal Reserve Board press release, 12 March 2023 (Bank Term Funding Program); Board of Governors, Review of the Federal Reserve's Supervision and Regulation of Silicon Valley Bank, 28 April 2023.

Section 7

What to carry forward

01

Three episodes are three stories, not a test: in 2022 rates led and the stock-bond hedge broke; Q4 2023 was peak coherence; in March 2023 credit led and a weekly review would have missed it.

02

Express the view with the cleanest instrument, then size it by target risk over volatility times correlation, with a hard cap.

03

Time horizon is the edge: daily discretionary macro is extremely hard. Longer horizons, systematic rules and patience, tested before trusted. Nothing here is tested on this desk.

What this now lets you do

Read a market move as one layer of a larger story, ask what should confirm it and what would invalidate it, and be honest that the framework gives context and discipline rather than a signal.
