Fixed Income, or Why Everything Is Priced Off the Bond Market

A bond's price and its yield are one fact seen from two ends. Duration says how far a yield move travels, and the yield curve, credit spreads and real yields say what the move means. This is the most mathematical lesson of the six (intermediate ideas, advanced-leaning maths), taught as a framework: the bond arithmetic is fact, and the claim that bonds lead everything else is not.

The whole idea
Section 1

Price up means yield down. Always.

A bond is a fixed list of payments. What you pay for the list sets the return you earn on it.

⚖️

The $50 versus $60 story

You hold a bond paying $50 a year (5% on $1,000). New bonds pay $60. Nobody pays full price for yours, so its price falls until its effective yield matches the market.

📋

The vocabulary

Par (face value, repaid at maturity, typically $1,000), coupon (annual interest as a % of face), maturity, and YTM (yield to maturity: the return if held to the end, "the yield" people quote).

🏷️

Reading a price quote

100 = par (yield = coupon). 95 = discount (yield above coupon; gain at maturity). 105 = premium (yield below coupon; loss at maturity).

📰

Translating a headline

"10Y yield hits 4.5%" means investors demand 4.5% to hold 10-year Treasuries. If yesterday was 4.3%, yields rose and prices fell: holders lost, new buyers get a better rate.

Yield ≈ Coupon ÷ Price. The coupon is fixed, so the only thing that can move is the price, and yield moves the opposite way.
Section 2

Duration: how far does a yield move travel?

Duration is roughly the percentage price change per 1% yield move. Longer maturity means higher duration means more rate sensitivity.

Follow it through
ΔP/P ≈ −D × Δy
D is duration, Δy the yield change in the same units, and the minus sign says yields up means price down. A 10-year note with D ≈ 8 loses about 4% when yields rise half a point. It is the straight tangent line to the price-yield curve.
ΔP/P ≈ −D Δy
+
½ C (Δy)²
The curve is not straight. Convexity C measures its bend, and (Δy)² is positive whichever way yields move, so the correction always adds: it softens falls and boosts rises. For a 4.5% par 10-year, D = 7.98 and C = 76.2.
2Y · D ≈ 2 → ∓1%
10Y · D ≈ 8 → ∓4%
30Y · D ≈ 18 → ∓9%
The course's table for a 0.5-point move. Exact repricing of a 4.5% par bond gives durations 1.89, 7.98 and 16.37, and price changes of 0.94%, 3.90% and 7.73% for a half-point rise. The "~18" for the 30-year depends on the coupon.

Section 4

Why 2022 was historic, and why stocks have duration too

Bonds in 2022
  • The course: 10Y yields rose about 2.5 points (roughly 1.5% to 4%). Duration 8 gives about a 20% loss.
  • Exact repricing of a 4.5% par 10-year for a 2.5-point rise: −17.8%. Duration alone says −20.0%.
  • The worst bond market in decades.
Growth stocks
  • Their value sits mostly in far-future earnings, so a higher discount rate hits them harder, like a long bond.
  • For a constant-growth stream, duration is 1 ÷ (r − g): a 4-point gap gives 25, the course's "25+".
  • This is why the Nasdaq and 30-year Treasuries (TLT) often trade together. Value stocks and banks have low duration.
Where this needs qualifying

Positive convexity (most bonds) helps holders; negative convexity (mortgage-backed securities, because owners prepay when rates fall) is worse than duration predicts in both directions. For most trading, duration suffices. Convexity matters for big books, mortgage bonds and big moves.

Section 6

Why an inverted curve is read as a recession warning

→
Market looks aheadHikes expected to slow the economy and force cuts
→
Long rates averageA 10Y reflects the expected average of future short rates
→
2Y > 10YThe market is pricing forced cuts

The course: an inverted curve has preceded every US recession in 50+ years, with a variable 6 to 24 month lead. A warning sign, not a precise timer. The popular spread is 2s10s (10Y minus 2Y); some research prefers 3M–10Y; 2s30s is more volatile.

Where this needs qualifying

"Every recession" is a handful of episodes, and the claim runs from recession back to inversion, not forward. The 2022 inversion lasted about two years, and whether a recession was dated afterwards is something to check rather than assume. The lead is too wide to use as an entry timer.

Desk test: nothing foundTen inversion episodes, 1976 to 2026. A recession within 24 months followed 6 of 10 (60%, interval 31% to 83%, which contains a coin flip). Forward Nasdaq returns did not separate from a matched control at any horizon, and steepening episodes did worse than inversions.
Section 7

Four ways the curve moves

Bull means yields fell, bear means yields rose. Steepener means the 2s10s gap widened, flattener means it narrowed.

🐂

Bull steepener

Short rates fall faster than long. Fed cuts being priced; early recession or crisis response.

🐻

Bear steepener

Long rates rise faster than short. Growth or inflation expectations rising; recovery or inflation fear.

🛡️

Bull flattener

Long rates fall faster than short. Flight to quality; growth fears; long bonds as a haven.

🔨

Bear flattener

Short rates rise faster than long. Fed tightening; hikes expected to slow the economy; inversion risk.

As taught: steepening generally favours cyclicals, value and banks; flattening toward inversion calls for caution; a rapid un-inversion often comes right before the recession actually starts. None of these positioning rules is tested here.
Section 8

Credit spreads: the fear gauge

Credit spread = corporate yield − Treasury yield. Treasury 4%, corporate 6% is a 200 bp spread: pay for default risk. Widening is fear, tightening is confidence.

Try it · read the course's HY bands

A slow drift: the level matters more than the speed here.

The course's reasoning for why credit leads: bond investors are institutional, downside-focused and less swayed by narrative. Its example: HY spreads began widening in summer 2007, equities made new highs in October 2007. Direction and speed matter more than level; +20 bp in a day often precedes equity volatility. (The course's bands leave 700 to 800 bp unlabelled.)

Desk test: nothing foundCredit agrees with equities in the same window but did not lead them. On 22 years of Moody's spreads, widening was not followed by lower Nasdaq or S&P returns, or by extra volatility, at 1, 5 or 20 sessions. One descriptive leg (ICE high-yield spread and next-week volatility) needs a longer series before it is a finding.
Section 9

Real yields and breakevens: nominal = real + breakeven

TIPS pay a real yield. The gap between the nominal yield and the TIPS yield is the market's expected average inflation.

Why it matters: real yields are the opportunity cost of holding things that pay nothing. Gold does well when real yields fall or are negative and struggles when they rise; growth stocks feel the same discount rate. On the course's rounded 2022 numbers, 2.5 of the 2.7-point rise in the 10-year was real yield. If a rising nominal is all breakeven, real yields may not be rising much.

Desk data: base rateReal yields explained gold in only part of the windows. Over 689 rolling 60-session windows (2010 to 2026) rates alone drove gold in 19%, the dollar alone in 18%, both in 16%, neither in 45%. "Gold follows real yields" is regime-dependent.

Why the 2-year: it tracks the policy path, is less noisy than the 3-month, and is more policy-driven than the 10-year (which carries term premium and inflation uncertainty). Watch the spread, not absolute levels. The course's case study: USD/JPY went from about 110 to 150+ as the US 2-year went from about 0.25% to about 5% while Japan stayed near zero.

Where this needs qualifying

Differentials do not always win: risk-off panic sends JPY and CHF up regardless of rates; central banks can intervene against the trend (Japan did, repeatedly, without reversing it); balance-of-payments crises sink high-rate EM currencies; and extreme positioning can reverse a pair despite the spread.

Desk test: nothing foundThe spread explains the hour, not the next day. On 61,000 hourly bars (2012 to 2026), the German-minus-US 10-year spread and EUR/USD correlate at −0.30 in the same hour, but at +1 hour the correlation is −0.015 and beyond +2 hours it is zero.
Section 11

Bonds leading equities: the cascade and the 5-minute scan

The course's argument: bond investors are institutions, obsessed with downside, in the most liquid markets, so they react to macro first.

Bonds react · hours to days
→
FX adjusts · days to weeks
→
Equities reprice · weeks to months
Bond-first questions
  • What are yields doing? Rising is a headwind for growth.
  • What is the curve saying? Flattening or inverting means caution.
  • What are credit spreads doing? Widening is a risk-off warning.
  • What is MOVE doing? Elevated means expect equity volatility.
  • Any divergence? Bonds warning, stocks rallying: be sceptical.
The daily 5-minute scan
  • 10Y yield, against yesterday and last week
  • 2s10s: steepening or flattening? still inverted?
  • 2Y: policy expectations shifting?
  • HY spreads: tightening or widening?
  • MOVE, 10Y TIPS, and the US-versus-partner differentials

The MOVE index is the bond market's VIX: under 80 calm, 80 to 120 normal, 120 to 150 elevated, over 150 stress (as the course gives it). Higher rates also lower price-to-earnings multiples through the discount rate, and the rate of change matters more than the level.

Desk test: nothing foundYield moves did not predict next-period direction. The coupling with FX and indices is real only in the same bar. The scan describes the market's present state; it is not a forecast of where a pair or index goes next.
Section 12 · It actually happened

2022, then Silicon Valley Bank

It actually happened

In the course's rounded numbers, 2022 saw the 10-year yield go from about 1.5% to about 4.2%, real yields flip from deeply negative to positive (gold and growth stocks fell), 2s10s invert, high-yield spreads roughly double, and equities follow: the S&P about −25% and the Nasdaq about −35%. On 8 March 2023 Silicon Valley Bank announced it had sold about $21 billion of securities, bought when yields were low, at a loss of about $1.8 billion; depositors tried to pull about $42 billion on 9 March and the bank was closed on 10 March.

Section 13

Quick check

Nothing is scored beyond this device. The explanation is the useful part.

Question 1

A 10-year note has a duration of about 8. Yields rise half a percentage point. Roughly what happens to its price?

ΔP/P ≈ −D × Δy = −8 × 0.5 = −4%. Yields up means price down, and duration is the multiplier.
Question 2

Compared with the exact price change, the duration-only estimate for a large rise in yields is:

The price-yield curve bends above its tangent, so a rise costs less than the straight line says, and a fall gains more.
Question 3

The 10-year yield is 4.5% and the 10-year TIPS yield 2.0%. If the nominal yield rises to 5.0% with the TIPS yield unchanged, what moved?

Breakeven = nominal − TIPS. With TIPS fixed, the whole rise is breakeven. Gold and growth stocks respond to real yields, so this is a different story from a real-yield rise.
Question 4

Short rates fall faster than long rates. The course calls this a:

Bull means yields fell; steepener means 2s10s widened. A bull flattener is long rates falling faster, and a bear flattener is short rates rising faster.
Question 5

What is the right way to hold the claim that an inverted curve has preceded every US recession in 50+ years?

The claim runs from recession back to inversion, rests on a small number of episodes, and says nothing precise about timing or about equity returns.
Your call
Scenario drill · 8 March 2023

A bank sells its "safe" bonds at a loss

Silicon Valley Bank has just announced that it sold about $21 billion of securities at a loss of about $1.8 billion and will raise $2.25 billion of new equity. Its holdings are Treasury and agency securities, bought mostly in 2020 and 2021 when yields were very low; yields have since risen several points. A colleague says: "It is all government paper, so how can it lose money?" What does this lesson tell you?

  1. It cannot be the bonds. Government securities carry no risk, so this must be hidden credit losses in the loan book.
    Government bonds carry almost no default risk, but they carry full price risk. Duration turns a multi-point rise in yields into a large fall in the price of long bonds, whatever the credit quality.
  2. It is duration. Long bonds bought at low yields fall in price by roughly duration times the yield rise; a loss that was only on paper becomes real the moment the bonds must be sold to meet withdrawals.
    This is the lesson's rule applied. On illustrative numbers, a portfolio with a duration of 6 hit by a 3-point rise falls by roughly 18% before convexity. Held to maturity the loss may never be realised; a forced sale realises it.
  3. The loss is only on paper until the bonds are sold, so it does not matter.
    Right about the accounting, wrong about the risk. A paper loss matters when the holder is forced to sell: withdrawals turn an unrealised loss into a realised one, and the market reads the sale as a signal about the rest of the book.
  4. Convexity will protect the bank, since bond prices rise more than they fall.
    Convexity cushions the fall slightly, as the worked example shows (0.38 points on a 7.98-point loss). It does not offset a multi-point rise in yields on a long portfolio.

What happened. After the announcement, customers tried to withdraw about $42 billion on 9 March. On 10 March 2023 the California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation closed the bank and appointed the FDIC as receiver. Rising rates had cut the value of long-dated securities bought when yields were low, and a run on deposits forced the sale that made the loss real.

Sources: Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, Review of the Federal Reserve's Supervision and Regulation of Silicon Valley Bank (Barr review), 28 April 2023.

Section 14

What to carry forward

01

Price and yield are opposites, by arithmetic: a fixed coupon plus a falling price is a rising yield.

02

Duration is rate sensitivity: ΔP/P ≈ −D Δy. Convexity, ½C(Δy)², corrects it and always adds, so duration overstates losses and understates gains.

03

Growth stocks have duration too (about 1/(r−g)), which is why the Nasdaq and long Treasuries often move together and why 2022 hurt both.

04

The curve (2s10s, 3M–10Y, 2s30s) and the four curve moves are a vocabulary; an inversion is a warning with a 6 to 24 month variable lead, not a timer.

05

Credit spreads are the fear gauge, real yields (nominal − breakeven) drive gold and growth, and 2-year differentials are the course's main fundamental FX driver, with known exceptions.

06

"Bonds lead, equities follow" is a framework, not a result. The desk's tests of credit leading equities and of yields predicting direction both came back null.

What this now lets you do

Turn any yield headline into a price move with one multiplication, say how much of a nominal move was real yield versus inflation expectations, name a curve move (bull or bear, steepener or flattener) and what it is said to signal, and run the five-question bond-first check while keeping in mind which of its claims have a test behind them.
